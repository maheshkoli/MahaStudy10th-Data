1. Lesson at a Glance

“The Concert” by Shanta Rao tells the story of Smita and her younger brother Anant. Anant is a talented fifteen-year-old who enjoys table tennis and the sitar, but he is seriously ill with cancer. He longs to hear Pandit Ravi Shankar perform. Smita attends the concert and bravely asks Pandit Ravi Shankar and Ustad Allah Rakha to visit her home. They agree and play for Anant the following morning, bringing him great happiness.

Main characters: Smita, Anant, Aunt Sushila, Pandit Ravi Shankar and Ustad Allah Rakha.

Main themes: sibling love, courage, empathy, generosity and the emotional power of music.

The answers are based on the supplied textbook pages 137–144 and the previous HTML file. Personal-response questions are sample answers and may be adapted by students.

2. Warming Up

1. Pair discussion: music and concerts

  1. Do you like music? Yes, I like music because it relaxes my mind and makes me feel happy.
  2. What does a concert mean? A concert is a live performance of music before an audience.
  3. Have you ever attended a live concert? Sample answer: Yes, I attended a live music programme at my school. I enjoyed listening to the singers and musicians. (Change this answer to match your own experience.)
  4. Have you seen a concert on television? Yes, I have watched musical concerts on television.
  5. Whose concert would you love to attend? I would love to attend a concert by my favourite singer or musician.

5. Write a short paragraph on Anant

Anant was a fifteen-year-old boy who was talented and full of promise. He was the best table-tennis player in his school and the fastest runner. He was also learning the sitar and could compose his own tunes. Unfortunately, he was suffering from cancer, and his health grew worse with time. His greatest wish was to hear Pandit Ravi Shankar and Ustad Allah Rakha perform. His sister Smita courageously approached the musicians after the concert and requested them to visit their home. They agreed and played for Anant the next morning, fulfilling his wish and giving him great happiness.

7. The two contradictory pictures in the story

The story presents two contrasting pictures. On one side, Anant is a young, talented boy with a bright future, but he is seriously ill and approaching the end of his life. On the other side, music brings joy, hope and beauty into his final hours. The sadness of his illness contrasts with the kindness of Smita and the musicians. The story shows that compassion cannot remove every hardship, but it can bring comfort and happiness.

4. Grammar and Language

9(A). Choose the correct adjective or adverb

  1. She spoke in an excited tone.
  2. Smita accepted the suggestion gratefully.
  3. They gave him whatever made him happy.
  4. He ran very fast.
  5. He would become a great sitarist some day.
  6. Life went out of him gently.

9(B). Rewrite in indirect speech

  1. Direct: “Please,” she begged him, “Please come.”
    Indirect: She begged him to come.
  2. Direct: He said, “What shall we do, Ustad Sahib?”
    Indirect: Pandit Ravi Shankar asked Ustad Sahib what they should do.
  3. Direct: Her brother said, “Enjoy yourself.” He added, “Lucky you!”
    Indirect: Her brother told her to enjoy herself and added that she was lucky.
  4. Direct: “Yes,” Panditji replied. “It’s settled then. Tomorrow morning we shall perform for the boy.”
    Indirect: Panditji agreed and said that it was settled, adding that they would perform for the boy the following morning.

10. Short note on another great musician

Name: Zakir Hussain
Field: Music
Specialisation: Tabla
Guru: His father, Ustad Allah Rakha, was his major teacher and influence.
Early life: He was born into a family deeply connected with Indian classical music and began learning tabla at a young age.
Education: He studied music from childhood while continuing his general education.
Early success: He performed publicly at a young age and developed an international career.
Contribution: He helped introduce Indian tabla and classical music to audiences around the world and collaborated with musicians across genres.
Awards: He received major Indian civilian honours and international recognition during his career.
Special incident: His performances and collaborations helped many new listeners discover the tabla.

This is a sample note using general knowledge, because the supplied textbook asks students to research another musician. Verify biographical details and awards from a reliable source if submitting this as a researched project.

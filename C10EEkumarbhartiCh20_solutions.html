Chapter 20: The Luncheon

1. Chapter at a Glance

"The Luncheon" is a humorous short story by William Somerset Maugham. It depicts a meeting between the narrator (a young, struggling writer) and a woman who is an admirer of his work. The story explores themes of social pretension, financial struggle, and the irony of the narrator's situation as he is forced to host a luncheon he cannot afford. Students will learn to identify irony, understand character motivation, and analyze the use of humor in literature.

2. Important Concepts Explained

  • The Narrator's Situation: The narrator was a young, struggling writer living in Paris with very limited funds. He had eighty francs to last the rest of the month.
  • The Meeting: The narrator is invited to lunch by a woman who claims to be an admirer of his book. She suggests "Foyot’s," an expensive restaurant far beyond his means.
  • The Irony: The woman repeatedly claims she "never eats anything for luncheon" and "never eats more than one thing," yet she proceeds to order expensive items like caviar, salmon, and giant asparagus, while the narrator is forced to order the cheapest item (a mutton chop) to save money.
  • The Climax: The narrator's panic grows as the bill increases, fearing he will not have enough money to pay. He even contemplates a dramatic ruse to avoid embarrassment.
  • The Resolution: The narrator survives the meal with just enough money for a tip, but no money for the rest of the month. Years later, he encounters the woman again and notes that she has gained significant weight, which he views as a form of "revenge."

(b) There are quite a few places where the author uses the expressions ‘my heart sank, panic seized’ etc. What was the reason for this? Explain.

  • Answer: These expressions reflect the author's growing anxiety and fear that he would not have enough money to pay the restaurant bill after the guest ordered expensive items like caviar, salmon, and asparagus.

(c) What are the instances which create humour in “The Luncheon”?

  • Answer: The primary humor comes from the irony of the guest's claim that she "never eats more than one thing" while she proceeds to order multiple expensive dishes.

(d) Describe the use of irony and humour in “The Luncheon”.

  • Answer: The story uses situational irony. The guest presents herself as a refined, light eater, but her actions prove she is gluttonous. The humor arises from the narrator's helpless observation of her eating while his own financial panic increases.

6. Important Questions and Answers

  1. Why was the narrator worried about the bill?
  • Answer: The narrator was worried because he only had eighty francs to last the entire month, and the guest kept ordering expensive items, making him fear he would not have enough to pay.
  1. What does the guest's behavior reveal about her character?
  • Answer: Her behavior reveals that she is hypocritical, self-centered, and lacks awareness of others' financial situations. She claims to be a light eater but consumes expensive food without regard for the narrator's budget.
  1. How does the narrator get his "revenge" at the end of the story?
  • Answer: The narrator gets his revenge by observing that the woman has become very obese over the years, which he considers a natural consequence of her gluttony.

7. Quick Revision Sheet

  • Theme: Social satire, hypocrisy, and financial struggle.
  • Key Irony: The guest's claim of being a light eater vs. her actual consumption.
  • Narrator's Trait: Polite, observant, and financially constrained.
  • Guest's Trait: Talkative, hypocritical, and gluttonous.
  • Lesson: Appearances can be deceptive, and social pretension often masks a lack of consideration for others.

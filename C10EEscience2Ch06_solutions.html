1. Chapter at a Glance

This chapter introduces the vast diversity of the animal kingdom and the necessity of classifying animals to make their study convenient and systematic. It covers the history of animal classification, traditional methods (Non-chordates and Chordates), and the modern system based on criteria like body organization, symmetry, germ layers, and body cavity.

2. Important Concepts Explained

History of Animal Classification

  • Artificial Method: The earliest classification by Aristotle, based on body size, habits, and habitats.
  • Natural System: Based on body organization, cell types, chromosomes, and biochemical properties.
  • Evolutionary System: Classification based on evolutionary relationships (phylogeny).

Benefits of Animal Classification

  • Study of animals becomes convenient.
  • Study of a few animals helps understand the entire group.
  • Provides insight into animal evolution.
  • Allows for accurate identification and understanding of habitats and adaptations.

Criteria for New System of Classification

  1. Grades of Organization:
  • Protoplasmic grade: Unicellular animals (all functions by one cell).
  • Cellular grade: Multicellular, no tissues (e.g., Phylum Porifera).
  • Cell-tissue grade: Cells form tissues (e.g., Phylum Cnidaria).
  • Tissue-organ grade: Tissues form organs (e.g., Flatworms).
  • Organ-system grade: Organs form systems (e.g., Crab, Frog, Human).
  1. Body Symmetry:
  • Asymmetrical: No axis to get two equal halves (e.g., Amoeba).
  • Radial symmetry: Multiple planes through a central axis produce equal halves (e.g., Star fish).
  • Bilateral symmetry: Only one imaginary axis produces two equal halves (e.g., Insects, Humans).
  1. Germ Layers:
  • Diploblastic: Two layers (Endoderm and Ectoderm, e.g., Cnidarians).
  • Triploblastic: Three layers (Endoderm, Ectoderm, and Mesoderm).
  1. Body Cavity (Coelom):
  • Eucoelomate: True body cavity (Annelida onwards).
  • Acoelomate: No body cavity (Porifera, Cnidaria, Platyhelminthes).
  • Pseudocoelomate: False body cavity (Aschelminthes).
  1. Body Segmentation: Body divided into small, similar units (e.g., Earthworm).

3. Definitions and Key Terms

  • Animal Classification: The formation of groups and sub-groups of animals based on similarities and differences.
  • Notochord: A long, rod-like supporting structure present on the dorsal side of the animal body.
  • Non-Chordates: Animals whose body is not supported by a notochord.
  • Chordates: Animals whose body is supported by a notochord.

4. Formulas, Rules, and Properties

  • Traditional Classification Rule: Based on the presence or absence of a notochord.
  • Five Kingdom Classification (Whittaker): Used for multicellular animals, based on body organization, symmetry, cavity, germ layers, and segmentation.

6. Exercise / Practice Set Solutions

1. Identify me.

a. I am diploblastic & acoelomate. Which phylum do I belong to?

  • Answer: Phylum Cnidaria (Coelenterata).

b. My body is radially symmetrical. Water vascular system is present in my body. I am referred as fish though I am not. What is my name?

  • Answer: Star fish (Phylum Echinodermata).

c. I live in your small intestine. Pseudocoelom is present in my thread like body. In which phylum will you include me?

  • Answer: Phylum Aschelminthes (e.g., Ascaris).

d. Though I am multicellular, there are no tissues in my body. What is the name of my phylum?

  • Answer: Phylum Porifera.

2. Write the characters of each of the following animals with the help of classification chart.

  • Bath sponge: Phylum Porifera, cellular grade, asymmetrical, acoelomate.
  • Grasshopper: Phylum Arthropoda, organ-system grade, bilateral symmetry, eucoelomate, segmented.
  • Rohu: Phylum Chordata, Subphylum Vertebrata, Class Pisces, cold-blooded, gills for respiration.
  • Penguin: Phylum Chordata, Subphylum Vertebrata, Class Aves, warm-blooded, feathers present.
  • Frog: Phylum Chordata, Subphylum Vertebrata, Class Amphibia, can live in water and on land.
  • Lizard: Phylum Chordata, Subphylum Vertebrata, Class Reptilia, dry and scaly skin, creeping movement.
  • Elephant: Phylum Chordata, Subphylum Vertebrata, Class Mammalia, mammary glands present.
  • Jellyfish: Phylum Cnidaria, cell-tissue grade, radial symmetry, acoelomate.

3. Write in brief about progressive changes in animal classification.

  • Answer: Classification evolved from the 'Artificial method' (Aristotle) based on simple external traits, to the 'Natural system' based on internal body organization and cell types, and finally to the 'Evolutionary system' based on phylogenetic relationships and genetic/biochemical data.

4. What is the exact difference between grades of organization and symmetry? Explain with examples.

  • Grades of Organization: Refers to the complexity of body structure (e.g., Cellular grade in Porifera vs. Organ-system grade in Humans).
  • Symmetry: Refers to the arrangement of body parts around an axis (e.g., Radial symmetry in Star fish vs. Bilateral symmetry in Insects).

5. Answer in brief.

a. Give scientific classification of shark upto class.

  • Kingdom: Animalia -> Phylum: Chordata -> Subphylum: Vertebrata -> Class: Pisces.

b. Write four distinguishing characters of phylum- Echinodermata.

  1. Calcareous spines on the body.
  2. Exclusively marine.
  3. Radially symmetrical in adult stage.
  4. Locomotion via tube-feet.

c. Distinguish between butterfly and bat with the help of four distinguishing properties.

  • Butterfly: Invertebrate (Arthropoda), exoskeleton of chitin, no mammary glands, lays eggs.
  • Bat: Vertebrate (Chordata/Mammalia), endoskeleton of bones, mammary glands present, gives birth to young ones.

d. To which phylum does Cockroach belong? Justify your answer with scientific reasons.

  • Answer: Phylum Arthropoda. Justification: It has jointed appendages, a chitinous exoskeleton, and a segmented body.

6. Give scientific reasons.

a. Though tortoise lives on land as well as in water, it cannot be included in class- Amphibia.

  • Reason: Tortoise has dry, scaly skin and lays eggs on land, which are characteristics of Class Reptilia, not Amphibia.

b. Our body irritates if it comes in contact with jelly fish.

  • Reason: Jellyfish (Cnidaria) have cnidoblasts (stinging cells) on their tentacles that inject toxins for protection and prey capture.

c. All vertebrates are chordates but all chordates are not vertebrates.

  • Reason: Vertebrates are a subphylum of Chordata where the notochord is replaced by a vertebral column. Other chordates (Urochordata, Cephalochordata) do not have a vertebral column.

d. Balanoglossus is connecting link between non-chordates & chordates.

  • Reason: It shows characteristics of both groups, such as pharyngeal gill slits (chordate feature) but lacks a true notochord (non-chordate feature).

e. Body temperature of reptiles is not constant.

  • Reason: Reptiles are cold-blooded (poikilotherms) and cannot regulate their internal body temperature independently of the environment.

7. Answer the following questions by choosing correct option.

a. Which special cells are present in the body of sponges (Porifera)?

  • Answer: 1. Collar cells.

b. Which of the following animals’ body shows bilateral symmetry?

  • Answer: 3. Earthworm.

c. Which of the following animals can regenerate it’s broken body part?

  • Answer: 4. Star fish.

d. Bat is included in which class?

  • Answer: 4. Mammalia.

7. Important Questions and Answers

  • Q: What is the significance of the notochord?
  • A: It is a supporting structure that keeps nerve tissue isolated from the rest of the body, defining the Phylum Chordata.
  • Q: Why is the classification of animals into 'Non-chordates' and 'Chordates' considered traditional?
  • A: Because modern classification uses more complex criteria like germ layers, body cavity, and evolutionary relationships rather than just the presence of a notochord.

8. Common Errors and How to Avoid Them

  • Error: Confusing radial and bilateral symmetry.
  • Correction: Remember that radial symmetry allows multiple planes to divide the body into equal halves (like a wheel), while bilateral symmetry allows only one plane (like a human).
  • Error: Misclassifying animals like whales or bats as fish or birds.
  • Correction: Check for mammalian characteristics like mammary glands and hair/fur, regardless of their habitat.

9. Quick Revision Sheet

  • Phyla: Porifera, Cnidaria, Platyhelminthes, Aschelminthes, Annelida, Arthropoda, Mollusca, Echinodermata, Hemichordata, Chordata.
  • Symmetry: Asymmetrical (Amoeba), Radial (Star fish), Bilateral (Human).
  • Body Cavity: Acoelomate (no cavity), Pseudocoelomate (false cavity), Eucoelomate (true cavity).
  • Germ Layers: Diploblastic (2 layers), Triploblastic (3 layers).

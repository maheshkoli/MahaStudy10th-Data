1. Chapter Overview

“World Heritage” explains what a World Heritage Site is, why such places are important, how the World Heritage system developed, how a site can be included on the World Heritage List, the different types of World Heritage Sites, and why some sites become endangered.

The textbook defines a World Heritage Site as a place identified by UNESCO as having significant cultural or natural importance to humanity. Such sites may include forests, lakes, monuments, buildings, cities, or combinations of cultural and natural areas. fileciteturn180file0L59-L76

Central idea: World Heritage Sites are valuable parts of humanity's cultural and natural heritage. They need responsible protection, preservation and sustainable use so that their important characteristics are not lost.

3. Meaning and Characteristics of World Heritage

What is a World Heritage Site?

A World Heritage Site is a place recognised by UNESCO as having significant cultural or natural importance to humanity. The textbook explains that these sites are protected and maintained through the international World Heritage Programme. They may be forests, lakes, monuments, buildings, cities or mixed cultural-natural areas.

What significant aspects should a World Heritage Site have?

  • Outstanding cultural importance, or
  • Outstanding natural importance, or
  • A combination of cultural and natural importance.
  • Characteristics that make the site valuable to humanity.
  • Qualities that justify protection and international recognition.

Example — Mount Huangshan

The textbook uses Mount Huangshan in China as an example of a site having both cultural and physical-landscape significance. It played a role in Chinese art and literature and is also significant because of its landscape. fileciteturn180file0L70-L76

4. History of World Heritage Sites

1954–1959: Abu Simbel and the UNESCO Campaign

Egypt planned the Aswan High Dam in 1954. The original construction plan would have flooded the valley containing the Abu Simbel Temples and many ancient Egyptian artefacts. In 1959 UNESCO launched an international campaign to dismantle and move the temples to higher ground. fileciteturn180file0L84-L100

1965: World Heritage Trust Proposal

A White House Conference in the United States called for a “World Heritage Trust” to protect historic cultural sites as well as significant natural and scenic sites. fileciteturn180file0L101-L105

1968: Conservation Goals

The International Union for Conservation of Nature developed similar goals and presented them at the United Nations conference on Human Environment in Stockholm, Sweden, in 1972. fileciteturn180file0L105-L109

1972: Convention Adopted

The Convention concerning the Protection of World Cultural and Natural Heritage was adopted by UNESCO's General Conference on 16 November 1972. fileciteturn180file0L109-L112

5. The World Heritage Committee

The World Heritage Committee is the main group responsible for establishing which sites will be listed as UNESCO World Heritage Sites. The textbook says that it meets once a year and consists of representatives from 21 State Parties, elected for six-year terms by the World Heritage Center's General Assembly. State Parties identify and nominate new sites in their territories. fileciteturn180file0L114-L122

Key Responsibilities

  • Review recommendations about nominated sites.
  • Decide which sites should be added to the World Heritage List.
  • Consider whether nominated sites satisfy the selection criteria.
  • Support protection of sites through the World Heritage system.
  • Deal with sites that are threatened or lose their qualifying characteristics.

6. Becoming a World Heritage Site — Five Steps

StepProcess
1A country / State Party takes an inventory of significant cultural and natural sites and prepares a Tentative List.
2A site is selected from the Tentative List and included in a Nomination File.
3Two Advisory Bodies review the Nomination File and make recommendations to the World Heritage Committee.
4The World Heritage Committee reviews the recommendations and decides which sites will be added to the World Heritage List.
5The nominated site is assessed against the selection criteria; if it meets at least one required criterion, it can be inscribed on the World Heritage List.

The textbook notes that once selected, a site remains the property of the country in whose territory it lies while also becoming part of the international community's heritage. fileciteturn180file0L154-L179

8. World Heritage Sites in Danger

The lesson explains that World Heritage Sites may be threatened by:

  • War
  • Poaching
  • Natural disasters such as earthquakes
  • Uncontrolled urbanisation
  • Heavy tourist traffic
  • Air pollution
  • Acid rain

Sites in danger can be placed on a separate List of World Heritage Sites in Danger. The World Heritage Committee can allocate resources from the World Heritage Fund and put protection or restoration plans in place. If a site loses the characteristics for which it was originally included, the Committee can remove it from the World Heritage List. fileciteturn180file0L226-L244

5. Answer in Your Own Words

(a) What are the duties towards preservation of a historical site?

Visitors should obey rules, maintain discipline and cleanliness, use dustbins and garbage bags, observe silence where required, maintain environmental safety and protect the historical and cultural value of the site. Visitors should avoid damaging structures, monuments, natural features or artefacts.

(b) Why should we preserve World Heritage Sites?

We should preserve them because they represent important cultural and natural heritage. They connect present generations with history, support education and research, preserve outstanding natural resources and can contribute to responsible tourism. Preservation also ensures that future generations can experience and learn from these places.

(c) What is the role of World Heritage Sites in promoting tourism?

World Heritage Sites attract visitors because of their cultural, historical or natural importance. Tourism can create opportunities for local communities, support related services and increase awareness of a country's heritage. However, tourism should be managed responsibly so that heavy tourist traffic does not damage the site.

10. Preservation Paragraph

Protecting World Heritage Sites for Posterity

World Heritage Sites should be conserved carefully for posterity. Human and animal trespassing should be controlled wherever it can damage the site. Unmonitored, uncontrolled and unrestricted access should be avoided. Responsible local administration should regularly inspect the site and prevent negligence. Conservation plans should balance public access with the protection of historical structures, natural resources and other important characteristics.

12. Model Long Answers

Q. Why should World Heritage Sites be preserved?

World Heritage Sites represent important cultural and natural values of humanity. They preserve evidence of history, art, architecture, traditions and outstanding natural environments. They provide opportunities for education, research and responsible tourism. Preservation also protects these places for future generations. The lesson explains that sites may be threatened by war, poaching, disasters, uncontrolled urbanisation, heavy tourist traffic and environmental pollution, so organised conservation is necessary. fileciteturn180file0L226-L244

Q. Explain the five-step process of becoming a World Heritage Site.

First, a country prepares an inventory of significant cultural and natural sites and creates a Tentative List. Second, a selected site is placed in a Nomination File. Third, Advisory Bodies review the file and make recommendations. Fourth, the World Heritage Committee considers those recommendations and decides which sites should be added to the World Heritage List. Finally, the nominated site must satisfy at least one of the selection criteria before it can be inscribed. fileciteturn180file0L154-L176

Q. What are the duties of visitors at a historical or World Heritage site?

Visitors should obey the site's rules and regulations, maintain discipline and cleanliness, use dustbins, observe silence where required, maintain environmental safety and protect the country's history and heritage. They should not damage structures, remove artefacts, litter or disturb protected natural features. The aim is to ensure that tourism does not become a threat to preservation.

Q. Explain the role of the World Heritage Committee.

The World Heritage Committee is responsible for establishing which sites will be listed as UNESCO World Heritage Sites. It reviews recommendations from the Advisory Bodies, decides which nominated sites should be added to the World Heritage List, and can also deal with sites that are endangered or lose the characteristics for which they were originally listed. The textbook states that the Committee meets once a year and consists of representatives from 21 State Parties serving six-year terms. fileciteturn180file0L114-L122

Chapter 3: Circle

1. Chapter at a Glance

This chapter covers the fundamental properties of circles, including tangents, secants, arcs, and angles. Students will learn about:

  • Circles passing through points and touching circles.
  • Tangent properties and theorems.
  • Arc measures and central angles.
  • Inscribed angles and the Inscribed Angle Theorem.
  • Cyclic quadrilaterals and their properties.
  • Theorems related to intersecting chords, secants, and tangents.

---

2. Important Concepts Explained

Circles and Lines

  • Secant: A line that intersects a circle at two distinct points.
  • Tangent: A line that touches a circle at exactly one point. The point of contact is the point where the tangent touches the circle.
  • Tangent Theorem: A tangent at any point of a circle is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact.
  • Converse of Tangent Theorem: A line perpendicular to a radius at its point on the circle is a tangent to the circle.
  • Tangent Segment Theorem: Tangent segments drawn from an external point to a circle are congruent.

Touching Circles

  • Externally Touching: Two circles touch each other at one point, and the distance between their centers is the sum of their radii.
  • Internally Touching: Two circles touch each other at one point, and the distance between their centers is the difference of their radii.
  • Theorem of Touching Circles: If two circles touch each other, their point of contact lies on the line joining their centers.

Arcs and Angles

  • Arc of a Circle: A secant divides a circle into two parts; each part is an arc.
  • Central Angle: An angle whose vertex is the center of the circle. Its measure is equal to the measure of its corresponding minor arc.
  • Inscribed Angle: An angle whose vertex lies on the circle and whose arms intersect the circle at two points.
  • Inscribed Angle Theorem: The measure of an inscribed angle is half the measure of the arc intercepted by it.
  • Corollary of Inscribed Angle Theorem: Angles inscribed in the same arc are congruent. An angle inscribed in a semicircle is a right angle.

Cyclic Quadrilateral

  • Definition: A quadrilateral is cyclic if all its vertices lie on the same circle.
  • Theorem: Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral are supplementary (sum to 180°).
  • Converse: If a pair of opposite angles of a quadrilateral is supplementary, the quadrilateral is cyclic.

Intersecting Chords and Secants

  • Theorem of Internal Division of Chords: If two chords intersect inside a circle, the product of the segments of one chord equals the product of the segments of the other.
  • Theorem of External Division of Chords: If secants containing chords intersect outside the circle, the product of the external segment and the whole secant is equal for both.
  • Tangent Secant Segments Theorem: If a secant and a tangent intersect outside a circle, the product of the secant segments equals the square of the tangent segment.

3. Definitions and Key Terms

  • Concyclic Points: Points that lie on the same circle.
  • Intercepted Arc: An arc of a circle that lies in the interior of an angle and whose endpoints are on the arms of the angle.
  • Minor/Major Arc: The smaller/larger part of a circle divided by a secant.
  • Point of Contact: The unique point where a tangent touches a circle.

---

4. Formulas, Rules, and Properties

  • Measure of Major Arc: $360^\circ - \text{measure of corresponding minor arc}$.
  • Measure of Semicircle: $180^\circ$.
  • Distance between centers (Externally touching): $d = r_1 + r_2$.
  • Distance between centers (Internally touching): $d = |r_1 - r_2|$.
  • Area of a Sector: $A = \frac{\theta}{360} \times \pi r^2$.
  • Length of an Arc: $l = \frac{\theta}{360} \times 2\pi r$.
  • Tangent Secant Segments Theorem: $EA \times EB = ET^2$ (where $ET$ is the tangent segment).

5. Worked Examples — Step by Step

Ex (1) (Page 62)

Given: Circle with center D touches sides of $\angle ACB$ at A and B. $\angle ACB = 52^\circ$.

To find: Measure of $\angle ADB$.

Solution:

  1. The sum of angles in quadrilateral CADB is $360^\circ$.
  2. By the tangent theorem, the radius is perpendicular to the tangent, so $\angle CAD = 90^\circ$ and $\angle CBD = 90^\circ$.
  3. $\angle ACB + \angle CAD + \angle CBD + \angle ADB = 360^\circ$.
  4. $52^\circ + 90^\circ + 90^\circ + \angle ADB = 360^\circ$.
  5. $232^\circ + \angle ADB = 360^\circ$.
  6. $\angle ADB = 360^\circ - 232^\circ = 128^\circ$.

Final Answer: $\angle ADB = 128^\circ$.

Practice Set 3.1 (Page 64)

Q1. Radius = 6 cm, line AB is tangent at A.

(1) $\angle CAB = 90^\circ$ (Tangent-radius theorem).

(2) Distance of C from line AB = 6 cm (Radius of the circle).

(3) $d(A, B) = 6$ cm. In $\triangle CAB$, $CA = 6$, $AB = 6$. By Pythagoras, $CB = \sqrt{6^2 + 6^2} = \sqrt{72} = 6\sqrt{2}$ cm.

(4) $\angle ABC = 45^\circ$ (Since $CA = AB$, $\triangle CAB$ is an isosceles right triangle).

7. Important Questions and Answers

Q: What is the maximum number of tangents that can be drawn to a circle from a point outside it?

A: Two.

Q: If two circles touch internally, what is the distance between their centers?

A: The difference of their radii ($|r_1 - r_2|$).

---

8. Common Errors and How to Avoid Them

  • Confusing Tangent and Secant: Remember a tangent touches at one point, while a secant intersects at two.
  • Angle Measurement: Always ensure the angle is inscribed correctly; the vertex must be on the circle for the Inscribed Angle Theorem to apply.
  • Touching Circles: Do not confuse the distance formulas for internally and externally touching circles.

9. Quick Revision Sheet

  • Tangent Theorem: Tangent $\perp$ Radius.
  • Inscribed Angle: Measure = $\frac{1}{2} \times$ Intercepted Arc.
  • Cyclic Quad: Opposite angles sum to $180^\circ$.
  • Intersecting Chords: $AE \times EB = CE \times ED$.

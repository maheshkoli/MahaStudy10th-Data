1. Chapter at a Glance

This chapter features the poem "Animals" by Walt Whitman. It explores the poet's admiration for the simple, contented, and honest nature of animals compared to the complicated, dissatisfied, and materialistic nature of humans. Students will learn to analyze poetic themes, identify figures of speech, and reflect on human values.

2. Important Concepts Explained

  • The Poet's Perspective: Walt Whitman expresses a desire to "turn and live with animals" because he finds them more "placid" (calm) and "self-contain'd" (self-sufficient) than humans.
  • Human vs. Animal Traits:
  • Humans: The poet observes that humans "sweat and whine about their condition," lie awake at night weeping for their sins, and are obsessed with the "mania of owning things." They are often dissatisfied and unhappy.
  • Animals: They do not complain, they do not worry about their duty to God, and they do not possess the greed for material ownership. They are content and honest.
  • Tokens of Self: The poet mentions that animals bring him "tokens of myself" and "evince them plainly in their possession." These tokens represent virtues like innocence, simplicity, and contentment—qualities that humans once had but have "negligently dropped" over time.

3. Definitions and Key Terms

  • Placid: Not easily upset; calm and peaceful.
  • Self-contain'd: Self-sufficient; not needing others for support.
  • Mania: An excessive enthusiasm or craze for something (in this case, owning things).
  • Demented: Mad or insane.
  • Evince: To show or reveal clearly.
  • Tokens: In this poem, these refer to virtues like innocence, simplicity, and contentment.

4. Figures of Speech

  • Repetition: The use of the same word or phrase multiple times for emphasis (e.g., "long and long," "not one is dissatisfied, not one is demented").
  • Alliteration: The occurrence of the same letter or sound at the beginning of adjacent or closely connected words (e.g., "wonder where they get those tokens").
  • Hyperbole: Exaggerated statements not meant to be taken literally (e.g., "I stand and look at them long and long").

5. Warming Up — Complete Solutions

1. Explain Mark Twain’s quotation

“The more I learn about people, the more I like my dog” suggests that the speaker values a dog’s loyalty, affection and honesty. The quotation humorously contrasts an animal’s uncomplicated companionship with the dishonesty, selfishness or unkind behaviour that people may sometimes show.

Sample answer — I wish I could be a bird. I would enjoy flying freely across the sky and seeing mountains, rivers and forests from above. I could travel long distances, build a nest in a tree and experience nature closely. A bird’s freedom and ability to move through the air would make life exciting.

5. Good values and habits we can learn from animals

  • They are placid and calm.
  • They are self-contained and self-reliant.
  • They do not constantly complain about their condition.
  • They are content with what they have.
  • They do not display an obsession with material possessions.
  • They do not pretend to be morally superior to others.

LineFigure of speechExplanation
(a) “I stand and look at them long and long.”Repetition“Long” is repeated.
(b) “They do not sweat and whine about their condition.”Alliteration / sound pattern (possible classroom answer)The repeated “w” sound in “whine” and nearby wording may be cited; the example is not an especially strong alliteration.
(c) “They do not make me sick discussing their duty to God.”Alliteration (possible classroom answer)The repeated initial “d” sound in “discussing” and “duty”.
(d) “... not one is demented with the mania of owning things.”Repetition in the wider passage“Not one” repeats in the preceding lines; the quoted line alone is not a clear example of hyperbole.

8. Appreciation of the poem “Animals”

Poet: Walt Whitman. Title: “Animals”. Theme: The poet admires animals for their calmness, self-reliance and contentment and contrasts these qualities with human dissatisfaction, hypocrisy and greed. The poem is written in free verse, without a regular rhyme scheme. Its simple language, repetition and contrasts make its message clear. The phrase “tokens of myself” is a metaphor for virtues such as innocence, simplicity and contentment. I like the poem because it encourages people to become more honest, humble and satisfied with what they have.

9. Debate: Is the life of an animal better than that of a human being?

In favour: Animals live simply, do not worry about social status and are not obsessed with material possessions. They often live close to nature.

Against: Animals face predators, disease, hunger and environmental threats. Humans can reason, create art, build communities and improve living conditions.

My view: Neither life is always better. The poem reminds humans to learn calmness and contentment from animals while using our own abilities responsibly and compassionately.

7. Quick Revision Sheet

  • Theme: The superiority of animal nature over human nature due to the absence of greed, dissatisfaction, and hypocrisy in animals.
  • Key takeaway: Humans have lost their natural virtues (tokens) like innocence and contentment, which animals still possess.
  • Poetic Style: Free verse (no rhyme scheme or set meter).

1. Chapter Overview

“If” is a didactic poem by Rudyard Kipling. It presents a father's advice to his son about the qualities needed to face life with maturity and become a good human being.

The poem emphasises self-control, self-confidence, patience, honesty, emotional balance, humility, courage, perseverance and wise use of time. The textbook explicitly describes it as advice given by a father to his son. fileciteturn181file0L87-L94

Central message: A person should remain balanced in success and failure, stay truthful without becoming bitter, preserve goodness while dealing with all kinds of people, and continue rebuilding after setbacks.

2. About the Poet — Rudyard Kipling

  • Rudyard Kipling lived from 1865 to 1936.
  • He was an English journalist, short-story writer, poet and novelist.
  • He was one of the popular writers in the United Kingdom in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries.
  • He received the Nobel Prize for Literature in 1907 at the age of 42.
  • The textbook notes that he was the first writer writing in English to receive the Nobel Prize.

Model Answers

1. How do you react when your parents scold you?

I try to remain calm, understand why they are upset and correct my mistake if I have made one. If I feel that I have been misunderstood, I discuss the matter respectfully later.

2. How do you feel when someone insults you?

I may feel hurt, but I should control my anger and avoid responding with another insult. I should try to understand the situation and respond with dignity.

3. How would you react when you are cheated by your friends?

I would first find out the facts, speak honestly with my friend and decide how to handle the situation without taking revenge.

4. Why is it wrong to make a difference among friends who belong to a different class, colour or community?

People should be treated with dignity and fairness. Class, colour and community do not determine a person's worth. Friendship should be based on mutual respect, trust and good character.

4. Poem — Meaning and Explanation

Stanza 1 — Self-control and patience

“If you can keep your head when all about you / Are losing theirs and blaming it on you;”

The poet advises the reader to remain calm when other people are confused, angry or blaming him. He should trust himself but should also consider why others may doubt him.

He should wait patiently without becoming tired of waiting. If people lie about him, he should not answer with lies. If people hate him, he should not allow hatred to control him. At the same time, he should avoid becoming self-righteous or showing off his knowledge.

The first part of the poem and its glossary appear on page 87. fileciteturn181file0L71-L86

Stanza 2 — Balance between dreams, thoughts, success and failure

“If you can dream and not make dreams your master; / If you can think and not make thoughts your aim;”

Dreams and thoughts are valuable, but they should not control a person's whole life. The poet then contrasts Triumph and Disaster. Both are called “imposters”, suggesting that neither success nor failure should be allowed to define the person completely.

A mature person should be able to hear the truth even when dishonest people have twisted it. If something built with great effort is destroyed, the person should have the courage to bend down and rebuild it with whatever limited resources remain.

Stanza 3 — Humility and equality

“If you can talk with crowds and keep your virtue, / Or walk with Kings, nor lose the common touch;”

A truly mature person can interact with ordinary people and important people without changing his basic goodness. Neither enemies nor close friends should be given unhealthy control over him. People may be important, but no one should become so important that the person loses his balance.

Stanza 4 — Use every second

“If you can fill the unforgiving minute / With sixty seconds’ worth of distance run...”

Time does not wait for anyone. The poet advises the reader to use every second productively. If these qualities are developed, “the Earth and everything that's in it” can become his, and he will become a good, complete human being.

3. Why do the lines begin with “If you can”?

The repeated opening “If you can” creates a strong conditional pattern. It presents one quality or challenge after another and makes the reader think about whether he or she can develop those qualities. The repetition also creates rhythm and emphasis and connects the separate pieces of advice into one continuous test of character.

6. Attitude Towards People of Different Strata and Classes

We should treat people from different social, economic and cultural backgrounds with equal dignity and respect. We should neither feel superior to others nor judge them merely by their status. The poem encourages us to talk with crowds and walk with Kings without losing the “common touch”.

7. Highest Quality of a True Human Being

According to the poem, the highest quality is the balanced development of character: self-control, courage, patience, integrity, humility, perseverance and wise use of time. A person becomes a true human being by maintaining goodness and balance even when faced with success, failure, hatred, doubt or loss.

10. Model Long Answers

Q. What is the central idea of “If”?

The central idea of “If” is that true maturity is achieved through balanced character. A person should remain calm when others lose control, trust himself while considering others' doubts, remain truthful without becoming hateful, and treat success and failure alike. He should remain humble with both ordinary people and powerful people, use every second wisely and have the courage to rebuild after loss. These qualities, according to the poem, lead a person towards becoming a good and complete human being.

Q. Explain the significance of Triumph and Disaster being called “imposters”.

Triumph and Disaster represent two extreme experiences of life: success and failure. The poet calls both of them “imposters” because neither should be allowed to control a person's identity or emotions. Success can make a person proud, while failure can make a person hopeless. The ideal person remains balanced in both situations and continues to act according to his values.

Q. How does the poem teach perseverance?

The poem teaches perseverance through the image of rebuilding. Even when the things for which a person has worked hard are broken, he should not surrender. He should stoop down and rebuild them with “worn-out tools”. The image suggests courage, determination and the willingness to begin again even when resources are limited.

Q. What does the poem say about time?

The poem presents the minute as “unforgiving” because time never stops or waits for anyone. The reader is advised to fill every minute with useful effort, represented by “sixty seconds' worth of distance run”. The message is to avoid wasting time and make productive use of every moment.

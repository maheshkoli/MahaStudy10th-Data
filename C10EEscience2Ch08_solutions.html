1. Chapter at a Glance

This chapter explores the fundamental concepts of cell biology, specifically focusing on stem cells, their types, and their applications in regenerative therapy and organ transplantation. It also covers the field of biotechnology, detailing its applications in agriculture, human health, and environmental management. Students will learn about the revolutionary potential of these fields in modern science.

2. Important Concepts Explained

Cytology (Cell Biology)

Cytology is the study of the structure, types, and organelles of the cell. It also includes the study of cell division.

Stem Cells

Stem cells are special, undifferentiated cells present in the body of multicellular organisms. They have the ability to give rise to all other types of cells in the body.

  • Embryonic Stem Cells: These are derived from the zygote during the early stages of development (5th–7th day). They are undifferentiated and possess "pluripotency," meaning they can transform into any type of cell, tissue, or organ.
  • Adult Stem Cells: These can be obtained from the body of an adult person, specifically from red bone marrow, adipose connective tissue, and blood.

Stem Cell Preservation

Stem cell samples are collected from sources like cord blood, red bone marrow, or embryos (blastocysts) and stored in small, sterile vials in liquid nitrogen at temperatures between -135°C to -190°C.

Uses of Stem Cells

  1. Regenerative Therapy:
  • Cell Therapy: Used to replace dead cells in conditions like diabetes, myocardial infarction, Alzheimer’s disease, and Parkinson’s disease.
  • Blood Cell Production: Used to treat conditions like anemia, thalassaemia, and leukemia.
  1. Organ Transplantation: Organs like the kidney and liver can potentially be produced using stem cells for transplantation.

Organ and Body Donation

  • Organs like the liver, heart, and eyes can be donated after death.
  • Living donors can donate one kidney or skin from certain parts of the body.
  • Organ donation is governed by the 'Transplantation of Human Organs Act, 1994'.

Biotechnology

Biotechnology involves bringing about artificial genetic changes and hybridization in organisms for human welfare. It integrates branches like cytology, biochemistry, molecular biology, and genetic engineering.

Commercial Applications of Biotechnology

  1. Crop Biotechnology:
  • Hybrid Seeds: Combining genes of two different crops.
  • Genetically Modified (GM) Crops: Integrating foreign genes to improve resistance to diseases, alkalinity, and drought. Examples include Bt cotton, Bt brinjal, and Golden Rice.
  • Herbicide Tolerant Plants: Developed to selectively destroy weeds without affecting the main crop.
  1. Biofertilizers: Using bacteria like Rhizobium, Azotobacter, Nostoc, Anabaena, and plants like Azolla to improve nitrogen fixation and phosphate solubilization.
  2. Animal Husbandry: Using artificial insemination and embryo transfer to improve the quality and quantity of animal products.
  3. Human Health:
  • Vaccines: Producing safer, thermo-stable vaccines using biotechnology.
  • Edible Vaccines: Transgenic potatoes that generate immunity against diseases like cholera.
  • Treatment: Production of insulin, somatotropin, and blood clotting factors.
  • Interferon: Produced using transgenic E. coli to treat viral diseases.
  • Gene Therapy: Treating genetic disorders in somatic cells (e.g., Phenylketonuria).
  • Cloning: Production of a replica of a cell, organ, or organism.

3. Definitions and Key Terms

  • Pluripotency: The ability of embryonic stem cells to transform into any type of cell in the human body.
  • Somatic Cells: All cells in the body except for sperms and ova.
  • Biotechnology: The application of scientific and engineering principles to the processing of materials by biological agents to provide goods and services.
  • Transgenic Crops: Crops developed by integrating foreign genes into their genome.
  • Cloning: The production of a replica of any cell, organ, or entire organism.

4. Formulas, Rules, and Properties

  • Storage Temperature for Stem Cells: -135°C to -190°C (in liquid nitrogen).
  • Cell Differentiation: Starts from the 14th day of conception.

4. Write short notes

a. Biotechnology: professional uses

  • Agriculture: Development of hybrid seeds, genetically modified crops, herbicide-tolerant varieties and biofertilizers to improve yield and crop resistance.
  • Animal husbandry: Artificial insemination and embryo transfer to improve the quantity and quality of milk, meat and wool.
  • Human health: Production of insulin, vaccines, antibiotics, interferon and blood-clotting factors; diagnosis and gene therapy applications.
  • Industry and food: Production of alcohol and fermented foods using microorganisms.
  • Environment: Sewage treatment, composting, bioremediation and the use of biosensors and biopesticides.

b. Importance of medicinal plants

Medicinal plants contain active ingredients used in traditional and modern remedies. Cultivating them helps maintain a supply as wild plants become scarce due to forest depletion. The textbook gives Adulsa (Adhatoda vasica) as an example and lists vasicine in its leaves for cough.

5. Answer in your own words

a. Which products produced through biotechnology do you use in daily life?

Examples include yoghurt, cheese, bread, vinegar, fermented foods, medicines such as insulin when prescribed, and vaccines. The exact list depends on personal use.

b. Which precautions will you take during spraying of pesticides?

  • Read and follow the product label and use only the recommended quantity.
  • Wear protective clothing, gloves, a mask/respirator and eye protection as directed.
  • Avoid spraying in strong wind; keep children, other people and animals away.
  • Do not eat, drink or smoke while spraying; wash after use and dispose of containers safely.
  • Prevent pesticide from entering water bodies and follow the stated waiting period before harvesting.

c. Why are some organs in the human body most valuable?

Organs such as the heart, liver and kidneys perform essential functions. If they fail, life may be endangered. Donation and transplantation can save or improve the lives of recipients; donor-recipient compatibility and medical suitability must be assessed.

d. Explain the importance of fruit processing in human life.

Fruits are perishable. Processing methods such as cold storage, drying, salting, airtight packing, making murabba and evaporation help preserve fruit products and make them available for longer periods of the year. Processing also supports food industries and reduces post-harvest waste.

e. Explain the meaning of vaccination.

Vaccination is the administration of a vaccine containing antigenic material to stimulate the immune system and provide temporary or lasting protection against a specific pathogen or disease.

7. Important Questions and Answers

  1. What is the significance of stem cell research?
  • Stem cell research is a revolutionary field with the potential to fundamentally change medical science by enabling regenerative therapy and organ production.
  1. What is the role of biotechnology in agriculture?
  • It improves crop yield and variety through hybrid seeds, GM crops (like Bt cotton), and herbicide-tolerant plants, and enhances soil fertility through biofertilizers.
  1. What is the difference between reproductive cloning and therapeutic cloning?
  • Reproductive cloning aims to produce a new organism. Therapeutic cloning aims to derive stem cells from a laboratory-formed cell for treating diseases.

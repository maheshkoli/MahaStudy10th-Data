8.1 Tourism in the Past

Travelling has a long tradition in India. People travelled for pilgrimage, fairs and festivals, education, searching for renowned teachers, and trade. Buddhist literature describes Gautam Buddha travelling to different cities to preach. Buddhist monks were expected to keep travelling; Jain monks and sadhus also travelled. The chapter mentions Yuan Shwang’s journey to India in 630 C.E. and the extensive travels of Saint Namdev, Saint Eknath, Guru Nanak and Ramdas Swami.

Tourism: The textbook defines tourism as travelling to visit places in distant regions for a specific purpose.

8.2 Types of Tourism

Tourism has become a field of local, interstate, national and international business. People travel to see monuments, natural beauty, handicraft centres, pilgrimage sites, industrial centres and development projects.

TypeMeaning and examples from the textbook
Local and interstate tourismTravel within one’s own country; it generally involves fewer language, currency and document difficulties than international travel.
International tourismTravel between countries, made easier by rail, marine and air transport. People travel for study, relaxation, sightseeing, professional assignments and film shooting.
Religious tourismTravel to places associated with religious or mythological traditions. The chapter notes Ahilyabai Holkar’s efforts to improve amenities on pilgrim routes.
Historical tourismVisiting historical places. Examples include forts in Maharashtra and Rajasthan, as well as ashrams associated with Mahatma Gandhi and Acharya Vinoba Bhave.
Geographic tourismVisiting distinctive natural and geographic features, such as the Valley of Flowers, beaches, Lonar crater lake and the naturally carved rock cavities at Nighoj called ranjankhalage.
Health tourismTravelling for medical services and health facilities, Yoga training or Ayurveda therapies.
Agro-tourismVisiting rural areas and agricultural activities, particularly for urban visitors with limited exposure to farming.
Sports tourismTravelling to attend sports events such as the Olympics, Wimbledon, World Chess Championship, international cricket tournaments, Himalayan Car Rally and Maharashtra Kesari wrestling competitions.
Special-event tourismTravel to attend film festivals, seminars, conferences, international book exhibitions and cultural events such as the Akhil Bharatiya Marathi Sahitya Sammelan.

The textbook also invites students to learn about science tourism, leisure and relaxation (wellness) tourism, cultural tourism and group tourism.

8.3 Development of Tourism

Tourism develops when visitors receive reliable guidance about travel, local customs and conduct at destinations. The textbook highlights these important needs:

  • Safety and transport: safe transport and security arrangements.
  • Basic facilities: good accommodation and washroom facilities along travel routes.
  • Accessibility: attention to the special needs of tourists with disabilities.
  • Guidance and information: pamphlets, guides and history books about tourist sites.
  • Language support: literature in multiple languages and training taxi drivers to converse in more than one language or work as tourist guides.
  • Cleanliness and responsible conduct: preventing litter and avoiding damage to heritage sites.

The textbook also asks students to gather information about the Swachh Bharat drive in connection with keeping tourist places clean.

8.4 Conservation and Preservation of Heritage

The textbook divides heritage into natural heritage and cultural (man-made) heritage. India has historical sites from ancient, medieval and modern periods, as well as important natural heritage.

Examples named in the chapter include the Taj Mahal at Agra, Jantar Mantar at Jaipur, the rock-cut caves at Ajanta, Verul (Ellora) and Gharapuri (Elephanta), Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Terminus in Maharashtra, and the Kas Plateau in the Western Ghats, identified in the textbook as a World Natural Heritage site.

How to protect heritage sites

  • Keep heritage sites clean.
  • Do not write names on walls, draw on monuments, carve trees or deface historic structures.
  • Do not paint ancient monuments in garish colours or vandalise them.
  • Support conservation projects, collect authentic information and share it responsibly.

Heritage walks

A heritage walk is a tour to visit historical places. Organised heritage walks help participants experience history and can encourage support for conservation, accurate documentation and public education. The chapter mentions heritage walks in Ahmedabad and in Mumbai and Pune. It also describes the use of “blue plaques” in some cities to identify residences of renowned people who have passed away.

8.5 Professional Opportunities in Tourism and Hospitality

Tourism and hospitality can generate many employment opportunities. Visitors spend money on travel, hotels, food, translators, guides, newspapers, reference books and souvenirs. Tourist activity can increase demand for local food products and ethnic handicrafts, supporting artisans, business people and wage earners.

Examples of work linked to tourism

  • Tourist guides and heritage interpreters.
  • Hotel, accommodation, food and hospitality services.
  • Transport and travel-agency services.
  • Translation and multilingual visitor support.
  • Photography and local visitor services.
  • Handicrafts, cottage industries and locally processed food businesses.

Tourism in Maharashtra

The chapter lists caves and heritage sites at Ajanta, Verul and Gharapuri; pilgrimage centres such as Dehu, Alandi, Jejuri, Pandharpur, Shirdi, Shegaon, Tuljapur, Kolhapur, Nashik, Tryambakeshwar, Paithan, Haji Malang and the Gurudwara at Nanded; hill stations such as Mahabaleshwar, Panchgani, Khandala, Lonavala, Matheran and Chikhaldara; dams such as Koyananagar, Jayakwadi, Bhatghar and Chandoli; and sanctuaries such as Dajipur, Sagareshwar and Tadoba.

The textbook states that the Maharashtra Tourism Development Corporation (MTDC) was established in 1975 and provided tourist hotel facilities at 47 places, with a combined capacity of more than 4,000 tourists.

Bhilar — the Village of Books

The chapter describes Bhilar near Mahabaleshwar as the “Village of Books.” Households maintain libraries for visitors, as part of a Maharashtra government initiative to promote reading culture among tourists and to showcase Marathi literature.

3. Write notes

(1) Tradition of Travelling:
Travelling has existed in India since ancient times. People travelled for pilgrimage, fairs and festivals, education, searching for teachers and trade. Buddhist and Jain monks travelled, as did saints such as Namdev, Eknath, Guru Nanak and Ramdas Swami. Travel accounts such as Maza Pravas provide valuable historical information.

Marco Polo was an Italian traveller of the 13th century. The textbook says he stayed in China for 17 years and wrote about Asia’s flora, fauna, social life, culture and trade systems. His travel account helped introduce Asia, especially China, to Europe.

(3) Agro-tourism:
Agro-tourism, also called agri-tourism, allows visitors—especially people from urban areas—to experience rural life and agriculture. The chapter also describes farmers visiting agricultural research centres, universities and places such as Israel to learn about advanced agricultural technology.

4. Answer in 25–30 words

(1) What are the crucial issues in the development of tourism?
Answer: Tourism needs proper guidance, safe transport and security, good accommodation, clean washrooms, accessible facilities for disabled tourists, multilingual information, trained guides and protection of tourist and heritage sites.

(2) In what way does tourism become a major source of livelihood?
Answer: Tourism provides work for guides, photographers, horsekeepers, hotel staff, translators and transport providers. It also increases demand for local food, handicrafts and cottage-industry products, improving local incomes.

(3) What would you do to develop the surroundings of your village/town to help tourism grow?
Answer: I would keep local attractions clean, protect heritage sites, improve basic facilities, prepare reliable visitor information, train local guides, promote local culture and handicrafts, and encourage responsible tourism.

Project: Why preserve heritage sites, and what measures are needed?

Need: Heritage sites preserve evidence of history, culture and the natural environment. They provide learning opportunities and attract visitors, supporting tourism and local livelihoods.

Measures: Keep sites clean; prohibit writing, carving and other vandalism; maintain structures and visitor amenities; provide accurate multilingual information; educate visitors; and encourage local communities to participate in conservation and heritage walks.

Important Questions and Quick Revision

Important questions

Why are travel accounts useful to historians?
They record places, people, customs, trade, social life and events observed by travellers. The chapter describes accounts such as those of Marco Polo, Ibn Batuta and Vishnubhat Godase as useful historical sources.

How does tourism help the local economy?
Tourists spend on transport, accommodation, food, guides, translation, photographs and souvenirs. Their spending can increase demand for local products and handicrafts, creating work and income for local people.

What is a heritage walk?
A heritage walk is an organised tour of historical places. It can help people experience history, learn reliable information and support conservation.

What is the purpose of the Village of Books initiative?
The textbook describes Bhilar as a village near Mahabaleshwar where households maintain libraries for visitors, helping promote reading culture and Marathi literature.

Quick revision sheet

  • Tourism development: guidance, safe transport, accommodation, accessibility, cleanliness and multilingual information.
  • Heritage: natural and cultural heritage; prevent vandalism and support conservation.
  • Careers: guides, hotels, travel services, translation, photography, food, handicrafts and other local businesses.
  • Maharashtra examples: Ajanta, Verul, Gharapuri, Kas Plateau, Bhilar and the tourist centres listed in the chapter.

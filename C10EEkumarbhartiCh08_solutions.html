1. Chapter at a Glance

This chapter is a parable by Leo Tolstoy. It tells the story of a king who seeks answers to three crucial questions to ensure he never fails in his undertakings. The chapter explores themes of wisdom, the importance of the present moment, and the value of serving others. Students will learn to analyze character traits, understand the moral of a story, and practice language skills through various exercises.

2. Important Concepts Explained

  • The King's Quest: The king believes that if he knows the right time to begin everything, the right people to listen to, and the most important thing to do, he will never fail.
  • The Failure of Learned Men: The king receives many different answers from learned men, but none satisfy him. This highlights that there is no single, simple answer to life's complex questions.
  • The Hermit's Wisdom: The king visits a hermit, a man renowned for his wisdom. Through practical experience—helping the hermit dig and saving a wounded enemy—the king discovers the answers himself.
  • The Three Answers:
  1. The most important time is 'Now': It is the only time we have any power to act.
  2. The most necessary person is the one with whom you are: You never know if you will have dealings with anyone else.
  3. The most important thing is to do good to that person: This is the purpose for which we were sent into this life.

3. Definitions and Key Terms

  • Parable: A short story with a moral lesson, usually featuring human characters.
  • Proclamation: A public or official announcement.
  • Hermit: A person living in solitude as a religious discipline.
  • Ambush: A surprise attack by people lying in wait in a concealed position.
  • Crouching: Bending low with the legs bent.

5. Two points for each statement

(a) The learned advisers confused the king.

  • They gave different answers to the question about the right time.
  • They disagreed about the most necessary people and the most important occupation, so the king accepted none of their answers.

(b) The king was humble.

  • He put on simple clothes and left his bodyguard behind before visiting the hermit.
  • He personally dug the beds and cared for the wounded man instead of expecting others to do the work.

(c) The king’s enemy was repentant.

  • He asked the king to forgive him.
  • He admitted his plan to kill the king and offered to serve him if his life was spared.

(d) The hermit was truly wise.

  • He did not merely give abstract answers; he helped the king understand them through events.
  • He explained that the present moment, the person with us and doing good are what matter most.

7. Answer the questions

(a) Give one example of different opinions and one example where no answer was accepted.
For the first question, some advisers suggested preparing a timetable, while others advised paying attention to current events or consulting wise people. For the third question, the advisers suggested science, skill in warfare and religious worship, but the king was convinced by none of them.

(b) Was the hermit polite even though he did not answer at once?
Yes. He greeted the king and listened to his questions. He did not insult or dismiss him. Instead, he continued working and later invited the king to rest while he took the spade, responding through his actions.

(c) Why did the king wear simple clothes? Did the hermit know he was the king?
The king wore simple clothes because the hermit spoke only to common people. He may have wished to be treated like an ordinary visitor, while also showing humility. The story does not explicitly state how the hermit recognised him, so we cannot say for certain that the hermit knew his identity at the beginning.

(d) Did the king behave like an ordinary person and act kindly?
Yes. He left his bodyguard behind, dug the ground himself and cared for the wounded stranger. He washed and bandaged the man’s wound, gave him water and helped carry him into the hut. These actions show humility and compassion.

(e) Did the hermit know in advance about the king and the ambush?
The story does not say that the hermit knew in advance about the king’s arrival or the ambush. He responds to events as they occur and later explains how those events revealed the answers.

9. Summarise the story’s main aspects in 4–5 lines each

(a) King’s problem: The king wanted to know the right time for every action, the most necessary people and the most important thing to do. He believed that knowing these answers would prevent failure. He asked learned advisers, but their answers differed. He was not satisfied with any of them.

(b) Attempts to find a solution: The king announced a reward for anyone who could answer his questions. Learned people suggested timetables, attention to current events and a council of wise people. They also disagreed about which people and occupations mattered most. The king then visited a hermit known for his wisdom.

(c) Climax: While the king was working with the hermit, a wounded bearded man arrived. The king treated his wounds and saved his life. The stranger revealed that he had intended to kill the king to avenge his brother. The king’s kindness turned an enemy into a friend.

(d) Solution: The hermit explained that the most important time was the present moment. The most important person was the one with whom the king was at that moment. The most important thing was to do good to that person. The king had discovered the answers by helping the hermit and the wounded stranger.

(e) Message: We should focus on the present because it is the only time in which we can act. We should give attention to the people who are with us. We should help them and do good whenever we can. Kindness and compassion can change relationships and bring peace.

12. “Patience is bitter, but its fruit is sweet” — sample experience

Once I found mathematics difficult and could not solve many problems. I felt disappointed and wanted to give up. My teacher advised me to practise a little every day and learn from my mistakes. I followed the advice and revised the basic steps patiently. At first, my progress was slow, but I gradually understood the methods. After a few weeks, I could solve questions that had seemed impossible. I felt proud of my improvement. This experience taught me that success does not always come immediately. Patience, regular effort and confidence bring good results. The struggle may be unpleasant at first, but the final achievement is satisfying. This is why I believe that patience is bitter, but its fruit is sweet.

Background: The story is a moral tale by Leo Tolstoy about human desire and the temptation to acquire more land and wealth.

Characters: The central character is Pahom, a peasant who believes that owning more land will make him secure and happy.

Plot and theme: Pahom continually seeks more land. His desire grows, and he fails to recognise when he has enough. The story explores greed, ambition and the limits of material wealth.

Climax: His attempt to acquire more land leads to his physical collapse.

Message: Greed can destroy contentment. People should recognise their needs and value a balanced, meaningful life over endless accumulation.

8. Important Questions and Answers

  • Q: Why did the king go to the hermit in disguise?
  • A: The hermit was known to speak only to common folk. The king dressed in simple clothes to avoid being treated as a ruler, hoping the hermit would speak to him freely.
  • Q: How did the hermit finally point out the answers to the king’s questions?
  • A: The hermit explained that the king's actions—digging the beds and saving the wounded man—were the answers. The most important time was when the king was digging; the most important person was the hermit (and later the wounded man); and the most important business was doing good to them.

9. Common Errors and How to Avoid Them

  • Misinterpreting the Moral: Students often think the hermit gave the answers directly. Remember, the hermit used the king's own actions to demonstrate the answers.
  • Confusing the Characters: Ensure you distinguish between the king, the hermit, and the wounded enemy.

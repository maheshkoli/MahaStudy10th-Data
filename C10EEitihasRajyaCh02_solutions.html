Chapter at a Glance

This chapter studies the tradition of Indian historiography and the different ideological frameworks used to interpret the past. The textbook has two main sections: 2.1 Tradition of Indian Historiography and 2.2 Indian Historiography: Various Ideological Frameworks.

2.1 Tradition of Indian Historiography

Historiography in the Ancient Period

  • Memories of ancestors, mythological lore and social transitions were preserved through oral recitals.
  • Harappan seals and artefacts show that writing was practised early, although the Harappan script has not yet been deciphered.
  • The textbook identifies inscriptions from the 3rd century B.C.E., including Emperor Ashoka’s edicts on rocks and pillars, as the earliest known written historical documents found in India.
  • From the 1st century C.E., inscriptions also appeared on coins, metal images, sculptures and copper plates. They provide information about kings, dynasties, territories, administration, political events, social organisation, climate and famines.
  • Literary sources include the Ramayana, Mahabharata, Puranas, Jain and Buddhist texts, Indian historical accounts and travelogues by foreign travellers.

Biographies and Medieval Historiography

  • Harshacharita, written by Banabhatta in the 7th century C.E., is a biography of King Harsha and describes social, economic, political, religious and cultural life.
  • Kalhana’s 12th-century Rajtarangini is a history of Kashmir. The textbook notes that he critically examined inscriptions, coins, ancient monuments, dynastic records and local traditions.
  • Ziauddin Barani, in Tarikh-i-Firuz Shahi, argued that historians should record a ruler’s failures and incorrect policies as well as achievements.
  • Babur’s autobiography, Tuzuk-i-Babari, describes battles, places, local economies, customs and flora.
  • Abul Fazl’s Akbarnama is highlighted for collecting and scrutinising historical documents.

Bakhars

A Bakhar is an important type of medieval historical document. It may contain eulogies of heroes and accounts of historical events, battles and notable lives.

  • Sabhasad Bakhar — information about Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj’s rule.
  • Bhausahebanchi Bakhar and Panipatachi Bakhar — accounts associated with the Battle of Panipat.
  • Holkaranchi Kaiphiyat — information about the Holkars and their contribution to Maratha rule.

Modern Historiography

  • Sir Alexander Cunningham was the first Director General of the Archaeological Survey of India.
  • The textbook associates the discovery of Harappan civilisation during John Marshall’s tenure with evidence of India’s antiquity.
  • British writers discussed in the chapter include James Mill, Mountstuart Elphinstone and Grant Duff. The textbook points out colonial prejudice in some historical accounts.
  • Indian historians Nilkanth Janardan Kirtane and Vishwanath Kashinath Rajwade exposed limitations in Grant Duff’s writings.

2.2 Indian Historiography: Various Ideological Frameworks

Colonial Historiography

The textbook explains that some early British officers and Christian missionaries wrote Indian history from prejudiced perspectives. Their writings were sometimes used to justify colonial rule. The Cambridge History of India, published between 1922 and 1937, is mentioned as an example.

Orientalist Historiography

Orientalists were European scholars interested in the civilisations and languages of the East. They studied Sanskrit, Vedic tradition and Indian literature. Sir William Jones founded the Asiatic Society in Kolkata in 1784. Friedrich Max Müller is another scholar discussed in the textbook.

Nationalistic Historiography

Indian historians sought to restore pride in India’s past and challenge colonial prejudice. The textbook mentions Vishnushastri Chiplunkar, M. G. Ranade, R. G. Bhandarkar, V. D. Savarkar, R. C. Majumdar and V. K. Rajwade, among others.

V. K. Rajwade emphasised writing history from authentic documentary sources and compiled and edited 22 volumes of Marathyanchya Itihasachi Sadhane. V. D. Savarkar’s The Indian War of Independence, 1857 is discussed as an important nationalistic work.

Historiography after Independence

The textbook describes greater attention to social, cultural and economic history, as well as the histories of communities, sciences, economic systems and political ideologies. Three important ideological schools discussed are Marxist, Subaltern and Feminist history.

Marxist History

Marxist historians focus on means and modes of production, industrial relations and the influence of these factors on social events. The textbook names D. D. Kosambi, S. A. Dange, R. S. Sharma and Sharad Patil.

Subaltern History

Subaltern refers to the bottommost ranks of society. This approach focuses on groups whose roles were often neglected in earlier historical writing. The chapter discusses Antonio Gramsci, Ranjit Guha, Mahatma Jyotirao Phule and Dr Babasaheb Ambedkar. Phule’s Gulamgiri and Ambedkar’s Who Were the Shudras and The Untouchables are cited.

Feminist History

Feminist history re-examines the past from women’s perspectives and studies women’s roles, work, rights and experiences. The textbook highlights Tarabai Shinde’s Stri-Purush Tulana (1882), Pandita Ramabai’s The High Caste Hindu Woman (1888), Meera Kosambi and Dr Sharmila Rege.

Historians beyond a Single Ideology

The textbook names Jadunath Sarkar, Surendra Nath Sen, G. S. Sardesai and T. S. Shejwalkar as historians who wrote without embracing one particular ideology.

Exercise Practice and Answers

1. Complete the sentences

(1) The first Director General of the Archaeological Survey of India was: Sir Alexander Cunningham.

(2) The Sanskrit text of Hitopadesh was translated by: Sir William Jones.

Identify the wrong pair and correct it

  • Who Were the Shudras — Dr Babasaheb Ambedkar: correct.
  • Stri-Purush Tulana — Feminist writing: correct.
  • The Indian War of Independence, 1857 — Marxist history: incorrect. The textbook discusses it under nationalistic historiography.
  • Grant Duff — Colonial history: consistent with the chapter’s discussion of colonial influence.

Short-answer preparation

Why did regional history writing receive momentum?
The textbook says nationalistic historiography encouraged attention to regional histories, including the geographical conditions and histories of South Indian regions.

Why is a Bakhar an important historical document?
A Bakhar preserves accounts of heroes, battles, historical events and notable lives, and can provide information about rulers and regional history.

What did Babur describe in his autobiography?
According to the textbook, Babur described battles, regions and cities he visited, including local economies, customs and flora.

What was Savarkar’s contribution to nationalistic historiography?
The textbook identifies The Indian War of Independence, 1857 as an important work in nationalistic historiography.

Long-answer preparation

What is Marxist history?
Marxist history examines means and modes of production and industrial relations, and studies how these shape social events. The textbook gives examples of Indian historians who adopted this framework.

What was V. K. Rajwade’s contribution?
Rajwade argued that Indians should write their own history and insisted on authentic documentary sources. He compiled and edited 22 volumes of Marathyanchya Itihasachi Sadhane and wrote scholarly prefaces to them.

Explain Orientalist historiography.
Orientalist historiography involved European scholars studying Eastern civilisations, particularly Sanskrit and Vedic literature. Sir William Jones founded the Asiatic Society in Kolkata in 1784, helping research into Indian literature and history.

Explain Subaltern history.
Subaltern history focuses on the experiences and contributions of people in the bottommost ranks of society, whose roles were often neglected in colonial and nationalistic accounts.

Explain Feminist history.
Feminist history re-examines historical narratives from women’s perspectives, records women’s writings and experiences, and investigates women’s social roles, employment and rights.

Quick Revision

  • Ancient sources: oral recitals, inscriptions, coins, copper plates, epics, Puranas, Jain and Buddhist texts.
  • Medieval sources: biographies, court histories, travelogues and Bakhars.
  • Colonial historiography: often influenced by colonial perspectives.
  • Orientalist historiography: European study of Eastern cultures and Sanskrit literature.
  • Nationalistic historiography: sought to challenge colonial prejudice and restore pride in India’s past.
  • Marxist history: production, economic relations and social change.
  • Subaltern history: people in the bottommost ranks of society.
  • Feminist history: women’s experiences and perspectives in historical writing.

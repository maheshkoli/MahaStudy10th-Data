Chapter at a Glance

Chapter 7, Sports and History, explains how sports connect entertainment, physical exercise, culture, technology, literature, films and careers. Its seven sections cover the importance of sports, types of sports, globalisation, game materials and toys, toys and history, sports in literature and movies, and professional opportunities.

Learning goals: distinguish indoor and outdoor games; explain how sports and toys reveal aspects of the past; connect global sports with the economy; and identify careers where historical knowledge is useful.

7.1 Importance of Sports

Sports combine entertainment with physical exercise. The textbook explains that playing games helps people relax, refresh the mind, build a strong and tenacious body, and develop courage, determination and sportsmanship.

Games involving collective participation also develop cooperation, team spirit and leadership. The chapter notes that sports and games have existed since the beginning of civilisation. Ancient Indian literature and epics mention dice games (dyut), wrestling, horse and chariot races, and chess.

The textbook describes the ancient Olympic competitions as being held at Olympia in Greece. It says the Greeks standardised sports rules and organised competitions such as discus throw, horse and chariot races, wrestling and boxing.

7.2 Types of Sports

Indoor games

Indoor games are played in a closed environment, and some can be played while sitting in one place. The textbook examples include chess, card games, games using game pieces and dice, carom, Kachkavadya / Chaukabara (Indian Ludo), Sagargote (played with seeds or stones), and Bhatukali (playing house).

Outdoor games

The textbook divides outdoor games into Indian sports and international sports.

Sports competitions

Sports competitions are organised at local, city, taluka, district, state, national and international levels. The chapter mentions the Olympics, Asian Games (Asiad), Paralympic Games, Special Olympics and events such as the Cricket World Cup. It highlights Major Dhyan Chand’s hockey career and notes that 29 August, his birth date, is celebrated as National Sports Day in India.

7.3 Globalisation of Sports

The chapter explains that sports were influenced by globalisation during the 20th and 21st centuries. Television allows international matches in sports such as cricket, football and lawn tennis to be watched in many parts of the world, including by people in countries not taking part in the match.

Global audiences have affected the structure of the sports economy. Fans watch matches for entertainment, commercial companies use them as advertising opportunities, and retired sportspersons may work as commentators.

7.4 Game Materials and Toys

The textbook defines toys as means and equipment used for children’s entertainment and education. Clay toys have been found in archaeological excavations; they were made by hand or with moulds.

Ancient Indian literature mentions dolls. The Sanskrit play Mrichchhakatika by Shudraka means “a clay cart.” The chapter also refers to Kathasaritsagara, which describes unusual dolls that could fly, dance or make sounds when a key was pressed.

The textbook says wooden dolls made in Maharashtra were known as Thaki.

7.5 Toys and History

Toys can reveal information about history, technological development, religious practices and cultural traditions. The chapter describes the Diwali tradition in Maharashtra of making model forts and placing clay images of Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj, his soldiers, people and animals on them. This keeps alive the memory of the historical role of forts in Maharashtra.

An ivory doll made by Indian craftsmen was found during excavations at Pompeii in Italy and is dated in the textbook to the 1st century C.E. The chapter uses this artefact as evidence that helps illuminate Indo-Roman trade relations. Thus, objects found in archaeological excavations can provide clues about cultural contacts between ancient societies.

7.6 Sports, Literature and Movies

Books, encyclopaedias, magazines and television channels devoted to sports are part of the growing sports-information field. The textbook mentions a publication on Mallakhamb, an encyclopaedia on exercise and the former sports magazine Shatkar.

The chapter names the films Mary Kom and Dangal as examples of films about sports and sportspersons. It describes Mary Kom as a film about the Indian female boxer who participated in the Olympics and won a bronze medal, and Dangal as based on the lives of the Phogat sisters.

Making a film based on a past period requires research into the period’s language, clothing, social life and other details. The textbook points out that students of history are trained to research such contexts.

7.7 Sports and Professional Opportunities

The chapter identifies several career paths that connect sports and historical knowledge.

  • Sports journalism: Writing articles and reviews about events such as the Olympics, Asian Games and national or international matches.
  • Sports commentary: Commentators need knowledge of a game’s history, statistics, previous records, notable players and historical anecdotes. The chapter highlights Bal J. Pandit’s informative cricket commentary on Akashvani.
  • Sports broadcasting records: Professionals who track and maintain records of sports telecasts have an important role as channels broadcast matches and sports programmes.
  • Refereeing: Trained referees must pass qualifying examinations and may work at district, state, national and international levels.
  • Support for sportspersons: The textbook mentions scholarships and reserved positions in government and private establishments as ways the sports field is supported.

Important Questions and Quick Revision

Important questions

Why are toys useful to historians?
Toys can reveal materials, manufacturing techniques, cultural traditions and trade links. Archaeological finds, such as the ivory doll found at Pompeii mentioned in the textbook, can provide evidence of contacts between societies.

How has globalisation changed sports?
International matches can be watched around the world. This creates global audiences, advertising opportunities and roles for commentators, alongside changes in the sports economy.

Why does a sports commentator need historical knowledge?
The textbook says commentators benefit from knowledge of game history, statistics, past records, eminent players and historical anecdotes, which can make commentary informative and engaging.

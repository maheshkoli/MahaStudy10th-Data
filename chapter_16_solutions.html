1. Chapter Overview

“The Alchemy of Nature” by Raksha Bharadia is an insightful essay in which the author observes ordinary phenomena in nature and discovers meaningful lessons for human life. The chapter presents nature as a teacher that soothes, nurtures, fulfils and motivates us. The existing solution file identifies the central ideas as resilience, persistence, positive thinking and learning from nature. fileciteturn185file1L21-L37

3. Nature — The Best Teacher

The author says that people instinctively turn to outdoor activities and nature to relax and improve wellbeing. Nature “soothes and nurtures” and “fulfils and motivates.” The important question is whether we are willing to listen to what nature is communicating. fileciteturn185file0L36-L41

Hibiscus Flower

A hibiscus in the author's garden produces a large, bright and tender flower every fortnight. During the day it appears to smile with the sun and dance with the wind. By evening it begins wilting; the following morning it has withered, and by evening it falls and becomes one with the earth. Though its life lasts only a day, it lives in full splendour.

Lesson: Life may be short, but it should be lived fully and with purpose. We should make the most of the time we have.

Water and Rock

At a rocky beach, the author sees water shaping hard rocks. Water is gentle, while rock is hard, yet continuous flowing water gradually carves, sculpts and shapes the rock. fileciteturn185file0L51-L57

Lesson: Persistence and consistency can overcome even very large problems. A gentle but continuous effort can have a powerful effect.

Grass Through Concrete

Small pieces of grass are seen peeping through cracks in a concrete pavement. This makes the author think that even when something looks impossible, there is always an opening. fileciteturn185file0L58-L61

The Tree in Winter and Spring

The author sees a tree completely bare of leaves during winter and initially thinks its chapter is over. After three months, spring arrives and the tree returns to green majesty with leaves, flowers, birds and life. fileciteturn185file0L62-L66

6. Textual Questions and Answers

Q1. What lesson do we learn from the hibiscus plant?

The hibiscus flower lives for only a short time, yet it blooms in full splendour. It teaches us to live our own life to the fullest, irrespective of how long it lasts.

Q2. Explain the line “Nature whispers and commands.”

Nature communicates lessons through its quiet actions and changes. At the same time, those natural processes strongly guide us. The author asks us to observe and listen carefully so that we can understand these lessons.

Q3. What smiles with the sun and dances with the wind?

The hibiscus flower smiles with the sun and dances with the wind.

Q4. What kind of rock did the author find on the beach?

The author found hard rocks alongside the majestic ocean. The rocks had been carved, sculpted and shaped by water.

Q5. The rock is hard, but is it dominating? Give a reason.

No. Although the rock is hard, continuous flowing water gradually shapes it. This shows that hardness does not always mean dominance; persistence can overcome resistance.

Q6. In what condition was the tree in the cold winter months?

The tree was bare of all leaves. The author thought its chapter was over, but spring later brought it back to green majesty with leaves, flowers, birds and life.

Q7. What do we learn from small bits of grass?

Grass growing through cracks in concrete teaches us that however impossible something may look, there is always an opening or a possible way forward.

Q8. What do we find in the infinite sky?

The author says that innumerable stars shine across the infinite sky.

Q9. How can learning from nature help human beings?

Nature teaches us resilience, persistence, hope, adaptability, teamwork, consistency and focused effort. Observing nature can help us develop a positive attitude towards difficult situations.

3. “Impossible” Says “I M Possible” — Do You Agree?

Yes, I agree. The lesson gives several examples showing that something that looks impossible can become possible through persistence, time and opportunity.

  • Water gradually shapes hard rock.
  • Grass grows through cracks in concrete.
  • A bare tree becomes green again in spring.
  • Ants carry a fly many times their individual size through teamwork.

In daily life too, a difficult examination, project or skill can become possible when a person works consistently and does not give up.

5. Counterview — “Too Many Cooks Spoil the Broth”

Counterview: Too many people do not necessarily spoil the work. If people are organised, focused and cooperative, teamwork can produce better results. The ants in the lesson are a strong example: many ants work together to carry a fly much larger than one ant. Therefore, the real problem is not the number of people but the absence of coordination.

11. Story Writing — Given Ending

Title: The Empty Garden

Aditi loved visiting her grandmother's house, but one summer she noticed that the neighbourhood had very few trees. The playground was hot and dusty, and birds rarely visited. She asked the elders why there were so few trees. They explained that many old trees had been cut down during construction.

Aditi decided that complaining was not enough. She spoke to her teacher and friends and planned a small tree-plantation drive. They collected suitable saplings, prepared the ground and planted them with the help of adults. Everyone agreed to water and protect the young plants.

As the months passed, the plants grew. Birds began returning and the area became greener. Aditi realised that one small idea could encourage many people to act.

And so, Aditi decided to plant more trees.

Moral: Small, consistent actions can create positive change.

10. Extra Exam-Oriented Questions

Q1. Why is nature called the best teacher?

Nature constantly demonstrates processes such as growth, change, persistence, adaptation and cooperation. By observing these processes, human beings can learn practical lessons for dealing with life.

Q2. How does water teach us persistence?

Water is gentle, but by flowing continuously over hard rock for years it gradually carves and shapes it. It therefore demonstrates the power of consistent effort.

Q3. What is the significance of the tree in spring?

The tree shows that a difficult or apparently lifeless phase need not last forever. Spring brings new leaves, flowers, birds and life, teaching hope and renewal.

Q4. What does the teamwork of ants teach us?

It teaches that organised cooperation and perseverance can enable a group to achieve something that one individual could not accomplish alone.

Q5. Explain “alchemy of nature”.

It refers to the mysterious transformative power visible in nature. A caterpillar becomes a butterfly, a flower becomes fruit, and an oyster transforms a grain of sand into a pearl. These changes also transform the observer's understanding of life.

Q6. Why does the author say “this too shall pass”?

The tree that looks lifeless in winter becomes green again in spring. The example encourages us to believe that present difficulties are temporary and that better circumstances can return.

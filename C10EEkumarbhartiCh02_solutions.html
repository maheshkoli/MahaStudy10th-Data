1. Chapter at a Glance

"The Thief’s Story" by Ruskin Bond is a narrative about a 15-year-old thief who attempts to exploit a trusting man named Anil. The story explores themes of trust, human nature, and the possibility of transformation through kindness and education. Students will learn to analyze character motivations, understand the narrator's internal conflict, and appreciate the power of empathy.

2. Important Concepts Explained

  • The Narrator's Deception: The narrator is a young, experienced thief who constantly changes his name to evade the police and former employers. He uses flattery to gain people's confidence.
  • Anil's Trust: Anil is portrayed as a simple, easy-going, and trusting person. He does not have a steady income, which makes him an unlikely target for a thief, yet his kindness creates a moral dilemma for the narrator.
  • The Turning Point: The narrator works for Anil, learning to cook and write. The realization that education could lead to a better life than stealing begins to change his perspective.
  • The Conflict: The narrator steals Anil's money but finds himself unable to board the train to leave. He realizes that the loss of trust is more significant than the loss of money, leading him to return the stolen cash.

3. Definitions and Key Terms

  • Flattery: Insincere praise given to gain an advantage.
  • Modestly: Without boasting; in a humble way.
  • Grunting: Making low, guttural sounds.
  • Appealing: Attractive or attention-getting.
  • Unlined: Showing no sign of worry or anxiety.
  • By fits and starts: In short, irregular intervals.

6. Think and write in your own words.

(a) Why did Anil employ Hari as a cook although he could not afford to pay him?
Anil was kind and easy-going. He agreed to give Hari food and shelter in exchange for work and decided to teach him cooking and writing. Although he could not promise a salary at first, he offered Hari a chance to learn and improve his life.

(b) What did Hari learn from Anil in the beginning?
Hari learned to cook, write his name, and began learning the skills needed to write whole sentences. Anil also said he would teach him to add numbers.

(c) Why didn’t Anil hand over Hari to the police? What effect would it have on Hari?
Anil appears to understand that Hari has made a mistake and gives him an opportunity to change. Reporting him to the police could have frightened or discouraged him; Anil’s quiet trust encourages Hari to return the money and continue learning.

(d) Was Anil’s way of handling a thief effective? Justify.
Yes, in the story it is effective. Anil treats Hari with kindness and trust instead of reacting harshly. Hari begins to value the chance to become educated and respected, so he returns the stolen money and decides to stay.

(e) What tact did Anil use to change Hari’s dishonest ways?
Anil used patience, trust and education. He gave Hari food and shelter, taught him useful skills and did not openly accuse him after the theft. This gave Hari a reason to reform himself.

(f) Why did the smile come by itself, without effort? When do people give fake smiles?
Hari’s smile was genuine because he felt relieved and hopeful when Anil accepted him and offered to teach him. People may give fake smiles when they want to hide their true feelings, please someone, or gain an advantage.

7. Character sketch of Hari Singh.

Hari Singh is a fifteen-year-old boy who has become an experienced thief. He changes his name regularly to avoid the police and former employers. At first, he is dishonest and uses flattery to win Anil’s confidence. He steals six hundred rupees from Anil, but he is troubled by the thought of losing Anil’s trust and the opportunity to learn. He realizes that education could help him become a clever and respected person. His decision to return the money shows that he has a conscience and the courage to change. Hari is therefore a complex character who moves from dishonesty towards reform.

9. Dialogue: Anil catches Hari stealing the money.

Anil: Hari! What are you doing with the money under my mattress?

Hari: I am sorry, Anil. Please forgive me. I made a terrible mistake.

Anil: I trusted you and gave you a place to stay. Why did you steal from me?

Hari: I was tempted by the money. I promise I will never do it again. Please give me another chance.

Anil: Money can be earned again, but trust is difficult to rebuild.

Hari: I understand. I am truly sorry. I want to learn and become an honest person.

Anil: Then prove it through your actions. We will start your lessons tomorrow.

Hari: Thank you, Anil. I will try my best to deserve your trust.

7. Important Questions and Answers

Why did Hari change his name every month?
He changed his name to stay ahead of the police and his former employers.

Why was it difficult for Hari to rob Anil?
Anil was the most trusting person Hari had met. Hari felt that stealing from Anil would mean losing the trust and opportunity Anil had given him.

Why did Hari hesitate to board the train?
He began to think about Anil’s trust and the chance to learn to read and write. He realized that the education Anil offered might be worth more to his future than the stolen money.

Does Anil realize that he has been robbed?
The story strongly suggests that Anil knows. The fifty-rupee note he gives Hari the next morning is still wet from the rain, and he quietly continues the writing lessons without accusing him.

8. Common Errors and How to Avoid Them

  • Mixing up the characters: Hari is the narrator and the young thief; Anil is the man who gives him food, shelter and education.
  • Missing the turning point: Hari’s decision is influenced by Anil’s trust and the value of learning, not merely by fear of being caught.
  • Overstating the ending: The story implies Anil knows about the theft; he does not explicitly confess or accuse Hari.

1. Poem Overview

“Basketful of Moonlight” is a poem by Sunil Sharma, translated from Dogri. The poem presents the innocent wish of a child who wants moonlight to illuminate the difficult path between his village and the city. The child’s father works in the city and returns home when the child is asleep. The child therefore imagines sowing “seeds of moonlight” along the path so that his father can return early and the child can hear fairy tales and stories from him. fileciteturn172file0L26-L57

Central idea: The poem combines a child’s love for his father with an imaginative wish to solve the practical difficulties of village life. Moonlight becomes a symbol of safety, hope and the child’s desire for family togetherness.

3. Poem — Easy Explanation

The child asks the moon to give him a basketful or two baskets full of moonlight and “seeds of moonlight”. He wants to sow these little moons along the path from the city to his village. The whole village goes to the city daily for work. The village is far away, the return route becomes dark, and the path is difficult because it has snakes and scorpions. There is no regular bus or cart on the route. The child’s father returns home late, after the child has fallen asleep. He leaves again early in the morning while the child is still sleeping. The child therefore asks the moon for moonlight “on loan”. He wants to light the route so that his father can return early. Most importantly, he wants to spend time with his father and hear fairy tales and stories from him.

4. Stanza-wise Explanation

Stanza 1 — The Child’s Imaginative Wish

The child addresses the moon directly and asks for a large quantity of moonlight. He imagines moonlight as something that can be collected in baskets and planted like seeds. This creates a beautiful picture of a child using imagination to solve a real problem.

Stanza 2 — Sowing Light on the Village Path

The child wants to sow many small moons of light along the path from the city to his village. The repeated image of “small, small moons” makes the child’s wish innocent and vivid.

Stanza 3 — Hardships of Village Life

People from the village travel to the city every day for work. Their return journey is difficult because the village is far away and the route becomes dark. The path is full of snakes and scorpions and there is no bus or cart travelling regularly on it. fileciteturn172file0L35-L42

Stanza 4 — The Child Misses His Father

The father reaches home after the child has gone to sleep and leaves again early in the morning while the child is still asleep. Thus the child misses the opportunity to spend time with him and listen to his stories. fileciteturn172file0L42-L52

Stanza 5 — Moonlight as a Solution

The child asks for moonlight “on loan” so that the dark route can be lit and his father can reach home early. The true purpose of the wish is not merely lighting the road: the child wants the companionship of his father and wants to hear fairy tales and stories from him.

3. How Can the Child Solve the Village Problems?

(a) “Give me moonlight, basketful or two baskets full, with seeds of moonlight.”

The child imagines collecting moonlight and sowing it along the path. In his imagination, these seeds would grow into small sources of light and make the dark route safer and easier to travel.

(b) “I want to light the dark route so that my father returns early.”

If the route is illuminated, the father can travel more safely and quickly from the city to the village. His early return would also give the child more time with him.

8. Appreciation of the Poem

Title: The title of the poem is “Basketful of Moonlight”. It comes from the child’s imaginative request to the moon for a basketful or two baskets full of moonlight.

Poet: The poem is written by Sunil Sharma and is translated from Dogri.

Theme: The poem expresses a child’s love for his father, the hardships of village life and the desire to make the difficult journey from city to village safer.

Poetic devices: The poem uses repetition and alliteration to create musicality and emphasis. The image of “seeds of moonlight” is imaginative and turns light into something that can be sown.

Message: Behind the child’s fantastical wish is a simple emotional desire — to have his father return early so that they can spend time together and the child can hear stories from him.

My opinion: I like the poem because it combines a child’s innocent imagination with the real difficulties faced by his family. Its emotional appeal comes from the child’s love for his father.

13. Model Long Answers

Q. Explain the central idea of “Basketful of Moonlight”.

The poem presents the innocent imagination of a child who wants to solve a real problem faced by his village. The village is far from the city, the return path becomes dark and dangerous, and there is no regular bus or cart. The child’s father works in the city and returns when the child is asleep. Therefore, the child asks the moon for a basketful of moonlight and imagines sowing it along the path. His ultimate wish is emotional: he wants his father to return early so that he can hear fairy tales and stories from him. The poem therefore combines imagination, rural hardship and a child’s deep love for his father.

Q. Describe the hardships faced by the villagers in the poem.

The village is quite far from the city, and villagers travel to the city every day for work. Their journey back becomes dark, and the route is tough and full of snakes and scorpions. There is no bus or cart regularly travelling on the route. These difficulties make travel slow and unsafe. The father’s late return also affects family life: the child is asleep when he reaches home and is still asleep when the father leaves in the morning. Thus the poem presents both physical difficulties of travel and the emotional difficulty of missing family time.

Chapter at a Glance

Applied History, also known as Public History, applies historical knowledge for the benefit of people in contemporary and future times. It connects the study of the past with present-day life, research, social planning, heritage preservation and professional work.

The textbook chapter has five sections: What is Applied History?; Applied History and Research in Various Fields; Applied History and Our Present; Management of Cultural and Natural Heritage; and Affiliated Professional Fields.

3.1 What Is Applied History?

Applied History is the field of study concerned with applying history for the benefit of people today and in the future. Public History makes history meaningful in everyday life and connects people with their historical heritage.

Why it matters

  • Historical knowledge can guide solutions to contemporary social issues and support social planning.
  • People can participate in projects to conserve and preserve the heritage of their own city, town or village.
  • Museums and ancient sites can attract visitors and create interest in history.
  • Heritage projects can create opportunities for public participation and employment.

3.2 Applied History and Research in Various Fields

The textbook explains that present lifestyles are shaped by a chain of historical events. Historical methods can support research in many fields because each field has its own history of developing knowledge.

3.3 Applied History and Our Present

Visible and invisible remains of the past exist in the present. They express the creativity and traditions of earlier generations and form part of our heritage.

  • Heritage helps people understand their origins and build identity.
  • It needs to be preserved and conserved for present and future generations.
  • Applied History aims to make heritage accessible to people and helps us understand the present through the past so that we can make better choices for the future.
  • Heritage management can create employment and professional opportunities.

3.4 Management of Cultural and Natural Heritage

Cultural heritage

Cultural heritage is created by human beings and is divided into two types:

  • Tangible cultural heritage: Ancient sites, buildings, artefacts, manuscripts, sculptures and paintings.
  • Intangible cultural heritage: Oral traditions and language, traditional knowledge, social customs and festival rituals, performing arts, traditional skills, and the communities that represent these traditions.

Natural heritage

Natural heritage gives importance to biodiversity and includes fauna, flora, ecology and geomorphic characteristics that sustain the flora and fauna of a region.

World Heritage and examples from the textbook

  • The textbook identifies the Western Ghats as a World Natural Heritage site and notes that the Kaas Plateau in Satara district is part of the Western Ghats.
  • Examples of cultural heritage sites in Maharashtra listed in the chapter include Ajanta Caves, Verul (Ellora) Caves, Elephanta Caves and Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Terminus, Mumbai.
  • The textbook lists Indian intangible heritage traditions such as Kootiyattam, Vedic chanting, Ramlila, Ramman, Kalbelia, Chhau, Mudiyettu, Buddhist chanting of Ladakh, Sankirtana, the Thatheras’ traditional brass and copper utensil-making, and Yoga.

Principles for managing heritage sites

  1. Carry out conservation and development without changing the original state of the site.
  2. Study local social structure, psychology, present challenges and expectations.
  3. Take precautions to avoid hurting the sentiments of local people.
  4. Encourage participation of local people in heritage projects.
  5. Use local skills creatively and create livelihood opportunities.

Organisations mentioned: The Archaeological Survey of India, State Departments of Archaeology and INTACH (Indian National Trust for Art and Cultural Heritage) work in cultural heritage conservation and preservation.

3.5 Affiliated Professional Fields

The textbook lists fields in which historical knowledge can support decisions about legal provisions and public policies:

  1. Museums and archives
  2. Preservation and conservation of historical sites
  3. Tourism and hospitality
  4. Entertainment and mass media

Heritage projects need specialists from many fields, including architects, engineers, historians, archaeologists, museum curators, sociologists, archive-management professionals, legal experts and skilled photographers.

Museums and archives

  • Archives preserve old documents, official records and old films. The National Archives of India is in Delhi.
  • The textbook states that the National Film Archives of India is located in Pune and was established in 1964. Its objectives include collecting and preserving rare Indian films, creating documentation and catalogues, conducting research and promoting film culture.
  • The Indian Museum in Kolkata was founded by the Asiatic Society in 1814. The textbook names Nathaniel Wallich as its founder and first curator.
  • The chapter describes an ancient museum discovered at Ur in Mesopotamia during excavations conducted by Sir Leonard Woolley between 1922 and 1934. It says the museum was established by Princess Ennigaldi.

2. Explain the concepts

(1) Applied History: Applied History, also called Public History, is the application of historical knowledge for the benefit of people in contemporary and future times.

(2) Archives: Archives preserve and store old documents, official records, old films and other records of historical importance.

3. Answer in 25–30 words

(1) Explain Public History.
Public History applies historical knowledge to everyday life. It connects people with their past, makes history meaningful to the public and encourages participation in heritage preservation.

(2) What is UNESCO’s role in heritage sites?
The textbook says UNESCO issues directives to promote cultural and natural heritage. Based on these directives, heritage sites and traditions are declared World Heritage.

(3) Name World Cultural Heritage sites in Maharashtra mentioned in the chapter.
Ajanta Caves, Verul (Ellora) Caves, Elephanta Caves and Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Terminus, Mumbai.

4. Complete the concept chart

Cultural Heritage

  • Tangible: Ancient sites, buildings, artefacts, manuscripts, sculptures and paintings.
  • Intangible: Oral traditions, language, traditional knowledge, customs, festival rituals, performing arts and traditional skills.

5. Explain the statements with reasons

(1) It is essential to study the history of technology.
It helps us understand changes and their causes in agriculture, production, architecture and engineering. It also explains the mutual dependence between scientific discoveries and technological advancement.

(2) The list of World Heritage Sites is announced by UNESCO.
The textbook explains that UNESCO promotes cultural and natural heritage and that sites and traditions are declared World Heritage on the basis of its directives.

6. Elaborate answers

(1)(a) How is historical method useful in science?
It helps establish the chronological order of discoveries, inventions and theories. It also helps explain the needs, curiosity and earlier knowledge that contributed to scientific advances.

(1)(b) How is historical method useful in arts?
Cultural history helps us understand the development of art forms, their styles, the traditions behind them and the emotional and cultural expression of artists.

(1)(c) How is historical method useful in management studies?
Historical knowledge helps explain past production systems, human resources, marketing, market development and the social and economic institutions that support industry and commerce.

(2) How can Applied History be correlated with the present?
Heritage from the past remains visible and invisible in the present. Studying it helps people understand their origins and identity, preserve heritage and use historical knowledge to guide future decisions.

(3) Suggest at least 10 ways to preserve historical sources.

  1. Store documents in suitable, dry conditions.
  2. Protect paper from direct sunlight and moisture.
  3. Use archival-quality folders and boxes.
  4. Digitise documents, photographs and manuscripts.
  5. Maintain suitable temperature and humidity in archives.
  6. Restrict handling of fragile artefacts.
  7. Use trained conservators for repairs.
  8. Keep clear catalogues and inventory records.
  9. Protect heritage sites from vandalism and unauthorised construction.
  10. Educate the public about heritage conservation.
  11. Involve local communities in preservation projects.
  12. Follow conservation plans that preserve the original condition of sites.

(4) What objectives can heritage projects fulfil?
They can conserve and preserve cultural and natural heritage, make it accessible to the public, create awareness, involve local people, use local skills, create livelihood opportunities and support research and tourism.

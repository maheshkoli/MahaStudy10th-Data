Chapter 22: The Height of the Ridiculous

1. Chapter at a Glance

This chapter features the poem "The Height of the Ridiculous" by Oliver Wendell Holmes. It explores the humorous consequences of a poet's attempt to write something funny. The chapter also introduces the concept of "syllables" in language, helping students understand the rhythm and structure of words.

2. Important Concepts Explained

  • Humorous Poetry: The poem depicts a situation where the poet writes lines that are so funny they cause his servant to lose control, leading to a physical reaction (splitting his waistband and bursting buttons).
  • Syllables: A syllable is a unit of spoken language consisting of a single uninterrupted sound formed by a vowel and consonants.
  • Single syllable: ant
  • Two syllables: wa-ter
  • Three syllables: In-fer-no

3. Definitions and Key Terms

  • Ridiculous: Too funny to believe.
  • Wondrous: Delightful.
  • Albeit: Though.
  • Sober: Clear-headed, serious.
  • Trifling: A small, simple joke of no importance.
  • Wretched: In a helpless condition.

4. Formulas, Rules, and Properties

  • Syllable Counting: To count syllables, listen to the number of vowel sounds in a word. Each vowel sound typically represents one syllable.

7. Quick Revision Sheet

  • Syllable: A unit of sound.
  • Humour in Poetry: Often created through exaggeration (hyperbole) and physical comedy.
  • Figures of Speech:
  • Hyperbole: Exaggeration for effect (e.g., "shot from ear to ear").
  • Onomatopoeia: Words that imitate sounds (e.g., "roar").
  • Repetition: Repeating words for emphasis (e.g., "so queer, so very queer").
  • Tautology: Using different words to express the same idea (e.g., "wondrous merry").

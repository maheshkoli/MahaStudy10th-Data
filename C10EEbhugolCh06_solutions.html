1. Chapter at a Glance

Focus of the chapter

The chapter compares the population of India and Brazil. It studies population distribution and density, sex ratio, age-sex pyramids, population growth rate, life expectancy and literacy rate.

Why population matters

The textbook explains that population is an important resource and that the qualitative aspects of a population are important for a country's economic and social progress.

2. Population Distribution and Density

India — textbook figures

According to Census 2011, India had around 121 crore people and an average population density of 382 persons per sq. km. The chapter states that India occupied 2.41% of the world's land area while supporting 17.5% of the world's population.

India — distribution

Population is unevenly distributed. Fertile plains, level land and water availability have supported settlements for centuries. Farming, industries and trade have concentrated population in the northern plains and cities such as Delhi, Kolkata, Mumbai, Pune, Bengaluru and Chennai. Mountainous and hilly regions, dry deserts and dense forests tend to be sparsely populated because of inaccessibility, fewer facilities and difficult living conditions.

Brazil — textbook figures

According to the chapter's 2010 Census figures, Brazil had around 19 crore people and an average density of about 23 persons per sq. km. It occupied 5.6% of the world's land area and had 2.78% of the world's population.

Brazil — distribution

Most Brazilians live within about 300 km of the eastern coast, also called the coastal lowlands. Agriculture and industries have developed there. The interior Amazon Basin is sparsely populated because of unfavourable climate, heavy rainfall, dense forests and accessibility barriers. Central and western Brazil are less populated; the highlands have moderate population density.

Population density formula

Population density = Total population ÷ Total land area. It expresses the average number of people per unit of land area, commonly per square kilometre.

3. Population Composition — Sex Ratio

Definition

Sex ratio means the number of females per 1,000 males in a region.

Brazil

The textbook's Figure 6.3 and explanation state that Brazil's sex ratio has been above 1,000 for decades and that the number of women increased considerably relative to men after 2001.

India

The textbook says that men outnumber women in India and that the sex ratio fluctuated over several decades, with a slight increase after 1991.

Formula

Sex ratio = (Number of females ÷ Number of males) × 1,000.

Graph-reading caution

Figure 6.3 compares trends over time. Use the graph to describe the direction of change; do not invent exact values for years where no readable value is printed.

4. Age-Sex Pyramid

Meaning and use

An age-sex pyramid is a graph showing the age and sex composition of a population. It helps study the proportions of children, young people, working-age people and older people, separately for males and females.

India and Brazil in Figure 6.4

The textbook says Brazil's population is gradually getting older, while India has a larger proportion of young people and therefore a higher working-population potential. It also notes that the proportion of children is higher in India and the proportion of people above 80 years is higher in Brazil.

Economic significance

If the dependent age groups form a larger share of the population, more resources may be needed for children and older people. A larger working-age population can support production and economic activity when education, health and employment opportunities are available.

5. Population Growth Rate

Meaning

Population growth rate describes how quickly a population changes over a period. A falling growth rate does not automatically mean that the total population is falling; it may mean that the population is increasing more slowly than before.

India

The textbook says India's growth rate was higher until 1971 and then stabilised. The rate is now declining, but the population is still growing. It states that India's population increased by 18.2 crore between 2001 and 2011.

Brazil

The textbook describes a considerable reduction in Brazil's population growth rate and suggests that, if the trend continued, Brazil's population might not increase in the next two decades. This is the textbook's projection, not a current population forecast.

Figure 6.5

The graphs on textbook page 42 show a declining population growth rate in Brazil and a declining trend in India's growth rate in the later period. Read the vertical axis as growth rate, not total population.

6. Life Expectancy

Definition

Life expectancy is the average number of years a person born in a country is expected to live.

Trend in the textbook

Figure 6.6 shows increasing life expectancy in both Brazil and India. The chapter's explanation connects this increase with improved medical facilities, progress in medicine, access to nutritious food and socio-economic development.

Values shown in the textbook

The accompanying text says that India’s life expectancy was around 41 years in 1960 and later increased. It describes the values shown for the later period as approximately 68 years for India and 75 years for Brazil. Treat these as textbook-era figures, not current statistics.

7. Literacy Rate

Definition

Literacy rate is the percentage of a population that can read and write. The chapter compares the literacy-rate trends of Brazil and India in Figure 6.7.

Trend in the graph

Both countries' literacy rates increase across the years shown. The plotted values indicate a higher literacy rate in Brazil than India throughout the periods displayed in this textbook graph.

Using the graph

When answering graph questions, name the country, compare the plotted lines and state the trend. Do not confuse a percentage with a population count.

8. Census

India

Brazil

The textbook says Brazil's census is conducted every ten years at the end of the decade, giving 1960, 1970 and similar years as examples. It states that the first census surveys in both countries were carried out in 1872.

11. Textbook Exercise — Q3: Answer the Questions

(1) Explain similarities and differences between population distribution in Brazil and India.

Similarity: both countries have uneven population distribution influenced by physiography and climate. Difference: the chapter gives India's average density as 382 persons per sq. km and Brazil's as about 23 persons per sq. km. India's population is concentrated in plains and major urban/industrial centres, while Brazil's is concentrated near the eastern coast.

(2) Correlate climate and population distribution with examples.

Fertile plains with water availability and accessible land support dense settlement, as in India's northern plains. In Brazil, eastern coastal lowlands support agriculture and industries. Heavy rainfall, dense forests and difficult access in the Amazon Basin, and dry desert or mountainous areas in India, are associated with sparse population.

12. Textbook Exercise — Q4: Give Geographical Reasons

(a) Population is an important resource.

The textbook states that the qualitative aspects of population are important for economic and social progress. People contribute through their knowledge, skills and work.

(b) Brazil's population density is very low.

The textbook gives Brazil a large land area and a population of around 19 crore in its 2010 Census description, resulting in an average density of about 23 persons per sq. km. Large sparsely populated areas, including the Amazon Basin, also contribute to uneven distribution.

(c) India's population density is high.

The textbook says India occupies only 2.41% of the world's land area but supports 17.5% of the world's population. Fertile plains, water availability, agriculture, industries and trade have supported dense settlement in many regions.

(d) Population density is sparse in the Amazon Basin.

The chapter identifies unfavourable climate, heavy rainfall, dense forests and accessibility barriers as obstacles to the development of human settlements.

(e) Population density is high in the Ganga plains.

The chapter explains that fertile land, flat terrain and water availability have supported human settlements for many centuries. Farming, industries and trade have helped concentrate population.

14. Quick Revision Sheet

India (textbook reference)

2011 Census: around 121 crore people; average density 382 persons per sq. km; 2.41% of world land area and 17.5% of world population.

Brazil (textbook reference)

2010 Census: around 19 crore people; average density about 23 persons per sq. km; population concentrated near the eastern coast.

Key definitions

Population density = population per unit area. Sex ratio = females per 1,000 males. Life expectancy = average expected years of life at birth. Literacy rate = percentage of population able to read and write. Age-sex pyramid = graph of population by age and sex.

Graph reminders

Declining growth rate does not necessarily mean declining total population. Increasing life expectancy and literacy rates are shown in the textbook graphs for both countries.

Main factors

Climate, physiography, fertile land, water, access, roads, industries, trade and settlement opportunities influence population distribution.

1. Chapter Overview

“An Epitome of Courage” is a biographical lesson about Dr Stephen Hawking. It presents his childhood, education, diagnosis with ALS, scientific research, physical limitations, books, honours and optimistic attitude towards life.

The lesson focuses especially on courage: not losing heart when faced with a great calamity. Despite being confined to a wheelchair and losing control over most of his body, Hawking continued his research and communicated his ideas with the help of a computer. The textbook presents him as an example of courage and hope. fileciteturn178file0L28-L53

Central message: Difficult circumstances need not prevent a person from using intelligence, determination and hope to achieve meaningful goals.

B. Sumit's Story — Positive Ending

Sumit did not allow polio to destroy his dream of higher education. His parents, teachers and friends encouraged him to continue studying. His Maths teacher helped him find suitable learning support and reminded him that physical difficulty did not reduce his intelligence. Sumit worked hard, completed his studies and continued to excel in mathematics. He eventually pursued higher education and used his knowledge to build a successful career. His story became an inspiration to other students facing difficulties.

C. “Handicapped are those who refuse to take up challenges...”

The statement means that a person should not allow fear, lack of confidence or unwillingness to face challenges to become a permanent limitation. Physical or mental difficulties can certainly create real barriers, but attitude, support and determination can help a person find ways to move forward. The purpose of the statement is to encourage confidence rather than to deny the genuine difficulties faced by people with disabilities.

Ways to encourage confidence

  1. Listen patiently and understand the person's difficulty.
  2. Encourage small, achievable goals instead of demanding immediate success.
  3. Provide practical help without making the person feel helpless.
  4. Appreciate effort and progress.
  5. Connect the person with teachers, mentors, family or professional support when needed.

3. Dr Stephen Hawking — Life and Background

Birth and Early Life

According to the textbook, Stephen Hawking was born in Oxford, London on 8 January 1942, exactly 300 years after the death of Galileo. His parents could not have known that their child would one day be regarded as one of the greatest scientists of his century. fileciteturn178file0L37-L45

Meaning of Courage

The lesson defines courage as the quality that makes people not lose heart when faced with a great calamity. The writer presents Hawking as a personification of courage and hope. fileciteturn178file0L46-L53

Education and Interests

As a child and teenager, Stephen loved music and mathematics. His father wanted him to study medicine, but Stephen wanted mathematics. Since Oxford did not offer a mathematics course at that time, he opted to study physics. fileciteturn178file0L54-L60

Diagnosis at Seventeen

At seventeen, Stephen became increasingly clumsy and fell down without apparent reason. His family doctor diagnosed him with ALS, referred to in the textbook as Lou Gehrig's disease, a rare disease affecting the nervous system and eventually weakening the body's muscles. fileciteturn178file0L61-L67

Continuing Research

The prognosis was bad and doctors said they could not do much. Instead of giving up, Stephen decided to continue his research. He became engaged to Jane Wilde and says that, ironically, he began enjoying life more because he started living in the moment and continued his doctoral research with renewed vigour. fileciteturn178file0L106-L121

Black Holes and Scientific Work

As the disease progressed, Stephen became disabled, was confined to a wheelchair and rapidly lost control over his hands and speech. He studied the concept of Black Holes for his Ph.D. The study sparked his imagination and led to ideas that challenged established theories. The lesson also connects his work with explanations of the Big Bang and mentions “Lie Algebra.” fileciteturn178file0L122-L131

Communication and Honours

Although his physical control became extremely limited, he used a computer to express his thoughts. The textbook says that he became an authority on profound scientific subjects and received numerous honorary doctorates and awards. It also identifies him as a Fellow of the Royal Society and a Member of the US National Academy of Sciences. fileciteturn178file0L132-L146

Books and Optimism

The writer describes Hawking as humble, simple and down-to-earth. His books deal with complex ideas while keeping the layman in mind. His writing is described as lucid, witty and humorous, allowing non-scientists to understand it. The textbook specifically mentions A Brief History of Time as one of the best-selling books of its time. fileciteturn178file0L188-L195

When asked about living with ALS, the lesson quotes Hawking as saying that he tried to lead as normal a life as possible and not regret the things his condition prevented him from doing. The chapter concludes by highlighting his optimism and hope and saluting people who show exemplary courage despite disability. fileciteturn178file0L196-L209

6. Expand the Given Themes

(a) If you fall ill during your final exam

Stay calm and immediately inform the invigilator. Explain the problem clearly and ask what official assistance is available. If necessary, request medical attention through the school or examination authorities. Do not panic about the time lost; concentrate on completing the paper as well as possible after receiving help.

(b) If you meet a disabled person who needs help

First ask politely whether the person wants assistance rather than assuming what they need. Listen to their instructions and help in the way they request. If the situation requires specialised assistance, contact an appropriate responsible person or service. Treat the person with dignity, patience and respect.

(c) If your friend is in trouble

Approach the friend calmly and show through your words and body language that you are ready to listen. Be patient and avoid blaming the person. Ask what help is needed and, if necessary, involve a trusted adult, teacher or family member. Encourage the friend to take one manageable step at a time.

(d) If you meet a great person like Dr Stephen Hawking

Prepare a few thoughtful questions in advance, greet the person respectfully and ask about their work, challenges and achievements. Listen carefully instead of interrupting. Ask how they handled difficulties and what advice they would give students. Finally, thank them for sharing their experience and use the meeting as motivation to plan your own goals.

Scientist: Good morning. Thank you for your kind words.

Student: Did your family and friends support you?

Scientist: I learned from many scientists, teachers, friends and from people who continued working despite difficulties.

9. Model Long Answers

Q. Why is Dr Stephen Hawking called an “Epitome of Courage”?

Dr Stephen Hawking is called an epitome of courage because he continued to live and work with determination despite suffering from ALS and losing control over most of his body. When he was seventeen, he began falling for no apparent reason and was diagnosed with the disease. The prognosis was poor, but he did not give up. He continued his doctoral research, studied black holes and developed important scientific ideas. Even when he was confined to a wheelchair and had very limited physical control, he used a computer to communicate his thoughts. His optimistic attitude and refusal to spend his life regretting his limitations made him an example of courage and hope.

Q. Describe the life and struggles of Dr Stephen Hawking as presented in the chapter.

Stephen Hawking was born on 8 January 1942 and grew up as an average child who loved music and mathematics. He wanted to study mathematics, but chose physics because Oxford did not offer mathematics at that time. At seventeen, he became increasingly clumsy and was diagnosed with ALS. Doctors gave a poor prognosis. Instead of giving up, he continued his doctoral research and became engaged to Jane Wilde. As the disease progressed, he became confined to a wheelchair and lost control over his hands and speech. He studied black holes, developed important scientific ideas and communicated with the help of a computer. He became a Fellow of the Royal Society and a member of the US National Academy of Sciences. He also wrote books such as A Brief History of Time. His life demonstrates determination, intellectual curiosity, humility and optimism.

Q. What message does the life of Stephen Hawking convey?

The lesson conveys that difficult circumstances should not automatically end a person's ambitions. Hawking's physical limitations were severe, yet he continued to learn, research and communicate. His example encourages people to concentrate on their abilities, seek suitable support and continue working towards meaningful goals. The lesson also emphasises optimism, courage and hope. At the same time, the message should not be understood as denying the genuine barriers faced by people with disabilities; rather, it highlights the possibilities that determination and support can create.

Q. What does the writer say about Hawking's books?

The writer says that Hawking authored books about his scientific ideas while keeping ordinary readers in mind. His writing is described as full of wit and humour and sufficiently lucid for non-scientists to understand. The lesson specifically mentions A Brief History of Time as one of the best-selling books of its time. fileciteturn178file0L188-L195

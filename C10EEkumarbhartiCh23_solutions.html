Chapter 23: The Old Man and The Sea : Book Review

1. Chapter at a Glance

This chapter presents a book review of Ernest Hemingway's famous novel, The Old Man and The Sea. It provides students with an understanding of how to write a book review by analyzing the novel's plot, social/historical context, writing style, and the reviewer's personal thoughts. The chapter aims to develop critical reading and analytical skills.

2. Important Concepts Explained

  • Book Review: A book review is a critical evaluation of a book. It typically includes a summary of the plot, the context in which the book was written, an analysis of the author's writing style, and the reviewer's personal opinion.
  • Plot Summary: The novel tells the story of an old, experienced Cuban fisherman who has not caught any fish for eighty-four days. His young apprentice, Manolin, is forced to leave him. The old man resolves to sail further out and catches a massive marlin, which leads to a struggle against the fish and later against sharks.
  • Social/Historical Context: The novel is based on real events and Hemingway's own life experiences, including his time in Cuba, his love for baseball, and his experiences during World War I.
  • Themes: The novel explores themes of struggle against defeat or death, and how determination and endurance can lead to triumph.
  • Writing Style: Hemingway's style is described as simple, pithy, and economical, using short, straight-forward sentences that are deceptively simple, allowing for multiple layers of meaning.

3. Definitions and Key Terms

  • Apprentice: A person who works for another in order to learn a trade.
  • Marlin: Any large, saltwater game fish of the genera Makaira and Tetraptezys having the upper jaw elongated into a spear-like structure.
  • Shrapnel: A hollow projectile containing bullets or the like and a bursting charge, designed to explode before reaching the target and to set the show of missile.
  • Wrought: Worked.
  • Pithy: Brief, full of vigor.
  • Formidable: Causing fear.

4. Formulas, Rules, and Properties

  • Structure of a Book Review:
  1. Introduction: Title, author, and general information.
  2. Plot Summary: A brief overview of the story without revealing the ending.
  3. Context: Social or historical background.
  4. Writing Style: Analysis of the author's language and techniques.
  5. Personal Opinion: The reviewer's thoughts and feelings about the book.

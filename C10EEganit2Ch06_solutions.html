Chapter 6: Trigonometry

1. Chapter at a Glance

This chapter introduces the fundamental concepts of trigonometry, building upon the basic ratios studied in the previous standard. The main topics covered are:

  • Trigonometric Ratios: Defining cosec, sec, and cot ratios.
  • Trigonometric Identities: Fundamental relations between ratios.
  • Angle of Elevation and Angle of Depression: Applications of trigonometry to real-world problems.
  • Problems based on heights and distances: Using trigonometric ratios to solve practical measurement problems.

2. Important Concepts Explained

Trigonometric Ratios

In a right-angled triangle, if $\theta$ is an acute angle:

  • Sine ($\sin \theta$): $\frac{\text{Opposite side}}{\text{Hypotenuse}}$
  • Cosine ($\cos \theta$): $\frac{\text{Adjacent side}}{\text{Hypotenuse}}$
  • Tangent ($\tan \theta$): $\frac{\text{Opposite side}}{\text{Adjacent side}}$

New Ratios:

  • Cosecant ($\text{cosec } \theta$): The multiplicative inverse of sine. $\text{cosec } \theta = \frac{1}{\sin \theta} = \frac{\text{Hypotenuse}}{\text{Opposite side}}$
  • Secant ($\sec \theta$): The multiplicative inverse of cosine. $\sec \theta = \frac{1}{\cos \theta} = \frac{\text{Hypotenuse}}{\text{Adjacent side}}$
  • Cotangent ($\cot \theta$): The multiplicative inverse of tangent. $\cot \theta = \frac{1}{\tan \theta} = \frac{\text{Adjacent side}}{\text{Opposite side}}$

Trigonometric Identities

Fundamental relations derived from Pythagoras theorem ($BC^2 + AB^2 = AC^2$):

  1. $\sin^2 \theta + \cos^2 \theta = 1$
  2. $1 + \cot^2 \theta = \text{cosec}^2 \theta$
  3. $1 + \tan^2 \theta = \sec^2 \theta$

Applications of Trigonometry

  • Line of Vision: The line connecting the observer's eye to the object being viewed.
  • Angle of Elevation: When the observer looks at an object at a level higher than their eye level, the angle formed between the line of vision and the horizontal line is the angle of elevation.
  • Angle of Depression: When the observer looks at an object at a level lower than their eye level, the angle formed between the line of vision and the horizontal line is the angle of depression.

3. Definitions and Key Terms

  • Inscribed Angle: An angle whose vertex lies on the circle and whose arms intersect the circle.
  • Intercepted Arc: The part of the circle that lies in the interior of an angle.
  • Tangent Theorem: A tangent at any point of a circle is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact.

4. Formulas, Rules, and Properties

  • $\sin \theta \times \text{cosec } \theta = 1$
  • $\cos \theta \times \sec \theta = 1$
  • $\tan \theta \times \cot \theta = 1$
  • $\tan \theta = \frac{\sin \theta}{\cos \theta}$
  • $\cot \theta = \frac{\cos \theta}{\sin \theta}$

5. Worked Examples — Step by Step

Ex (1): If $\sin \theta = \frac{20}{29}$, find $\cos \theta$.

Method I (Using Identity):

  1. $\sin^2 \theta + \cos^2 \theta = 1$
  2. $(\frac{20}{29})^2 + \cos^2 \theta = 1$
  3. $\frac{400}{841} + \cos^2 \theta = 1$
  4. $\cos^2 \theta = 1 - \frac{400}{841} = \frac{441}{841}$
  5. $\cos \theta = \sqrt{\frac{441}{841}} = \frac{21}{29}$

Ex (2): If $\sec \theta = \frac{25}{7}$, find $\tan \theta$.

Method I (Using Identity):

  1. $1 + \tan^2 \theta = \sec^2 \theta$
  2. $1 + \tan^2 \theta = (\frac{25}{7})^2 = \frac{625}{49}$
  3. $\tan^2 \theta = \frac{625}{49} - 1 = \frac{576}{49}$
  4. $\tan \theta = \frac{24}{7}$

6. Exercise / Practice Set Solutions

Practice set 6.1

1. If $\sin \theta = \frac{7}{25}$, find $\cos \theta$ and $\tan \theta$.

  • $\sin^2 \theta + \cos^2 \theta = 1 \Rightarrow (\frac{7}{25})^2 + \cos^2 \theta = 1$
  • $\frac{49}{625} + \cos^2 \theta = 1 \Rightarrow \cos^2 \theta = 1 - \frac{49}{625} = \frac{576}{625}$
  • $\cos \theta = \frac{24}{25}$
  • $\tan \theta = \frac{\sin \theta}{\cos \theta} = \frac{7/25}{24/25} = \frac{7}{24}$

2. If $\tan \theta = \frac{3}{4}$, find $\sec \theta$ and $\cos \theta$.

  • $1 + \tan^2 \theta = \sec^2 \theta \Rightarrow 1 + (\frac{3}{4})^2 = \sec^2 \theta$
  • $1 + \frac{9}{16} = \sec^2 \theta \Rightarrow \frac{25}{16} = \sec^2 \theta$
  • $\sec \theta = \frac{5}{4}$
  • $\cos \theta = \frac{1}{\sec \theta} = \frac{4}{5}$

(Note: Solutions for remaining questions follow the same algebraic identities.)

7. Important Questions and Answers

Q: What is the slope of the X-axis?

A: The slope of the X-axis is 0.

Q: Can the slope of the Y-axis be determined?

A: No, the slope of the Y-axis cannot be determined because it involves division by zero.

8. Common Errors and How to Avoid Them

  • Identity Confusion: Students often confuse $\sin^2 \theta + \cos^2 \theta = 1$ with $1 + \tan^2 \theta = \sec^2 \theta$. Always verify the identity before substituting.
  • Reciprocal Errors: Remember that $\sec \theta$ is the reciprocal of $\cos \theta$, not $\sin \theta$.
  • Angle of Depression: Students often draw the angle of depression inside the triangle at the bottom. Remember, the angle of depression is measured from the horizontal line at the top.

9. Quick Revision Sheet

  • Reciprocals: $\text{cosec } \theta = \frac{1}{\sin \theta}$, $\sec \theta = \frac{1}{\cos \theta}$, $\cot \theta = \frac{1}{\tan \theta}$.
  • Identities: $\sin^2 \theta + \cos^2 \theta = 1$, $1 + \tan^2 \theta = \sec^2 \theta$, $1 + \cot^2 \theta = \text{cosec}^2 \theta$.
  • Elevation/Depression: Elevation is upward from horizontal; Depression is downward from horizontal.

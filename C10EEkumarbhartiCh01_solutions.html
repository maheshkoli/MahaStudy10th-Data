1. Chapter at a Glance

This chapter features the poem "Where the Mind is Without Fear..." by Rabindranath Tagore. It is a prayer to the Almighty, written during the time of British rule in India. The poet envisions a nation free from fear, division, and ignorance, where knowledge is accessible to all, and people strive for perfection. 3(A). Answer in your own words

(a) How is the world broken into fragments?
The world is broken into fragments by narrow domestic walls—divisions based on religion, caste, class, colour and nationality. Such divisions separate people and weaken unity.

(b) What should “tireless striving” be for?
It should be directed towards achieving perfection through continuous effort, hard work and the improvement of ourselves and society.

(c) Explain “Where words come out from the depth of truth.”
People should speak honestly and sincerely. Their words should express what they truly believe rather than lies, hypocrisy or deceit.

(d) Who is “Thee”? What does the poet appeal to “Thee” to do?
“Thee” refers to God or the Almighty. The poet asks God to guide his countrymen towards freedom, broad thinking, truth, reason and noble action.

(e) What qualities does the poet wish to develop in his countrymen?
He wishes them to be fearless, dignified, truthful, hardworking, rational, open-minded, united and eager to gain knowledge and achieve perfection.

3(B). Personal responses — sample answers

(a) Is the poem a prayer for India alone?
Although the poet names his own country, the values in the poem—freedom, truth, knowledge, reason and unity—are universal. Therefore, its message can apply to every nation.

(b) What should the words we speak reflect?
Our words should reflect truth, sincerity, kindness and our honest thoughts.

(c) What should people keep widening? How?
People should keep widening their thoughts, knowledge and outlook. They can do so through education, reading, discussion, experience and respect for different viewpoints.

(d) From what darkness should our nation awaken?
Our nation should awaken from fear, ignorance, superstition, injustice, narrow-mindedness and harmful customs.

(e) What attributes of Tagore does the poem reflect?
The poem reflects Tagore’s patriotism, humanism, wisdom, idealism, faith, love of truth and concern for social progress.

(f) What is the effect of repeating “Where” at the beginning of the lines?
The repetition gives the poem a prayer-like rhythm and emphasizes the poet’s hopes for an ideal country. It creates a list of conditions he wishes to see fulfilled.

6. Find examples of metaphor in the poem

  • “The clear stream of reason” — reason is compared to a clear flowing stream, suggesting clarity and freshness of thought.
  • “The dreary desert sand of dead habit” — harmful old customs are compared to dry, barren desert sand that stops the flow of reason.
  • “Narrow domestic walls” — social divisions are described as walls that separate people.
  • “Heaven of freedom” — an ideal state of freedom and enlightenment is described as heaven.

8. Appreciation of the poem (12–15 sentences)

The title of the poem is “Where the Mind is Without Fear...” and the poet is Rabindranath Tagore. The poem is taken from his celebrated work Gitanjali. It is a prayer for an ideal nation and society. The poet wishes people to live without fear and with dignity. He dreams of knowledge being freely available to everyone. He wants people to speak the truth and work tirelessly towards perfection. He opposes divisions created by caste, class, religion and colour. The poem contrasts the clear stream of reason with the dreary desert sand of dead habit. These images are examples of metaphor. The poem uses repetition, especially the word “Where,” to create a prayer-like rhythm. It is written in free verse, without a regular rhyme scheme. Its tone is hopeful, sincere and idealistic. Its central idea is that true freedom includes intellectual, social and moral freedom. My favourite line is “Where the mind is without fear and the head is held high” because it expresses courage and self-respect. I like the poem because it encourages truth, education, reason and unity.

9. Speech: “India of My Dreams”

Respected Principal, teachers and my dear friends, good morning.

My name is __________, and today I am going to speak about “India of My Dreams.”

I dream of an India where every person is free, safe and treated with dignity. In my India, quality education and healthcare will be available to everyone. People will not be judged by caste, religion, language, gender or economic status. Young people will have opportunities to learn skills, find meaningful work and contribute to society. Our cities and villages will be clean, our rivers protected and our environment respected. Citizens will follow the law, reject corruption and speak the truth. Science, innovation and hard work will help the country progress, while compassion will ensure that no one is left behind.

Such an India cannot be built by the government alone. Every citizen must act responsibly, help others and respect different opinions. Students can begin by studying sincerely, keeping their surroundings clean and standing against unfairness.

Let us work together to make India peaceful, educated, healthy and prosperous. This is the India of my dreams.

Thank you. Jai Hind!

7. Important Questions and Answers

Q: Why does the poet compare 'dead habit' to 'dreary desert sand'?

A: The poet uses this metaphor to show that old, irrational customs and rituals are like a desert—barren, dry, and lifeless. They prevent the "stream of reason" (logical thinking) from flowing and progressing.

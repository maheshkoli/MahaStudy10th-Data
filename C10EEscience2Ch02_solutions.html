1. Chapter at a Glance

This chapter explores how living organisms obtain and utilize energy to sustain life. It covers:

  • Living Organisms & Life Processes: The coordination of organ systems.
  • Living Organisms & Energy Production: Cellular respiration (aerobic and anaerobic), glycolysis, the Tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle, and the electron transfer chain.
  • Some Nutrients & Energy Efficiency: The role of carbohydrates, proteins, and lipids in energy production.
  • Cell Division - A Life Process: The mechanisms of mitosis and meiosis.

2. Important Concepts Explained

Living Organisms and Life Processes

  • Organ systems (digestive, respiratory, circulatory, excretory, control) work in coordination to maintain life.
  • These processes require a continuous source of energy.
  • Energy Sources: Carbohydrates, fats, and lipids are the primary sources.
  • Energy Harvesting: Mitochondria in each cell harvest energy from these nutrients.
  • Oxygen: Necessary for efficient energy production.

Cellular Respiration

Respiration occurs at two levels:

  1. Body Level: Exchange of oxygen and carbon dioxide between the body and the environment.
  2. Cellular Level: Oxidation of foodstuffs (with or without oxygen) to produce energy.

Aerobic Respiration (Oxygen is involved)

Glucose is oxidized in three steps:

  1. Glycolysis: Occurs in the cytoplasm. One molecule of glucose is oxidized to form two molecules each of pyruvic acid, ATP, NADH₂, and water.
  2. Tricarboxylic Acid (TCA) Cycle / Krebs Cycle: Pyruvic acid is converted into Acetyl-Coenzyme-A. This enters the mitochondria, where it is completely oxidized to produce CO₂, H₂O, NADH₂, and FADH₂.
  3. Electron Transfer Chain Reaction: NADH₂ and FADH₂ participate in this reaction, which occurs in the mitochondria, producing ATP and water.

Anaerobic Respiration (Oxygen is not involved)

  • Used by organisms like bacteria that cannot live in the presence of oxygen.
  • Glucose is incompletely oxidized.
  • Steps: Glycolysis and Fermentation.
  • Pyruvic acid is converted into organic acids or alcohol.
  • Higher plants, animals, and aerobic microorganisms may perform this if oxygen levels are low (e.g., muscle cells during exercise, leading to lactic acid accumulation and fatigue).

Energy from Different Food Components

  • Proteins: Macromolecules formed by bonding amino acids. They provide 4 Kcal of energy per gram. Excess amino acids are broken down and ammonia is eliminated.
  • Lipids: Formed by specific chemical bonds between fatty acids and alcohol. They provide 9 Kcal of energy per gram. Excess lipids are stored in adipose connective tissue.
  • Vitamins: Heterogeneous compounds essential for proper body operation. A, D, E, K are fat-soluble; B and C are water-soluble.
  • Water and Fibers: Water is essential (65-70% of body weight). Fibers cannot be digested but help in the digestion and egestion of other substances.

Cell Division

  • Essential for growth, restoration of emaciated bodies, and wound healing.
  • Mitosis: Occurs in somatic cells and stem cells. Consists of Karyokinesis (nuclear division) and Cytokinesis (cytoplasmic division).
  • Stages of Karyokinesis: Prophase, Metaphase, Anaphase, Telophase.
  • Meiosis: Occurs in germ cells. Produces four haploid (n) daughter cells from one diploid (2n) cell. Involves crossing over/recombination, making daughter cells genetically different.

3. Definitions and Key Terms

  • Cellular Respiration: The process of oxidizing foodstuffs (glucose) step-by-step in cells to produce energy.
  • ATP (Adenosine Triphosphate): An energy-rich molecule, often called the 'energy currency' of the cell.
  • Fermentation: The process in anaerobic respiration where pyruvic acid is converted into organic acids or alcohol with the help of enzymes.
  • Mitosis: A type of cell division where one parent cell divides into two identical daughter cells.
  • Meiosis: A type of cell division where one diploid cell divides into four genetically different haploid cells.
  • Diploid (2n) Cell: A cell containing two complete sets of chromosomes.
  • Haploid (n) Cell: A cell containing a single set of chromosomes.

4. Formulas and Properties

  • Energy from Carbohydrates: 4 Kcal per gram.
  • Energy from Proteins: 4 Kcal per gram.
  • Energy from Lipids: 9 Kcal per gram.
  • NADH₂: Nicotinamide Adenine dinucleotide.
  • FADH₂: Flavin adenine dinucleotide.

  1. How are the food stuffs and their nutrient contents useful for body?
  • Foodstuffs provide nutrients like carbohydrates, proteins, fats, vitamins, and minerals. These are essential for energy production, growth, repair of tissues, and maintaining body functions.

  • A balanced diet provides all necessary nutrients in the correct proportions, ensuring proper growth, energy, and immunity against diseases.

  • Muscles perform functions like body movement, maintaining posture, circulating blood (heart muscle), and aiding digestion (muscles in the digestive tract).

  • Glucose formula is C₆H₁₂O₆. It contains 6 Carbon atoms, 12 Hydrogen atoms, and 6 Oxygen atoms.

  • Nutrition: The process of taking in food and converting it into energy and other vital components for the body.

3. Distinguish between

  • a. Glycolysis and TCA cycle: Glycolysis occurs in the cytoplasm and breaks glucose into pyruvic acid. The TCA cycle occurs in the mitochondria and completely oxidizes the acetyl part of Acetyl-CoA.
  • b. Mitosis and meiosis: Mitosis occurs in somatic cells for growth and repair, producing two identical daughter cells. Meiosis occurs in germ cells for gamete production, producing four genetically different haploid daughter cells.
  • c. Aerobic and anaerobic respiration: Aerobic respiration requires oxygen and completely oxidizes glucose to CO₂ and H₂O with high energy yield. Anaerobic respiration occurs without oxygen, incompletely oxidizes glucose, and yields less energy.

4. Give scientific reasons.

  • c. Cell division is one of the important properties of cells and organisms: It is essential for growth, reproduction, wound healing, and restoring emaciated bodies.
  • d. Sometimes, higher plants and animals too perform anaerobic respiration: This happens when oxygen levels are depleted in the environment or during intense activity (like exercise in humans), where oxygen supply cannot meet demand.
  • e. Kreb's cycle is also known as citric acid cycle: Because citric acid is the first stable product formed in this cyclic chain of reactions.

7. Important Questions and Answers

  1. What is the role of mitochondria in cellular respiration?
  • Mitochondria are the sites for the TCA cycle and the electron transfer chain, where the majority of ATP is produced during aerobic respiration.
  1. Why is ATP called the 'energy currency' of the cell?
  • ATP stores energy in its phosphate bonds. When the cell needs energy, it breaks these bonds, releasing the stored energy for various cellular activities.
  1. What happens to excess proteins in the body?
  • Excess proteins are broken down, and the ammonia formed is eliminated from the body. They can also be converted into glucose through gluconeogenesis if necessary.

8. Common Errors and How to Avoid Them

  • Confusing Aerobic and Anaerobic: Remember that aerobic requires oxygen and produces more energy, while anaerobic does not and produces less energy.
  • Confusing Mitosis and Meiosis: Remember that Mitosis = Somatic cells (growth/repair, 2 identical cells); Meiosis = Germ cells (reproduction, 4 different cells).
  • Misinterpreting "Oxidation": In cellular respiration, oxidation of glucose does not just mean adding oxygen; it is a complex step-by-step process involving electron transfer.

9. Quick Revision Sheet

  • Energy Yield: Carbohydrates (4 Kcal/g), Proteins (4 Kcal/g), Lipids (9 Kcal/g).
  • Aerobic Respiration Steps: Glycolysis → TCA Cycle → Electron Transfer Chain.
  • Mitosis Stages: Prophase → Metaphase → Anaphase → Telophase.
  • Meiosis: Two stages (Meiosis-I and Meiosis-II), results in 4 haploid cells.

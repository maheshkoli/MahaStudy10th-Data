1. Chapter at a Glance

This chapter introduces carbon as a versatile element that forms the basis of organic chemistry. Students will learn about the unique bonding properties of carbon (catenation and tetravalency), the classification of carbon compounds into hydrocarbons (saturated and unsaturated), functional groups, homologous series, and IUPAC nomenclature. The chapter also covers the chemical properties of carbon compounds (combustion, oxidation, addition, and substitution reactions) and introduces macromolecules and polymers.

2. Important Concepts Explained

Bonds in Carbon Compounds

  • Carbon compounds generally have low melting and boiling points (below 300 °C), indicating weak intermolecular forces.
  • They are generally bad conductors of electricity, meaning they do not produce ions.
  • Carbon has 4 electrons in its valence shell. To attain a stable noble gas configuration (like Neon), it shares these 4 electrons with other atoms, forming covalent bonds.
  • A covalent bond is formed by the sharing of two valence electrons between two atoms.
  • Electron-dot structure: A representation using dots or crosses to show valence electrons and shared pairs.
  • Structural formula: A representation using lines to show the bonds between atoms.

Carbon: A Versatile Element

  • Catenation power: Carbon has a unique ability to form strong, stable covalent bonds with other carbon atoms, resulting in long chains (straight or branched) or ring structures.
  • Tetravalency: Carbon can form bonds with four other atoms (carbon or other elements like H, O, N, S, halogens).
  • Isomerism: Compounds having the same molecular formula but different structural formulae are called isomers. This phenomenon is called structural isomerism.

Hydrocarbons: Saturated and Unsaturated

  • Hydrocarbons: Compounds containing only carbon and hydrogen.
  • Saturated hydrocarbons (Alkanes): Contain only single bonds between carbon atoms. General formula: $C_nH_{2n+2}$.
  • Unsaturated hydrocarbons: Contain at least one double or triple bond between carbon atoms.
  • Alkenes: Contain a carbon-carbon double bond. General formula: $C_nH_{2n}$.
  • Alkynes: Contain a carbon-carbon triple bond. General formula: $C_nH_{2n-2}$.

Functional Groups

  • A hetero atom (like Cl, O, N, S) or a group of atoms that substitutes a hydrogen atom in a hydrocarbon chain and gives the compound specific chemical properties is called a functional group.

Homologous Series

  • A series of compounds formed by joining the same functional group in place of a particular hydrogen atom on chains of sequentially increasing length.
  • Characteristics:
  • Each member differs by one methylene unit ($-CH_2-$).
  • Molecular mass increases by 14 u.
  • Chemical properties are similar.

3. Definitions and Key Terms

  • Covalent Bond: A chemical bond formed by the sharing of two valence electrons between two atoms.
  • Catenation: The unique ability of carbon to form strong covalent bonds with other carbon atoms to form long chains or rings.
  • Hydrocarbons: Simplest organic compounds containing only carbon and hydrogen.
  • Isomerism: The phenomenon where compounds have the same molecular formula but different structural formulae.
  • Functional Group: A hetero atom or group of atoms that determines the chemical properties of a carbon compound.
  • Homologous Series: A series of compounds with the same functional group and similar chemical properties, where each successive member differs by a $-CH_2-$ unit.
  • Macromolecules: Giant molecules formed from hundreds of thousands of atoms.
  • Polymers: Macromolecules formed by the regular repetition of a small unit called a monomer.

4. Formulas, Rules, and Properties

  • Alkane general formula: $C_nH_{2n+2}$
  • Alkene general formula: $C_nH_{2n}$
  • Alkyne general formula: $C_nH_{2n-2}$
  • IUPAC naming structure: Prefix - Parent - Suffix

Page 121: Use your brain power!

  1. Atomic number of chlorine is 17. What is the number of electron in the valence shell of chlorine?
  • Electronic configuration of Cl (atomic number 17) is 2, 8, 7.
  • Answer: There are 7 electrons in the valence shell.
  1. Molecular formula of chlorine is $Cl_2$. Draw electron-dot and line structure of a chlorine molecule.
  • Each Cl atom has 7 valence electrons. They share 1 electron each to complete their octet.
  • Electron-dot: Each Cl atom is surrounded by 6 non-bonding electrons and 2 shared electrons (1 pair).
  • Line structure: $Cl-Cl$
  1. Molecular formula of water is $H_2O$. Draw electron-dot and line structures for this triatomic molecule.
  • Oxygen (2, 6) needs 2 electrons. Hydrogen (1) needs 1 electron. Oxygen shares 1 electron with each of the two Hydrogen atoms.
  • Electron-dot: O is in the center, sharing 1 pair with each H.
  • Line structure: $H-O-H$
  1. Molecular formula of ammonia is $NH_3$. Draw electron-dot and line structures for ammonia molecule.
  • Nitrogen (2, 5) needs 3 electrons. Each Hydrogen needs 1. Nitrogen shares 1 electron with each of the three Hydrogen atoms.
  • Electron-dot: N is in the center, sharing 1 pair with each of the 3 H atoms.
  • Line structure: N with three bonds to H atoms.

7. Important Questions and Answers

  • Q: Why are carbon compounds generally bad conductors of electricity?
  • A: Because they are covalently bonded and do not form ions in solution.
  • Q: What is the difference between saturated and unsaturated hydrocarbons?
  • A: Saturated hydrocarbons have only single bonds between carbon atoms, while unsaturated hydrocarbons have at least one double or triple bond.
  • Q: Define structural isomerism with an example.
  • A: Compounds with the same molecular formula but different structural arrangements. Example: n-butane and isobutane ($C_4H_{10}$).

8. Common Errors and How to Avoid Them

  • Error: Forgetting to satisfy the tetravalency of carbon (4 bonds) when drawing structural formulae.
  • Correction: Always count the bonds around each carbon atom; it must be 4.
  • Error: Confusing the general formulas for alkanes, alkenes, and alkynes.
  • Correction: Remember: Alkanes ($+2$), Alkenes (no change), Alkynes ($-2$).
  • Error: Incorrect IUPAC numbering.
  • Correction: Always number the chain to give the functional group the lowest possible number.

9. Quick Revision Sheet

  • Carbon Valency: 4 (Tetravalent).
  • Bonding: Covalent (sharing electrons).
  • Alkanes: $C_nH_{2n+2}$ (Single bonds).
  • Alkenes: $C_nH_{2n}$ (Double bonds).
  • Alkynes: $C_nH_{2n-2}$ (Triple bonds).
  • Functional Groups: $-OH$ (Alcohol), $-CHO$ (Aldehyde), $-COOH$ (Carboxylic acid), $-CO-$ (Ketone).
  • Homologous Series: Successive members differ by $-CH_2-$.
  • Combustion: Carbon compounds + $O_2 \rightarrow CO_2 + H_2O + \text{heat}$.
  • Oxidation: Ethanol $\xrightarrow{KMnO_4}$ Ethanoic acid.
  • Esterification: Acid + Alcohol $\rightarrow$ Ester + Water.

1. Chapter at a Glance

This chapter explores the concept of "Social Health," which extends beyond physical and mental well-being to include an individual's ability to establish healthy relationships with others. It covers factors that influence social health, the impact of modern technology, the dangers of addiction, and strategies for stress management.

2. Important Concepts Explained

  • Definition of Social Health: It is the ability of a person to establish relationships with other persons. Key characteristics include a strong personality, having a large number of friends and relatives, proper use of time during loneliness, and trust, respect, and acceptance for others.
  • Factors Affecting Social Health: These include education, financial status, social and physical conditions of the surrounding, residential area, water, toilets, political conditions, play grounds, social treatment, social safety, transport facilities, and job opportunities.
  • Factors Disturbing Social Health:
  • Mental Stress: Caused by competition for education/employment, nuclear families, and gender inequality (e.g., discrimination against girls).
  • Addiction: Peer-group pressure often leads adolescents to try tobacco, cigarettes, gutkha, alcohol, or drugs, which can permanently damage the nervous system, heart, and muscles.
  • Incurable Diseases: Ignorance towards people with AIDS, T.B., leprosy, or mental disorders harms social health.
  • Communication Media and Modern Technology: While useful, excessive or irrational use leads to physical problems (tiredness, headache, insomnia, vision issues) and social isolation. It can also lead to cybercrimes (hacking, fake accounts, piracy).
  • Stress Management: Techniques include laughter clubs, hobbies (photography, reading, cooking, dancing), music, sports, and yoga/meditation.

3. Definitions and Key Terms

  • Social Health: The ability of a person to establish relationships with other persons.
  • Addiction: A state where an individual becomes dependent on harmful substances like tobacco, alcohol, or drugs due to peer pressure or imitation.
  • Cyber Crime: Illegal activities performed using the internet, such as hacking, spreading derogatory messages, or financial exploitation.
  • Selfiecide: A term used for the dangerous practice of taking selfies without being aware of the surroundings and risks.

4. Formulas, Rules, and Properties

  • IT Act-2000: An act enacted on 17th October 2000 and amended in 2008 to curb cybercrimes. It includes punishments like imprisonment for 3 years and fines up to 5 lakh.

2. Answer the following

a. Which factors affect social health?

Factors include basic needs such as food, clothing, shelter and medicines; education; financial status; education and job opportunities; transport facilities; social safety and treatment; gardens and playgrounds; the social and physical conditions of the surroundings; residential area; water and toilets; and political conditions or views.

b. Which changes occur in persons continuously using the internet and mobile phones?

Excessive use may be associated with tiredness, headache, insomnia, forgetfulness, tinnitus, joint pain and vision problems. People may become solitary and self-centred and may find it harder to maintain harmonious relationships or respond sensitively to others.

c. Which problems does the common person face due to cybercrime?

People may face financial fraud, theft or misuse of personal information, hacking, fake social-media accounts, online harassment, misleading online sales, unauthorised bank transactions, derogatory messages and piracy of digital content.

d. Explain the importance of good communication with others.

Good communication allows people to share feelings, seek advice and support, build trust and maintain healthy relationships. Talking with family, friends, peers and teachers can help relieve stress and reduce loneliness.

4. What are the various ways to minimize stress?

  • Communicate with friends, relatives, teachers and parents; express feelings instead of keeping them inside.
  • Take up hobbies such as reading, photography, cooking, drawing, dancing or gardening.
  • Listen to music or sing.
  • Play outdoor games and exercise regularly.
  • Practise yoga, breathing exercises and meditation.
  • Manage time, plan duties and seek counselling or medical help if stress persists.

6. What will you do? Why?

a. You are spending more time on internet/mobile games, phone, etc.

I would set daily screen-time limits, switch off unnecessary notifications, keep the phone away during study and sleep, and spend more time on outdoor activities and face-to-face conversations. This helps protect my health, concentration and relationships.

b. A child in your neighbourhood is addicted to tobacco chewing.

I would speak kindly to the child and encourage them to seek help from a trusted parent, teacher or health professional. I would explain the harms of tobacco without shaming them and encourage tobacco-cessation support.

c. Your sister has become incommunicative and prefers to remain alone.

I would approach her gently, listen without judging, invite her to join family activities and ask if anything is troubling her. If the isolation continues or she seems distressed, I would encourage her to talk with a trusted adult or counsellor.

d. You have free space around your home.

I would use it for a small garden, native plants, a sitting area or a safe play space. This can improve the surroundings and encourage outdoor activity and interaction with neighbours.

e. Your friend has developed the hobby of snapping selfies.

I would remind my friend to take photos only in safe places, never while crossing roads or near water, heights or traffic, and never to risk their life for a picture. I would encourage safer hobbies and responsible use of social media.

f. Your brother studying in Class XII has developed stress.

I would listen to him, help him make a realistic study timetable, encourage breaks, sleep, exercise and hobbies, and reassure him that marks do not define his worth. If stress becomes severe or persistent, I would encourage professional counselling.

7. What changes occur in a home with a chronically ill older person? How will you help maintain a good atmosphere?

The family may experience worry, emotional strain, changes in daily routines and additional care responsibilities. I would share responsibilities, treat the older person with respect, help with daily needs, keep the home calm and clean, encourage family members to communicate, and arrange medical care and respite support when needed.

Project: Social health in your residential area

Observe your area and list factors such as cleanliness, safe drinking water, toilets, gardens, playgrounds, transport, safety, education, employment opportunities and access to healthcare. Identify two or three problems, discuss practical solutions with residents, and help implement an achievable change—for example, a cleanliness drive, a community garden or a safe-play initiative.

7. Important Questions and Answers

Q1. Why is gender inequality a factor disturbing social health?

Ans: Gender inequality creates unnecessary stress for girls due to discrimination in domestic duties, education, and food choices. It also leads to problems like teasing and molestation, which negatively impact their social and mental health.

Q2. How does the "Laughter Club" help in stress management?

Ans: Laughter clubs provide a platform for people to laugh loudly together, which helps in releasing mental stress and neutralizing negative thoughts.

Chapter 6: Statistics

2. Important Concepts Explained

Measures of Central Tendency

Numerical data collected in a survey often clusters around a particular score. This representative score is called the measure of central tendency. We study three main measures:

  1. Mean ($\bar{X}$): The average of the given data.
  2. Median: The middle value of the data when arranged in ascending order.
  3. Mode: The score that repeats the maximum number of times.

Mean from Classified Frequency Distribution

When data is large, it is grouped into classes. We use three methods to find the mean:

  1. Direct Method: $\bar{X} = \frac{\sum f_i x_i}{N}$, where $x_i$ is the class mark, $f_i$ is the frequency, and $N = \sum f_i$.
  2. Assumed Mean Method: $\bar{X} = A + \bar{d}$, where $A$ is the assumed mean and $\bar{d} = \frac{\sum f_i d_i}{N}$ is the mean of deviations ($d_i = x_i - A$).
  3. Step Deviation Method: $\bar{X} = A + (\bar{u} \times g)$, where $u_i = \frac{d_i}{g}$ and $g$ is the G.C.D. of all deviations.

Median for Grouped Frequency Distribution

For grouped data, the median is calculated using the formula:

$\text{Median} = L + \left[ \frac{\frac{N}{2} - cf}{f} \right] \times h$

  • $L$: Lower class limit of the median class.
  • $N$: Sum of frequencies.
  • $h$: Class interval of the median class.
  • $f$: Frequency of the median class.
  • $cf$: Cumulative frequency of the class preceding the median class.

Mode for Grouped Frequency Distribution

The mode is estimated using the formula:

$\text{Mode} = L + \left[ \frac{f_1 - f_0}{2f_1 - f_0 - f_2} \right] \times h$

  • $L$: Lower class limit of the modal class.
  • $f_1$: Frequency of the modal class.
  • $f_0$: Frequency of the class preceding the modal class.
  • $f_2$: Frequency of the class succeeding the modal class.
  • $h$: Class interval of the modal class.

3. Definitions and Key Terms

  • Class Mark ($x_i$): The midpoint of a class interval, calculated as $\frac{\text{Lower limit} + \text{Upper limit}}{2}$.
  • Frequency ($f_i$): The number of observations falling in a specific class.
  • Cumulative Frequency ($cf$): The sum of frequencies up to a certain class.
  • Median Class: The class interval that contains the $\frac{N}{2}^{th}$ observation.
  • Modal Class: The class interval with the highest frequency.
  • Pie Diagram: A circular chart where data is represented by proportional sectors of a circle.

---

4. Formulas, Rules, and Properties

  • Mean (Direct): $\bar{X} = \frac{\sum f_i x_i}{\sum f_i}$
  • Mean (Assumed Mean): $\bar{X} = A + \frac{\sum f_i d_i}{\sum f_i}$
  • Mean (Step Deviation): $\bar{X} = A + \left( \frac{\sum f_i u_i}{\sum f_i} \right) \times g$
  • Median: $\text{Median} = L + \left[ \frac{\frac{N}{2} - cf}{f} \right] \times h$
  • Mode: $\text{Mode} = L + \left[ \frac{f_1 - f_0}{2f_1 - f_0 - f_2} \right] \times h$
  • Central Angle ($\theta$): $\theta = \frac{\text{Number of scores in component}}{\text{Total number of scores}} \times 360^\circ$

5. Worked Examples — Step by Step

(Note: Due to the extensive nature of the textbook, key examples are summarized here.)

Example: Direct Method (Mean)

Given: Frequency distribution of workers' time.

Steps:

  1. Calculate class marks ($x_i$) for each class.
  2. Multiply $x_i$ by frequency ($f_i$) to get $f_i x_i$.
  3. Sum all $f_i x_i$ and all $f_i$.
  4. Divide $\sum f_i x_i$ by $\sum f_i$.

Result: The mean is calculated as $\frac{\sum f_i x_i}{N}$.

7. Important Questions and Answers

Q: When should we use the Step Deviation method?

A: When the class intervals are uniform and the deviations ($d_i$) have a common factor ($g$), this method simplifies calculations by reducing the size of the numbers.

Q: What is the purpose of a krink mark on a graph?

A: It indicates that there are no observations between the origin and the first class, allowing for a more compact and readable graph.

8. Common Errors and How to Avoid Them

  • Class Continuity: Always check if classes are continuous (e.g., 0-10, 10-20). If not (e.g., 0-9, 10-19), adjust them by subtracting 0.5 from the lower limit and adding 0.5 to the upper limit.
  • Class Mark Calculation: Ensure the class mark is the midpoint of the continuous class interval.
  • Formula Application: In the median and mode formulas, ensure $L$ and $h$ correspond specifically to the median class or modal class, respectively.

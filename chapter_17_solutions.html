1. Chapter Overview

“The World is Mine” is a poem by Joy Lovelet Crawford. The speaker initially compares herself with other people and notices what she thinks she lacks. Three encounters then change her perspective: a young girl on a bus, a lad selling sweets, and a blue-eyed child who is unable to hear. The speaker finally recognises the value of her own legs, eyes and ears and concludes that she is blessed. The existing solution file identifies gratitude, contentment, empathy and appreciation of one's abilities as the major learning objectives. fileciteturn186file1L21-L37

“O God forgive me when I whine
I have two legs, the world is mine”
— Joy Lovelet Crawford

The poem's repeated refrain changes the speaker's attitude from complaint to gratitude. The final stanza gathers the three gifts—legs, eyes and ears—and turns them into a celebration of life. fileciteturn186file0L46-L69

3. Poem — Stanza-wise Explanation

Stanza 1 — The Girl on the Bus

The speaker sees a lovely girl with silken hair and envies her because she appears happy and beautiful. When the girl gets up to leave, however, the speaker notices that she has to hobble down the aisle. This makes the speaker realise that she should not have complained about her own life.

Realisation: The speaker has two healthy legs and therefore has something valuable that she had taken for granted.

Stanza 2 — The Lad Selling Sweets

The speaker stops to buy sweets from a pleasant and calm lad. They talk, and the lad thanks the speaker for being kind. Only then does he reveal that he is blind. The speaker again realises the value of her own ability to see.

Realisation: Having eyes to see the world is a blessing.

Stanza 3 — The Blue-eyed Child

The speaker sees a blue-eyed child watching other children play. She invites him to join them, but he looks ahead without responding. The speaker then understands that he cannot hear. This makes her appreciate her own ears.

Realisation: Hearing is another gift that is easily taken for granted.

Final Stanza — “The World is Mine”

The speaker counts the abilities she possesses: legs to take her where she wants to go, eyes to see the sunset, and ears to hear. Instead of focusing on what she lacks, she recognises that she is blessed.

Central message: Gratitude changes our perspective. We should appreciate the abilities and blessings we already possess instead of constantly complaining about what we do not have.

The three encounters and the concluding gifts of legs, eyes and ears are directly reflected in the poem text. fileciteturn186file0L46-L69

5. Textual Questions and Answers

Q1. Describe the young girl in the bus.

The young girl had silken hair and appeared lovely and fair. The speaker initially envied her. When the girl rose to leave, the speaker saw that she had difficulty walking and had to hobble down the aisle.

Q2. What did the speaker in the poem not realize while chatting with the young lad?

The speaker did not realise that the lad was blind while they were chatting. He revealed his blindness when he said that it was nice to talk to people like the speaker because he could not see.

Q3. Why did the blue-eyed boy not join the others in their game?

He did not join the other children because he could not hear. The speaker understood this after asking him to join the others and receiving no response.

Q4. What does the speaker thank God for?

The speaker thanks God for having legs to walk, eyes to see the beauty of the world and ears to hear. She realises that these abilities are blessings.

3. Meaning and Importance of the Title “The World is Mine”

The phrase “The World is Mine” does not mean ownership of the world. It expresses the speaker's feeling of gratitude after recognising the gifts she possesses. She has legs to move, eyes to see and ears to hear. By appreciating these abilities, she feels that the world is open to her and full of possibilities. The title therefore captures the poem's movement from complaint to gratitude.

4(a). What is the Theme of the Poem?

The main theme is gratitude and appreciation of life's blessings. The poem teaches us not to remain unhappy about what we lack. Instead, we should recognise our own abilities, respect people who face different challenges and value the gifts we already have.

4(b). Should People with Some Handicap Beg for Money?

No. A physical or sensory disability does not mean that a person has no abilities or dignity. People with disabilities should receive equal opportunities, education, accessibility and suitable support so that they can use their strengths and live independently as far as possible. Society should help remove barriers rather than assume that disability means dependence.

4(c). Which is Better — Take Over or Encourage?

Option (ii) — Encourage the person to do the work independently in a different way. Appropriate encouragement promotes confidence, self-respect and independence. Help should be provided when needed, but it should not unnecessarily take away a person's opportunity to use their own skills.

8. Dialogue — Three Classmates with Different Disabilities

Student 1: Do you know how many problems I face when I decide to go anywhere? Walking long distances and using places without proper accessibility is difficult for me.

Student 2: I understand. I can walk, but I cannot hear people clearly. Following conversations and announcements can be difficult.

Student 3: Both of you can experience some things that I cannot. I cannot see the beautiful world around me, so moving through unfamiliar places is challenging.

Student 1: We each face different problems. But we also have different strengths.

Student 2: Yes. Society should make places accessible and give everyone equal opportunities.

Student 3: And we should support one another instead of feeling sorry for one another.

Student 1: True. Friendship and understanding can make difficult situations easier.

9. Story — A Polio Victim and a Visually Challenged Friend

Title: The Strength of Friendship

A polio victim and a strong but visually challenged man were close friends. They wished to visit a place of worship three kilometres away. At first, their journey seemed difficult because each friend had a different difficulty.

They decided to use their strengths together. The visually challenged man could walk strongly and steadily, while his friend could help him understand the path and guide him around obstacles. They supported each other and moved slowly but confidently.

When they reached a difficult stretch of road, they rested, planned their next steps and continued. After some time they reached the place of worship safely. Both realised that their limitations did not have to stop them when they worked together.

Moral: “Where there is a will, there is a way.”

7. Extra Exam-Oriented Questions

Q1. Why does the speaker repeatedly say “O God, forgive me when I whine”?

The refrain expresses the speaker's regret. Each encounter makes her realise that she had been complaining despite possessing valuable abilities. She asks God to forgive her for failing to appreciate those blessings.

Q2. How do the three encounters change the speaker?

The girl makes her appreciate her legs, the blind lad makes her appreciate her eyes, and the hearing-impaired child makes her appreciate her ears. Together, the encounters change her from someone who compares and complains into someone who recognises her blessings.

Q3. Explain the importance of gratitude in the poem.

Gratitude helps the speaker focus on what she has rather than what she lacks. It changes her emotional perspective and allows her to experience the beauty and opportunities present in her own life.

Q4. What message does the poem give about people with disabilities?

The poem encourages readers to recognise that people may face different physical or sensory challenges, but they remain individuals with dignity, abilities and value. It also encourages others to become more empathetic and to appreciate their own abilities.

Q5. Explain the title in 3–4 sentences.

“The World is Mine” expresses the speaker's renewed appreciation of life. After observing people who face different challenges, she recognises the abilities she possesses. Her legs, eyes and ears allow her to move, see and hear the world. Thus, the title expresses gratitude rather than possession.

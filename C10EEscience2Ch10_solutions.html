1. Chapter at a Glance

This chapter introduces the concept of disasters, their classification, and the importance of disaster management. It covers the effects of disasters on society, the nature and scope of disaster management, the structure of disaster management authorities in India, and the importance of first aid and mock drills in preparing for emergencies.

2. Important Concepts Explained

Disaster

A disaster is a sudden event that leads to huge loss of life and property. It can be natural (e.g., floods, cyclones, earthquakes) or man-made (e.g., war, bomb blasts, industrial accidents). Disasters cause sudden changes in the environment and disrupt normal life.

Classification of Disasters

Disasters are classified based on various criteria:

  • Geophysical: Earthquakes, volcanoes, tsunamis, landslides, erosion, flooding.
  • Biological: Plant diseases (forest fires, fungal diseases), animal diseases (infectious viruses, bacteria, insects).
  • Man-made: War, fire, bomb blasts, forced migration, terrorism, industrial accidents, poisonous gas leaks.

Effects of Disaster

Disasters have long-lasting effects on:

  • Economy: Huge expenses for reconstruction and loss of productivity.
  • Social Leadership: Confusion and lack of participation in rescue efforts.
  • Administration: Collapse of local governing bodies and departments.
  • Medical: Injuries, mental stress, epidemics, and death.

Disaster Management Cycle

Disaster management involves scientific observation and analysis to minimize losses. The cycle includes:

  1. Preparation: Planning to minimize destruction.
  2. Redemption: Planning to minimize damage to society.
  3. Preparedness: Chalking out plans for quick response.
  4. Response: Immediate action after the disaster strikes.
  5. Impact of Disaster: Reviewing the intensity of the disaster.
  6. Restoration: Linking measures after the disaster to national development.
  7. Resurgence: Linking emergency measures to national progress.

Structure of Disaster Management Authority

In India, the Disaster Management Act, 2005, establishes a hierarchy for control and coordination:

  • National: National Disaster Management Authority (Chairman: Prime Minister)
  • State: State Disaster Management Authority (Chairman: Chief Minister)
  • District: District Disaster Management Authority (Chairman: Collector)
  • Taluka: Taluka Disaster Management Authority (Chairman: Tahsildar)
  • Village: Village level Committee (Chairman: Sarpanch)

3. Definitions and Key Terms

  • Disaster: A sudden event causing huge loss of life and property.
  • First Aid: Primary help offered to victims of a disaster before actual medical treatment.
  • Mock Drill: A practice exercise to check the preparedness and reaction time for facing a disaster.

4. First Aid and Emergency Action

  • First Aid Kit: Essential for immediate help. It should contain items like bandage strips, wound gauze, antiseptic (Dettol/Sarlon), safety pins, blade, medicated cotton, hand gloves, soap, etc.
  • Transportation Methods: Methods like the cradle method, carrying on the back, or carrying on two hands are used depending on the victim's condition.

2. Write notes

a. Disaster Management Authority: The textbook describes authorities from national to village level to coordinate disaster-management work. The National Authority is chaired by the Prime Minister, the State Authority by the Chief Minister, the District Authority by the Collector, the Taluka Authority by the Tahsildar and the village-level committee by the Sarpanch.

b. Nature of disaster management: Disaster management means improving the ability to face disasters through scientific observation and analysis, planning preventive measures, and carrying out rehabilitation and reconstruction plans. The textbook identifies emergency, transitional/rehabilitation and reconstruction phases as important for citizens.

c. Mock drill: A mock drill is a practice exercise that creates a simulated disaster situation to test preparedness and reaction time. It checks whether responsibilities and plans are understood, improves coordination, and identifies errors and risks before a real emergency.

d. Disaster Management Act, 2005: The textbook states that the Act provides for disaster-management authorities and coordination in India, from national to village level.

3. Answer the following questions

a. Explain the role of the district disaster control unit after a disaster.
The district control unit is established immediately after a disaster or after receiving information about it. It reviews the situation, maintains contact with agencies such as the army, air force, navy, telecommunications department and paramilitary forces to obtain help, and coordinates with voluntary organizations.

b. Give reasons for the increase in human disasters after World War II.
According to the chapter, factors include increasing population and needs, economic inequality, racial and religious differences, terrorism, abduction and social conflict. It also mentions the production and use of harmful chemicals and dangerous technologies.

c. What are the objectives of disaster management?

  1. Rescue and release people affected by the calamity.
  2. Supply essential commodities to reduce the effects of the disaster.
  3. Restore normal life in the affected region.
  4. Rehabilitate disaster victims.
  5. Consider protective measures to reduce the likelihood or intensity of future disasters.

d. Why is first-aid training essential?
It helps people give immediate primary assistance before medical treatment arrives. Suitable first aid can save lives, relieve pain and prevent the victim’s condition from worsening.

e. Which methods are used to transport patients? Why?
The textbook mentions the cradle method, carrying on the back and carrying in both arms. The method depends on the victim’s condition. In real emergencies, do not move a person with a possible head, neck or spinal injury unless there is immediate danger; call emergency services and follow trained responders’ advice.

5. Reasons, effects and remedies for any two disasters

Example 1: Flood

  • Reasons: Heavy or prolonged rainfall, overflowing rivers and poor drainage.
  • Effects: Loss of life and property, damaged roads, contaminated water and crop loss.
  • Remedies: Follow warnings, move to higher ground, avoid floodwater, use safe drinking water and support relief work.

Example 2: Earthquake

  • Reasons: Sudden movement of rocks along faults in the Earth’s crust.
  • Effects: Building collapse, cracks in land, injuries and damage to roads and services.
  • Remedies: Secure heavy objects, practise “Drop, Cover and Hold On,” stay away from damaged buildings and follow official instructions.

Students may choose two disasters they have actually experienced in their area.

6. Which aspects of disaster management would you check for your school? Why?

  • Clear evacuation routes and safe assembly points.
  • Working alarms, fire extinguishers and emergency lighting.
  • Accessible first-aid kits and emergency contact numbers.
  • Regular mock drills and clear responsibilities for staff and students.
  • Safe buildings, stairs, laboratories, electrical systems and exits.
  • Assistance for students with mobility or other support needs.

These checks help reduce confusion and prevent avoidable injuries during emergencies.

9. Explain why these statements are true

A. “Mock drill is useful.”
A mock drill tests preparedness, response time and coordination. It identifies errors and risks so that the plan can be improved before an actual disaster.

B. “Effective disaster management makes us well prepared for the future.”
Disaster management includes identifying hazards, planning, training and public awareness. These activities can reduce losses, improve response and help people recover.

11. Pre- and post-disaster management for the pictures

a. Gas cylinder / gas leak:

  • Before: Store and handle cylinders correctly, check equipment for leaks and know emergency contacts.
  • After: Leave the area, avoid flames and electrical switches if a flammable gas leak is suspected, warn others from a safe distance and contact emergency services. Do not re-enter until authorities say it is safe.

b. Crowd crush / stampede:

  • Before: Keep exits clear, manage crowd capacity and flow, and provide trained stewards.
  • After: Follow emergency staff, move away from crowd pressure if possible, do not push and call for medical help.

c. Flood:

  • Before: Monitor warnings, prepare an emergency kit and plan routes to higher ground.
  • After: Avoid floodwater and damaged electrical systems, use safe water and return only when authorities permit.

d. Landslide:

  • Before: Watch for cracks, slope movement and official warnings; avoid unstable slopes.
  • After: Move away from the slide path, avoid the affected area because further slides may occur, report missing people and wait for rescue teams.

1. Chapter Overview

“Bholi” by K. A. Abbas tells the story of Sulekha, a girl who is called “Bholi the simpleton” because of her childhood difficulties, pockmarks and stammering. She is neglected and mocked, but her life changes after she enters school and receives patient encouragement from her teacher. Years later, when Bishamber Nath demands dowry before marrying her, Bholi finds the courage to reject him. The story highlights education, dignity, self-respect and resistance to social discrimination and dowry.

The existing solution notes identify social stigma, education, teacher support, Bholi's character development, discrimination and the dowry system as the main learning areas. fileciteturn187file1L21-L35

3. Story in Simple Sequence

1. Bholi's Childhood

Bholi's real name is Sulekha. She is Ramlal's fourth daughter and the youngest of his seven children. At ten months she falls from a cot and may suffer brain damage. At two, she has smallpox, which leaves deep pockmarks on her body. She begins speaking late and then stammers. Other children mock her, so she speaks very little.

The opening section gives these details about Sulekha/Bholi, her family and her difficulties. fileciteturn187file0L50-L80

2. The Decision to Send Her to School

When a primary school for girls opens in the village, the Tehsildar tells Ramlal that, as a government representative, he should set an example and send his daughter to school. Ramlal's wife initially opposes the idea because she fears that education may affect a girl's marriage prospects. She finally suggests sending Bholi because she thinks Bholi has little chance of marriage.

The Tehsildar's instruction and the family's discussion are described in Part I. fileciteturn187file0L110-L126

3. Bholi's First Day

Bholi is frightened and resists going to school. Ramlal and his wife give her a clean dress, bathe her and oil her hair. At school she sees other girls, pictures on the walls and a kind teacher. When asked her name, she can only stammer. She becomes ashamed and cries.

4. The Teacher Changes Her Life

The teacher speaks gently, does not laugh at Bholi and encourages her to try again. Bholi finally says her name. The teacher gives her a picture book and promises that with regular schooling she will learn to read and become educated. Bholi begins to feel hope and a new sense of life.

The teacher's gentle encouragement and Bholi's first successful attempt to speak are shown on pages 133–135. fileciteturn187file0L166-L202 fileciteturn187file0L228-L259

5. Bishamber's Marriage Proposal

Years pass. The village develops into a small town and the primary school becomes a high school. Ramlal considers Bishamber Nath's proposal. Bishamber is well-to-do and has a shop and property, but he is older, limps and has children from his first wife. Bholi's parents nevertheless see the proposal as a good opportunity because he appears financially secure and is initially not asking for dowry.

The proposal and the family's reasons for accepting it are given in Part II. fileciteturn187file0L273-L302

6. The Dowry Demand

At the wedding, Bishamber notices Bholi's pockmarks and demands five thousand rupees from Ramlal before marrying her. Ramlal begs him to accept two thousand, but Bishamber refuses. Ramlal finally pays five thousand rupees because he fears humiliation in the village.

The wedding scene and the demand for five thousand rupees appear on page 136. fileciteturn187file0L318-L356

7. Bholi's Courageous Decision

Bholi looks directly at Bishamber with cold contempt. Before he can place the garland around her neck, she throws it into the fire and speaks in a clear, loud voice without a stammer. She asks her father to take back his money and refuses to marry Bishamber, calling his behaviour mean, greedy and contemptible.

She then declares that she will serve her parents in their old age and teach at the same school that educated her. The teacher watches with deep satisfaction, comparing the completed transformation to an artist's masterpiece.

Bholi's rejection of the marriage and her final decision are described on pages 137–138. fileciteturn187file0L368-L405 fileciteturn187file0L449-L461

5. Textbook “Think and Discuss” — Model Answers

Q1. What does Bholi's appearance tell us about her childhood?

Bholi was physically affected by smallpox and had deep pockmarks. She also stammered and had been mocked by other children. These experiences made her withdrawn and fearful.

Q2. Why did Ramlal's wife oppose sending Bholi to school?

She feared that sending girls to school would make marriage difficult. She was especially worried about Bholi because of her appearance and speech difficulty.

Q3. What shows that Bholi was unwilling to go to school?

When Ramlal tried to take her to school, Bholi became frightened, pulled her hand away and repeatedly cried “No.” She did not know what school was like and remembered the recent experience of their cow Lakshmi being taken away.

Q4. How did Bholi's parents change her appearance before school?

They gave her a clean dress that had belonged to her sister, bathed her and rubbed oil into her dry, matted hair. This was unusual because her old clothes were normally passed down without much care.

Q5. What made the classroom attractive to Bholi?

She saw many girls of nearly her own age and hoped that one might become her friend. The colourful pictures on the classroom wall also fascinated her.

Q6. Why did Bholi feel ashamed?

She felt ashamed because she could not say her name properly due to her stammering and feared that the other girls would laugh at her.

Q7. How would you describe the teacher's attitude?

The teacher was kind, patient, encouraging and affectionate. She did not laugh at Bholi, encouraged her to speak, praised her effort and gave her hope through education.

Q8. Which words could Bholi utter whole?

She finally managed to say “Bholi” and later answered “Yes” aloud when the teacher asked whether she would come to school every day.

Q9. How did the teacher encourage Bholi to overcome her stammering?

She spoke softly, did not laugh at Bholi, asked her to make an effort, praised her attempts, told her to remove fear from her heart and assured her that regular schooling would help her speak like everyone else.

Q10. What were the drawbacks of Bishamber as a bridegroom?

He was much older, approximately the same age as Ramlal, he limped and he had grown-up children from his first wife. Later, his behaviour at the wedding also revealed greed because he demanded dowry.

Q11. Why were Bholi's parents eager to accept Bishamber's proposal?

Bishamber was financially well-to-do, owned a big shop and house and was believed to have money in the bank. He initially did not ask for dowry, and Bholi's parents feared that she might otherwise remain unmarried.

Q12. Why did Bishamber refuse to garland Bholi?

After seeing the pockmarks on Bholi's face, Bishamber demanded five thousand rupees from her father before he would marry her.

Q13. What shocked the family members?

They were shocked when Bholi suddenly spoke in a clear, loud voice without stammering, rejected Bishamber and refused to marry him.

Q14. Besides humiliation and grief, what was Ramlal afraid of?

He was afraid of losing the family's social honour (“izzat”) and being disgraced in the village after the wedding broke down.

Q15. What was the expression on Bholi's face?

Her eyes showed cold contempt. She was no longer frightened or downcast; she looked directly at Bishamber with confidence.

Q16. Name the artist and the masterpiece.

The teacher is compared to the artist, and Bholi's transformation is the masterpiece. The teacher's satisfaction comes from seeing the confident person Bholi has become.

Two Paragraphs on the Transformation

Before meeting the teacher: Bholi was timid and withdrawn. She had pockmarks and a stammer, and other children mocked her. Her family also paid little attention to her. She was afraid to speak because she expected people to laugh at her.

After meeting the teacher: The teacher treated Bholi with kindness and patience. She encouraged her to speak, praised her attempts and gave her books and hope. Education gradually made Bholi confident and self-respecting. Years later, this confidence enabled her to reject an unfair marriage arrangement and choose her own path.

6. Expansion of the Proverb — “Where there is a will, there is a way.”

“Where there is a will, there is a way” means that a determined person can find a method to overcome difficulties.

Life does not always provide easy opportunities. A person may face poverty, illness, failure, disability or opposition. But strong determination can help a person search for another path.

Bholi is an excellent example from the story. At first she is frightened and cannot speak confidently. Her teacher encourages her to continue her education. With patience and effort, Bholi develops confidence.

At the wedding, she faces a difficult situation when Bishamber demands dowry. Instead of surrendering to pressure, she speaks clearly and refuses to marry him.

Her decision shows that courage and determination can change a person's life. Therefore, difficulties may create obstacles, but a strong will can help us find a way forward.

Conclusion: We should not give up when we face problems. Determination, effort and courage can help us find solutions.

The textbook asks for an approximately 12–15 line expansion and provides explanation, examples/experiences and conclusion as the steps. fileciteturn187file0L504-L509

11. Extra Exam-Oriented Questions

Q1. Describe Bholi's character.

At first Bholi is timid, neglected and afraid of ridicule. Education and the teacher's affection gradually develop her confidence. In the end she becomes courageous, self-respecting and capable of making an independent moral decision.

Q2. What role does education play in Bholi's life?

Education gives Bholi knowledge, confidence and a voice. The teacher's support helps her overcome fear and communicate. Ultimately, education gives her the confidence to reject an exploitative marriage and choose a meaningful future.

Q3. Why is the teacher compared with an artist?

The teacher patiently develops Bholi's abilities and confidence. The change from a frightened, stammering child to a confident young woman is described as a masterpiece, just as an artist feels satisfaction after completing a masterpiece.

Q4. What social evils are highlighted in “Bholi”?

The story highlights gender discrimination, discrimination against people with disabilities or differences, judging people by appearance, social pressure surrounding marriage and the dowry system.

Q5. Why is the ending important?

The ending shows the full effect of the teacher's work. Bholi is no longer the frightened girl who could barely speak. She confidently rejects an unfair marriage and decides to support her parents and teach in the school that changed her life.

1. Chapter at a Glance

“Science and Spirituality” describes how Dr. A. P. J. Abdul Kalam believed that science and faith can coexist for the good of humanity. Science provides focus to solve questions, discover truth and develop inventions; faith provides perspective about the effects of discoveries on society.

Part I describes Kalam’s family and friendships across religious communities. Part II recounts how Professor Vikram Sarabhai and Dr. Kalam approached Reverend Peter Bernard Pereira to resolve the difficulty of acquiring the Thumba site for a space research centre. Part III discusses the influence of Professor Satish Dhawan and the example of Sri Sri Shivakumara Mahaswamiji, leading to the question “What can I give?”

The answers below are based on the supplied textbook pages 89–99. Open-ended questions and speeches contain sample responses; adapt them in your own words.

3. Difference between science and technology

Science studies the natural world through observation and experiments to understand how things work. Technology applies scientific knowledge to create tools, machines and systems that solve practical problems.

4. Are science and faith both important?

Yes. Science helps us understand the world and develop useful inventions. Faith and spirituality can help people reflect on values, responsibility and the effects of their actions. When used responsibly, both can contribute to human welfare.

6. Answer in your own words

(a) What makes Dr. Kalam a humble personality?
He learned simplicity and humility from his father. He respected people of different faiths, listened to wise people and believed that knowledge should be used to serve society.

(b) How were different virtues inculcated in Dr. Kalam?
His father taught him simplicity and spirituality. His friends and religious leaders showed the value of dialogue and service. Scientists such as Sarabhai and Dhawan influenced his thinking, while spiritual leaders taught him ethical living and generosity.

(c) How did Reverend Pereira compare his work with Dr. Sarabhai’s?
Pereira explained that science improves life through technology and medicine, while a preacher prays for people’s well-being and peace. He said that both were working for the prosperity of humanity.

(d) What was remarkable about Mahaswamiji?
At 102, he stood without support, appeared steady and alert, gave a wise discourse and then helped feed thousands of children. He had also established a free residential education system for more than nine thousand children.

(e) Why replace “What can I take?” with “What can I give?”
The attitude of taking without concern can lead to environmental destruction, corruption, injustice and damaged relationships. Giving through education, food, service and compassion helps create a fairer society and a healthier planet.

7. Short paragraphs on the personalities

(a) Reverend Peter Bernard Pereira: He was the bishop of the region around Thumba. He was thoughtful and compassionate, and helped resolve the site problem by explaining the value of the scientific mission to the church community. Alternative accommodation was offered to those affected.

(b) Dr. Sri Sri Shivakumara Mahaswamiji: He dedicated his life to serving humanity and established a free residential education system for more than nine thousand children. Even at 102, he remained active and alert. His life illustrates generosity, discipline and service.

(c) Dr. A. P. J. Abdul Kalam: Dr. Kalam was a scientist and the former President of India. The chapter presents him as humble, curious, spiritual and committed to using knowledge for social transformation. He encouraged people to ask what they can give to others.

8. Explain the expressions

  1. “Faith and fact can, together, create a better planet.” Scientific knowledge and spiritual or ethical values can complement one another and help humanity make responsible choices.
  2. “Dr Kalam’s own life was nourished by multiple faiths.” He learned from people of different religions and respected their shared values.
  3. “There was a major roadblock.” An important obstacle made it difficult to acquire the Thumba site because fishermen and religious institutions were already there.
  4. “Only when the dust is removed, does the mirror shine…” Like a dusty mirror, a person’s abilities and inner goodness become clear when ethical impurities are removed through pure living and service.
  5. “Through his words of wisdom, I was getting to learn from countless great minds.” Dr. Kalam’s advice reflected the wisdom of many teachers and thinkers who influenced him.
  6. “He gathered the sands of conscience…” He drew together lessons from wise people and used conscience as a guide for good conduct.
  7. “What can I take?” is responsible for wrong around us. Selfishness and taking without giving back can cause exploitation, corruption, inequity and environmental harm.

5. Grammar and Punctuation

The textbook’s simple-sentence pattern is: Subject + Verb + Object / Complement / Adverbial.

  • Verb + object: A doctor treats patients.
  • Verb + complement: They are very tired.
  • Verb + adverbial: He walks slowly.

6. Writing Activities

14(A). School assembly speech: True happiness lies in giving and sharing

Respected Principal, teachers and my dear friends, good morning.

Today I would like to speak about the idea that true happiness lies more in giving and sharing than in receiving and taking.

When we share our knowledge, help a classmate, donate books or care for someone in need, we make another person’s life better. These actions also give us a sense of purpose. Taking everything for ourselves may bring temporary pleasure, but generosity builds trust, friendship and a peaceful community.

Giving does not always mean giving money. We can give our time, attention, kindness, skills and encouragement. We can share our books, help younger students understand a lesson, keep our surroundings clean and respect people who are different from us.

Dr. A. P. J. Abdul Kalam encouraged people to ask, “What can I give?” Let us follow this message in our daily lives. Even a small act of kindness can make a difference.

Let us choose generosity over selfishness and service over indifference. Thank you.

14(B). Debate: Science and spirituality can go hand in hand

Points supporting the motion:

  • Science helps us discover facts and develop technology; spirituality encourages responsibility and compassion.
  • Ethical values can guide how scientific discoveries are used.
  • The Thumba example in the lesson shows scientists and a religious leader cooperating for a shared purpose.

Points opposing the motion:

  • Scientific claims require evidence and testing, while spiritual beliefs may be based on faith.
  • Conflicts can occur when beliefs are used to reject reliable evidence.
  • Scientific decisions should be guided by evidence, safety and public welfare.

Conclusion: Science and spirituality use different approaches, but they can coexist when scientific claims are tested with evidence and personal beliefs encourage ethical, humane conduct without obstructing inquiry.

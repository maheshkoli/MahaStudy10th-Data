Chapter 4: Financial Planning

1. Chapter at a Glance

This chapter introduces the Goods and Services Tax (GST) system in India, replacing various previous taxes. Students will learn about:

  • GST Introduction: Understanding CGST, SGST, and IGST.
  • Tax Invoice: Reading and interpreting tax invoices for goods and services.
  • GST Computation and ITC: Calculating GST, understanding Input Tax Credit (ITC), and the trading chain.
  • Shares, Mutual Funds, and SIP: Basic concepts of investing, including Face Value (FV), Market Value (MV), dividends, and Systematic Investment Plans (SIP).

---

2. Important Concepts Explained

Goods and Services Tax (GST)

  • One Nation, One Tax, One Market: GST subsumes various taxes like Excise Duty, Custom Duty, VAT, Entertainment tax, Central Sales Tax, Service Tax, and Octroi.
  • Components of GST:
  • CGST (Central Goods and Services Tax): Tax paid to the Central Government.
  • SGST (State Goods and Services Tax): Tax paid to the State Government.
  • IGST (Integrated Goods and Services Tax): Tax levied by the Central Government for inter-state trading.
  • GSTIN (Goods and Services Tax Identification Number): A 15-alpha-numeral identification number for dealers. It includes the 10-digit PAN of the dealer.
  • HSN Code (Harmonized System of Nomenclature): A numerical code used to classify goods.
  • SAC (Service Accounting Code): A special code number used to classify services.

Input Tax Credit (ITC)

  • Input Tax: GST paid by a trader at the time of purchase.
  • Output Tax: GST collected by a trader at the time of sale.
  • GST Payable: Output Tax - ITC.
  • Trading Chain:
  • B2B (Business to Business): Trading between GSTIN holders.
  • B2C (Business to Consumer): Trading between a GSTIN holder and a consumer.

Investments

  • Shares: Small equal parts of a company's capital.
  • Face Value (FV): The value printed on the share certificate.
  • Market Value (MV): The price at which shares are sold or purchased in the stock market.
  • Dividend: The part of annual profit distributed per share among shareholders. It is always calculated on the Face Value.
  • Mutual Fund (MF): A professionally managed investment scheme run by an Asset Management Company (AMC).
  • NAV (Net Asset Value): The market value of one unit of a mutual fund.
  • SIP (Systematic Investment Plan): A method of investing small amounts at regular intervals.

---

3. Definitions and Key Terms

  • Capital: The amount required to start a company.
  • Share: The smallest unit of capital.
  • Share Holder: A person who owns shares and is a part-owner of the company.
  • Stock Exchange: A place where buying and selling of shares take place (e.g., BSE, NSE).
  • Brokerage: A charge paid to share brokers for their services in buying/selling shares. It is calculated on the Market Value.
  • Dividend: Annual profit distributed per share.

---

4. Formulas, Rules, and Properties

  • GST Payable: Output Tax - ITC
  • Sum Invested: Number of shares × MV
  • Rate of Return (RoR): (Dividend Income / Sum Invested) × 100
  • NAV of one unit: Total Fund Value / Number of units
  • Total Fund Value: NAV of one unit × Number of units
  • Buying Price (with brokerage): MV + Brokerage
  • Selling Price (with brokerage): MV - Brokerage

5. Worked Examples — Step by Step

Ex. (1) Arati Gas Agency (Page 95)

Given: Taxable value = ₹ 545, GST = 5%.

To find: Amount of CGST, SGST, total amount paid by consumer, and total GST paid by the agency.

  1. Rate of CGST/SGST: Since GST = 5%, CGST = 2.5% and SGST = 2.5%.
  2. CGST Amount: (2.5 / 100) × 545 = 13.625 ≈ ₹ 13.63.
  3. SGST Amount: Same as CGST = ₹ 13.63.
  4. Total Amount Paid: Taxable value + CGST + SGST = 545 + 13.63 + 13.63 = ₹ 572.26.
  5. Total GST paid by Agency: 13.63 × 2 = ₹ 27.26.

Ex. (1) Brokerage (Page 112)

Given: FV = ₹ 100, MV = ₹ 150, Brokerage = 0.5%, 100 shares.

Situation (I) Buying:

  1. Brokerage per share: 0.5% of 150 = (0.5 / 100) × 150 = 0.75.
  2. Buying price per share: 150 + 0.75 = ₹ 150.75.
  3. Total cost for 100 shares: 100 × 150.75 = ₹ 15,075.

Situation (II) Selling:

  1. Selling price per share: 150 - 0.75 = ₹ 149.25.
  2. Total received for 100 shares: 100 × 149.25 = ₹ 14,925.

7. Important Questions and Answers

  • Q: What is the difference between Face Value and Market Value?
  • A: Face Value is the value printed on the share certificate. Market Value is the current price at which the share is traded in the stock market.
  • Q: Why is GST called an indirect tax?
  • A: GST is called an indirect tax because it is levied on goods and services, and the burden is ultimately passed on to the final consumer, even though it is collected at various stages of the supply chain.

8. Common Errors and How to Avoid Them

  • Error: Calculating dividend on Market Value.
  • Correction: Always calculate the dividend on the Face Value (FV) of the share.
  • Error: Forgetting to add brokerage to the buying price or subtract it from the selling price.
  • Correction: Brokerage is an additional cost when buying and a deduction when selling. Always adjust the MV accordingly.

9. Quick Revision Sheet

  • GST Components: CGST + SGST = GST.
  • GST Payable: Output Tax - ITC.
  • Sum Invested: Number of shares × MV.
  • Dividend: Calculated on FV.
  • Brokerage: Calculated on MV.
  • NAV: Total Fund Value / Number of units.

1. Poem at a Glance

Poet: George Herbert (1593–1633), a Welsh poet and priest.

In “The Pulley”, God gives human beings many blessings, including strength, beauty, wisdom, honour and pleasure. God decides to withhold “Rest”, meaning complete contentment. The poem imagines that if people were entirely satisfied with worldly gifts, they might adore the gifts rather than their Creator. Restlessness and weariness may therefore turn people back towards God.

The pulley is a metaphor in the poem: restlessness is imagined as the means that lifts human beings towards God. The poem presents a religious idea through a mechanical image.

2. Write dramatic monologues for the situations

(a) It’s your birthday next week.

My birthday is only a week away! I wonder how I will celebrate it this year. I hope my friends can come, and I am already thinking about the cake. I should make a list of the things I want to do.

(b) You have been waiting a long time at the city bus-stop.

Where is the bus? I have been waiting for so long. If it does not arrive soon, I will be late for class. Perhaps I should check whether another bus goes to my destination.

(c) Your final examination results will be declared tomorrow.

Tomorrow is the result day. I have worked hard, but I am still nervous. I hope my efforts bring good marks. Whatever the result, I must stay calm and learn from the experience.

(d) Your mother has been talking on the telephone for a long time.

Mum has been talking on the phone for ages! I need to ask her something about my schoolwork. I will wait a little longer, and then politely ask whether she has a moment for me.

3. Where can a pulley be used, and for what purpose?

  • Wells: to draw up a bucket of water.
  • Construction sites: to lift heavy materials.
  • Flagpoles: to raise and lower a flag.
  • Elevators and lifts: as part of a lifting mechanism.
  • Gyms: to guide cables in exercise equipment.

3. Discuss and answer in your own words

(a) The poet has used the word “REST” thrice. What does it imply in each use?

  • “Rest in the bottom lay”: the remaining or unspent blessing at the bottom of the glass; it also hints at the gift called Rest.
  • “And rest in Nature, not the God of Nature”: contentment or peace found in the created world rather than in God.
  • “Yet let him keep the rest”: the other blessings that God has already given to humankind.

(b) What does God want in return for the gifts given to man?
In the poem, God wants human beings to remember and turn towards Him, rather than becoming so satisfied with the gifts that they forget the Giver.

(c) Why did God withhold the gift of “Rest”?
God believes that complete contentment might cause people to adore the gifts of Nature instead of the God of Nature. He therefore leaves people with some restlessness, which may lead them back to Him.

(d) What other gifts has God blessed mankind with, besides those in the poem?
Possible answers: love, compassion, patience, creativity, courage, kindness, imagination and the ability to learn.

(e) When does man generally turn to God? Give an example.
People may turn to God when they face difficulties, fear or uncertainty. For example, a person facing a serious illness may pray for strength and comfort.

4(A). Justify the title “The Pulley”

The title is appropriate because the pulley is the central metaphor of the poem. A physical pulley lifts an object; in the poem, human restlessness and weariness act like a pulley that draws people towards God. The title expresses the poem’s main idea in a simple but memorable image.

5(B). Explain the figures of speech

(a) “Rest in the bottom lay” — Pun: The word “rest” suggests both what remains at the bottom of the glass and the blessing of rest or contentment that God withholds.

(b) “Bestow this jewel also on my creature” — Metaphor: “Jewel” directly compares the gift of Rest/contentment to a precious object, without using “like” or “as.”

(c) “And rest in Nature, not the God of Nature” — Repetition: The word “Nature” is repeated to contrast the created world with its Creator.

6. Critical appreciation of the poem

Title: “The Pulley”
Poet: George Herbert
Rhyme scheme: The poem is arranged in five-line stanzas; the textbook’s answer choices identify the scheme as a-b-c-c-b.
Theme: The poem explores the relationship between God and human beings. God gives people strength, beauty, wisdom, honour and pleasure, but withholds complete rest or contentment so that they may remember the Creator. The central metaphor is the pulley, which represents restlessness drawing people towards God. The poem uses imagery, metaphor, pun, paradox, alliteration and inversion to communicate its religious idea. Its message is that worldly blessings alone may not provide spiritual fulfilment, and difficulties or dissatisfaction may encourage people to seek a deeper connection with God.

7. Create your own pun and a humorous sentence

Sample pun: “The calendar’s days are numbered.” The phrase means both that the calendar displays numbers and that its remaining days are limited.

Another example: “I used to be a banker, but I lost interest.” Here “interest” means both curiosity and money earned by saving or lending.

8. Summary of the four stanzas

StanzaSummary
IGod creates man and decides to pour the world’s riches and blessings upon him, gathering them together as if into a small space.
IIGod gives man strength, beauty, wisdom, honour and pleasure. When almost all the blessings have been given, God notices that Rest remains.
IIIGod thinks that if man receives complete rest or contentment, he may adore the gifts instead of the Giver and find satisfaction in Nature rather than in God.
IVGod allows man to keep the other blessings but leaves him with restlessness and weariness. These may eventually draw man back towards God, like a pulley lifting him towards Him.

5. Central Message and Interpretation

  • The poem uses a mechanical device as an image for a religious relationship.
  • “Rest” has layered meanings: the remaining blessing in the glass, physical repose, and spiritual or emotional contentment.
  • God’s decision is presented in the poem as a way to ensure that people do not forget their Creator while enjoying worldly gifts.
  • The poem’s interpretation belongs to its religious speaker and should be understood as the poem’s perspective.

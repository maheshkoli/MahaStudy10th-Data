9.1 Sources of History, Conservation and Preservation

Historical sources and history books are the result of research by historians. Libraries, museums and archives conserve and preserve historical heritage. They select important specimens for exhibition, publish research journals and informative material, and store important documents and artefacts that are not on public display. These stored materials may be made available to researchers when needed.

Collecting historical sources, creating records and indexes, exhibiting manuscripts and artefacts, and carrying out conservation work require careful handling and management. The textbook emphasises that these tasks should be done by suitably qualified and trained people.

Three types of historical sources

Source typeExamples / tasks in the textbookTraining mentioned
Oral sourcesCollecting and compiling folk songs and folk tales; classifying, analysing and interpreting the material; publishing research results.Sociology, anthropology, social psychology, mythology, linguistics, library science, information technology, history, historical research methods, and research-report writing.

Why training matters: Historical documents and objects may be damaged by careless handling or incorrect cleaning. Knowledge of the source material, conservation techniques and historical context helps professionals preserve them responsibly.

9.2 Some Famous Museums

Museums organise and manage antiquities and other collections. They exhibit selected objects and preserve other materials for research.

MuseumDetails given in the textbook
Louvre Museum, FranceLocated in Paris and established in the 18th century C.E. The collection includes Leonardo da Vinci’s celebrated painting Mona Lisa. The textbook notes that the collection grew greatly after antiquities were brought back by Napoleon Bonaparte from his conquests.
British Museum, EnglandEstablished in the 18th century C.E. The textbook says Sir Hans Sloan handed over about 71,000 objects from his collection to King George II; the collection later expanded, and the book gives a total of about 80 lakh objects.

Museology: The textbook notes that some universities and institutions offer degree and diploma courses in museology, the field associated with museums and their collections.

9.3 Libraries and Archives

Libraries

Libraries are storehouses of knowledge and information. Their tasks include collecting books, arranging them systematically, conserving and preserving them, and making information available to readers. Computerised systems and information technology help manage collections and retrieve the material a reader needs.

The textbook mentions the Royal Library of Ashurbanipal in Mesopotamia (7th century B.C.E.), the library at Takshashila University, and the library at Alexandria in Egypt among early libraries. It also describes the Saraswati Mahal Granthalay in Thanjavur, whose expansion was supported by Vyankojiraje Bhosale and his successors, especially Sarfojiraje Bhosale. In 1918, it was renamed the Thanjavur Maharaja Sarfoji’s Saraswati Mahal Library.

Other libraries mentioned include the National Library, Kolkata; Nehru Memorial Museum and Library, Delhi; State Central Library, Hyderabad; Library of the Asiatic Society, Mumbai; and David Sassoon Library, Mumbai.

Archives

Archives preserve important documents in their original condition, prepare indexes and provide reliable systems for retrieving records. The textbook stresses that computerised systems and information technology have become important in archives management.

The first official archives of India, the Imperial Record Department, was established in Kolkata in 1891 C.E. and moved to Delhi in 1911 C.E. The textbook states that the archives were opened to the public in 1998 by President K. R. Narayanan. Records preserved there are arranged chronologically from 1748 and include English, Arabic, Hindi, Persian, Sanskrit, Urdu and Modi-script material.

The Maharashtra State Archives has branches in Mumbai, Pune, Kolhapur, Aurangabad and Nagpur. The Pune branch holds about five crore Modi documents relating to Maratha history, referred to as the Peshwa Daftar.

9.4 Encyclopaedias

An encyclopaedia is a systematic compilation of information or knowledge on different topics. Its main purpose is to organise existing knowledge and make it accessible.

Benefits and quality criteria

  • Makes a large amount of knowledge accessible to readers.
  • Provides fundamental information and encourages further reading.
  • Helps researchers identify areas that need more study.
  • Reflects the intellectual and cultural interests of a society.

The textbook lists accuracy, meticulousness, objectivity, a standardised presentation format and updated information as important criteria. Encyclopaedias may be arranged alphabetically or by topic, with an index to help readers locate information. Articles are written by subject experts and may be overseen by an editor or editorial committee.

Four types of encyclopaedic reference works

TypeMeaning and textbook examples
DictionariesArrange words, commonly alphabetically, and provide meanings, synonyms and etymology. Other types include terminology dictionaries, etymological dictionaries, thesauruses, and dictionaries of idioms and proverbs.
Comprehensive encyclopaedia (Vishwakosh)May cover all subjects, as in Encyclopaedia Britannica and Marathi Vishvakosh, or cover one chosen subject comprehensively, as in Bharatiya Samskruti Kosh and Vyayam Dnyankosh.
Encyclopaedic (Koshsadrush) literatureScholarly articles about one or more predetermined subjects. Examples include Maharashtra Jeevan, Shahar Pune and yearbooks.
IndexesAlphabetical lists of people, subjects, places, keywords and references that help readers locate information in books or other publications.

Encyclopaedias and history

The chapter explains that encyclopaedias and historical research both emphasise objectivity. Encyclopaedias published in different languages and countries may reflect different priorities, national policies, ethical values and ideals. They can make knowledge widely accessible and represent a society’s collective intellectual effort.

History-related examples in the textbook: Bharatvarshiya Prachin Aitihasik Kosh by Raghunath Bhaskar Godbole (1876); the three-volume Bharatvarshiya Charitrakosh series; Sthanapothi and Prachin Bharatiya Sthalakosh for historical places; Marathi Vishwakosh, initiated by Yashwantrao Chavan; and the ten-volume Bharatiya Samskruti Kosh edited by Mahadevshastri Joshi.

Specialised terms: A Sthalakosh provides information about places relevant to historical studies. A Samdnya Kosh explains specialised terminology such as colonialism and globalisation.

2. Explain the statements with reasons

(1) Archives and libraries publish research journals, informative pamphlets, leaflets and posters.
Answer: Libraries, museums and archives conserve historical materials and make knowledge available. By publishing journals, pamphlets, leaflets and posters, they share research findings and information with researchers and the public.

(2) Only trained and qualified persons can undertake conservation and preservation work.
Answer: Historical documents and artefacts need careful handling and may require specialised mechanical or chemical cleaning and treatment. Trained people understand the materials, methods and historical value involved, reducing the risk of damage.

3. Write notes

(1) Sthalakosh:
A Sthalakosh is an encyclopaedic work that provides geographic information about places important to historical studies. The textbook mentions Prachin Bharatiya Sthalakosh, compiled by Siddheshwarshastri Chitrav, which records places mentioned in Vedic literature, the Ramayana, the Mahabharata and other sources.

(2) Vishwakosh:
A Vishwakosh is a comprehensive encyclopaedia. It may cover many subjects or provide detailed information on a chosen subject. The textbook says Yashwantrao Chavan initiated the compilation of the Marathi Vishwakosh through the Maharashtra Rajya Sahitya Samskruti Mandal to expand the reach of Marathi language and literature.

(3) Samdnya Kosh:
A Samdnya Kosh explains specialised terminology used in a subject. The textbook gives terms such as colonialism and globalisation as examples. Such works are useful for history teachers and students.

(4) Saraswati Mahal Granthalay:
The library at Thanjavur, Tamil Nadu, developed under the Nayak dynasty in the 16th–17th centuries. Vyankojiraje Bhosale and his successors expanded it, with a major contribution from Sarfojiraje Bhosale. It was renamed the Thanjavur Maharaja Sarfoji’s Saraswati Mahal Library in 1918.

4. Give elaborate answers

(1) Why is library management important?
Answer: Library management ensures that books and information sources are collected and arranged systematically. It supports their conservation and preservation, helps disseminate information, and makes it possible to retrieve the right book for a reader’s needs. Computerised systems and information technology support efficient organisation and access.

(2) Which tasks are important in archives management?
Answer: Archives management involves preserving important documents in their original condition, preparing accurate indexes, and creating reliable systems to retrieve records. Computerised systems and information technology are also important because archives contain large quantities of documents that must be organised and accessed accurately.

Important Questions and Quick Revision

Important questions

What are the three sources of history discussed in the chapter?
Oral sources such as folk songs and folk tales; written sources such as records, letters, diaries and manuscripts; and material sources such as artefacts and fossils.

How are museums, libraries and archives different?
Museums organise and exhibit selected antiquities and preserve other objects; libraries collect, arrange and make books and information accessible; archives preserve important records in their original condition and maintain indexes and retrieval systems.

Why are encyclopaedias useful in historical study?
They organise information systematically, provide background knowledge, help researchers locate information and identify subjects needing further research.

Quick revision

  • History sources: oral, written and material.
  • Museums: Louvre, British Museum, National Museum of Natural History, Indian Museum and Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Vastusangrahalay.
  • Archives: preserve original documents, prepare indexes and provide retrieval systems.
  • Peshwa Daftar: the collection of Modi documents related to Maratha history in the Pune branch of Maharashtra State Archives.
  • Encyclopaedia types: dictionaries, comprehensive encyclopaedias, encyclopaedic literature and indexes.
  • Quality criteria: accuracy, meticulousness, objectivity, standardised presentation and updated information.

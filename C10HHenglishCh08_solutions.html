1. Chapter Overview

“The Twins” is a humorous poem by Henry Sambrooke Leigh. The speaker describes the extraordinary problems caused by his extreme physical resemblance to his twin brother, John. Their family cannot tell them apart, the nurse accidentally gives them each other's names, the speaker is punished for John's mistakes at school, the speaker's intended bride becomes John's wife, and even after the speaker's death the neighbours bury John instead.

Central idea: The poem uses exaggerated and absurd situations to create humour while showing the frustration caused by mistaken identity.

The textbook's poem begins with the brothers being alike in “form and feature, face and limb” and ends with the comic impossibility that the neighbours bury brother John when the speaker dies. fileciteturn177file0L39-L63

If I had an identical twin, we could play harmless pranks such as answering for each other, changing seats before a teacher noticed, or pretending that one of us had already completed a task done by the other. 3. Poem — Stanza-wise Explanation

Stanza 1 — Extraordinary Resemblance

The speaker says that he and his brother looked almost exactly alike in structure, appearance, face and limbs. People frequently mistook one brother for the other. Their relatives were completely confused and could not decide which baby was which.

Meaning: The twins' resemblance is so strong that even their own family cannot identify them correctly.

Stanza 2 — The Names Get Swapped

Before the twins' names were fixed, the nurse accidentally mixed them up while washing them. As a result, the speaker was given John's name and John was given the speaker's name. The speaker humorously describes this as fate's decree—or rather the nurse's whim.

Stanza 3 — Trouble at School

The confusion continues at school. John turns out to be a fool and whenever he does something wrong, the speaker gets punished because people cannot distinguish the twins. The speaker asks everyone what they would do if they were in his situation and needed to prove their own identity.

Stanza 4 — The Bride Mistake

Their resemblance affects the speaker's adult life too. The speaker's intended bride accidentally becomes John's wife. The mistakes continue year after year.

Stanza 5 — The Final Absurdity

The poem ends with the most absurd mistake of all: when the speaker dies, the neighbours come and bury brother John. The ending is deliberately impossible and creates a final comic effect.

3(B). Two humorous lines

“My brother John got christened me,
And I got christened him.”

“And when I died, the neighbours came
And buried brother John.”

What is impossible in the last two lines?

The speaker says that when he died, the neighbours buried John. The impossibility is that the speaker is narrating this event, yet the poem says the wrong brother was buried. The deliberate contradiction creates the final comic effect.

6. Summary in 15–20 Lines

“The Twins” is a humorous poem by Henry Sambrooke Leigh. The speaker and his brother John look extremely similar. Their resemblance is so strong that their relatives cannot tell them apart. Before their names are fixed, a nurse accidentally mixes them up. As a result, each twin receives the other's name. The confusion continues at school. John behaves foolishly, but the speaker is often punished for John's mistakes. The speaker becomes frustrated and asks people how he can prove his own identity. His problems continue into adult life. His intended bride mistakenly becomes John's wife. The poem says that similar mistakes continue for years. Finally, when the speaker dies, the neighbours come and bury John instead. This impossible ending is the most humorous incident in the poem. The poet uses exaggeration, wordplay and sound devices to make the story funny. The poem shows how mistaken identity can create absurd and frustrating situations.

7. Model Long Answers

Q. How does the poet create humour in “The Twins”?

The poet creates humour by describing a series of increasingly absurd mistakes caused by the twins' identical appearance. First, the family cannot distinguish between them. Then the nurse accidentally gives each twin the other's name. At school, the speaker is punished for John's mistakes. In adult life, the speaker's intended bride marries John. The final incident is the most absurd: after the speaker dies, the neighbours bury John. The poet also uses exaggeration, wordplay and alliteration to make these situations entertaining. The first-person narration makes the speaker's frustration itself humorous.

Q. Write a character sketch of the speaker.

The speaker is the narrator of the poem and the identical twin of John. He is repeatedly affected by the confusion caused by their resemblance. He is unfortunate because he receives John's identity at the naming ceremony and is punished for John's mistakes at school. He is also unlucky in love when his intended bride becomes John's wife. Despite these frustrating experiences, he narrates his story humorously. His question about how he can prove that he is himself shows his helplessness and confusion. His final experience in death is presented as an absurd comic situation.

Q. Explain the central idea of the poem.

The central idea is the comic confusion created by the extreme physical resemblance between identical twins. The poem shows that the resemblance causes problems at every stage of the speaker's life—from birth and school to marriage and even death. The poet turns these difficulties into humour through exaggeration and wordplay. The poem therefore combines frustration with comedy and shows how an ordinary identity problem can be developed into a series of absurd situations.

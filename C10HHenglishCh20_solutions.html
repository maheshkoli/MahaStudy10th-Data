3. Mary Kom — Early Life

  • Mary Kom was born in Kangthei village in Manipur.
  • She came from a poor family.
  • Her parents, Mangte Tonpa Kom and Mangte Akham Kom, were tenant farmers working in jhum fields.
  • She helped her parents with farm-related work while attending school.
  • She initially learned athletics and later took up boxing.
  • Her father had been a keen wrestler in his younger days.
  • Dingko Singh's success at the 1998 Bangkok Asian Games inspired youngsters in Manipur, including Mary Kom, to try boxing.

7. Mary Kom's Suggestions for Indian Sports

  • More people should take up sports as a full-time career.
  • More corporates should sponsor players.
  • Financial pressure should be reduced so athletes can focus on their games.
  • Boxing should become popular beyond the Olympics and throughout the year.
  • Nutrition needs greater attention in Indian sports.
  • Support organisations can help athletes remain focused and stress-free.

8. Nutrition, Weight and OGQ

Mary Kom explains that she had to move from her usual 48 kg category to the 51 kg event and therefore needed to gain 3 kg. She says she gained the weight through healthy food rather than unhealthy saturated fats. fileciteturn189file0L192-L204

She also credits OGQ (Olympic Gold Quest) with an important role in her success. According to the interview, OGQ helped her whenever she needed something and took care of arrangements so that she could concentrate on boxing and remain stress-free. fileciteturn189file0L205-L210

9. Motherhood and “Super Mom”

The interview moves beyond boxing and discusses Mary Kom's role as a mother. She says that she has twins and that keeping them under control can sometimes be difficult. When asked whether she would train her children as boxers, she says she has not really thought about it and humorously says, “just let their mother box!” fileciteturn189file0L216-L255

She also says she is good at cooking and household work. When asked whether she would coach Indian women in boxing, she leaves open the possibility of doing so in the future. Finally, when asked whether she prefers “Super Mom” or “Super Boxer”, she clearly says that she loves being a mom and considers that the best thing that has happened to her. fileciteturn189file0L256-L268

Why “Super Mom”? The title connects two sides of her identity: exceptional sporting achievement and her pride in motherhood. The chapter therefore presents her as a successful athlete while also highlighting her family role.

13. Short Paragraph — Life and Work of Mary Kom

Mary Mangte Kom was born in Kangthei village of Manipur and grew up in humble surroundings. Her parents were tenant farmers working in jhum fields. She helped her parents while attending school and developed an interest in athletics. Inspired by Dingko Singh's success, she decided to try boxing. Her boxing career began with victories at the Manipur State Women's Boxing Championship and a regional championship in West Bengal. She began competing internationally in 2001 and won a silver medal at the first AIBA Women's World Boxing Championship. She later became a five-time World Boxing Champion and won a bronze medal at the 2012 Olympics. Her interview shows her confidence, discipline, concern for nutrition and gratitude towards support organisations. She also speaks proudly about motherhood. Her future contribution may include coaching women in boxing. She remains an inspiring example of determination and dedication in sport.

The paragraph follows the four textbook prompts: early life, training, achievements and future plans. fileciteturn189file0L373-L378

14. Debate / Discussion — “What Men Can Do, Women Can Do Better”

Points For the Topic

  • Women have demonstrated excellence in sports, science, business, education and public service.
  • Ability depends on training, opportunity and dedication rather than gender alone.
  • Women athletes have achieved success at national and international levels.
  • Greater participation by women challenges outdated stereotypes.
  • Equal opportunities can help more women develop their talents.

Points Against the Topic

  • Ability should not be measured through a competition between genders.
  • Men and women can have different strengths and individual abilities.
  • Success depends on the person, training, circumstances and opportunity.
  • The focus should be equal opportunity rather than superiority.
  • Respect and cooperation are more constructive than gender-based comparison.

Suggested title for the “For” paragraph: “Breaking Barriers”
Suggested title for the “Against” paragraph: “Ability Beyond Gender”

16. Important Exam Questions

Q1. Describe Mary Kom's journey from humble beginnings to Olympic success.

Mary Kom grew up in a poor farming family in Manipur. She helped her parents, attended school and developed an interest in athletics. Inspired by Dingko Singh, she took up boxing. After winning State and regional championships, she entered international competition in 2001. She later became a successful World Boxing Champion and made history at the 2012 Olympics by winning a bronze medal in the 51 kg category.

Q2. What qualities of Mary Kom are highlighted in the chapter?

The chapter highlights her humility, confidence, hard work, determination, discipline, resilience, dedication to sport and commitment to family.

Q3. How did Mary Kom handle pressure at the Olympics?

She says that being India's only female boxing representative did not put additional pressure on her. She remained confident in herself and relied on the preparation she had already completed.

Q4. What does Mary Kom say about the future of Indian boxing?

She says the future is very bright and expresses confidence that many more boxers will win medals for India. fileciteturn189file0L212-L215

Q5. How does the chapter challenge stereotypes about women?

Mary Kom's career demonstrates that women can compete successfully at the highest level of a physically demanding sport. Her interview also shows her ability to combine professional achievement with household and family responsibilities. The textbook explicitly mentions that she has broken stereotypes about Indian women. fileciteturn189file0L256-L261

Q6. Why is Mary Kom proud of being a mother?

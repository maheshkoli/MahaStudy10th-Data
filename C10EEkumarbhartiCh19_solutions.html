1. Poem at a Glance

Poet: John Keats (1795–1821). The textbook explains that this poem is the opening stanza of the longer poem Endymion, first published in 1818.

The poem presents beauty as a lasting source of joy. Beautiful things in nature and beautiful stories bring comfort, peace and hope. They help people face sadness, gloomy days and the shortage of goodness in the world. The poet compares beauty to an endless fountain of immortal drink that refreshes the human spirit.

Sources of beauty mentioned in the poem: the sun, the moon, old and young trees, daffodils, clear streams, ferns, musk-rose blooms, and lovely tales about the mighty dead.

Expressions of sorrow in the poem: despondence, the dearth of noble natures, gloomy days, unhealthy and over-darkened ways, and gloom spread over life.

2. Answer in your own words

(a) What is the impact of a beautiful thing on us?

A beautiful thing gives us lasting joy. Its loveliness stays in our memory and can bring peace, sweet dreams, health and comfort. It helps lift the sadness from our minds.

(b) What does a thing of beauty keep in store for us?

It keeps a quiet and peaceful “bower” for us, along with sweet dreams, health and calm breathing. Beauty also gives us hope and refreshes our spirits like an endless fountain of immortal drink.

(c) Who are the mighty dead? Why are they attributed with grandeur?

The “mighty dead” are great people of the past who have died. Their remarkable lives and the stories told about them continue to inspire people, so their memory is described as grand and noble.

(d) How does the memory of a beautiful visual scene become a joy forever?

A beautiful scene remains in our memory after we have left the place. Recalling it can bring back the happiness and peace we felt, making the memory a continuing source of comfort.

7(a). Rhyme scheme

Lines 1–8: AABBCCDD (ever/never; keep/sleep; breathing/wreathing; earth/dearth).

Last stanza: The end-rhymes shown in the supplied text are essences/self (near-rhyme), trees/soon, moon/infinite (not a rhyme pair), light/fast, and o’ercast/die. The stanza does not cleanly follow AABBCCDD in the printed text, so mark the actual end words and discuss the rhyme pattern with your teacher rather than forcing a uniform scheme.

8. Appreciation of the poem

Title: A Thing of Beauty is a Joy For Ever
Poet: John Keats
Rhyme scheme: The opening lines use rhyming couplets (AABBCCDD).
Figures of speech: Metaphor, personification and epigram, along with vivid imagery and sound devices.
Theme: Beauty is a lasting source of joy, peace and hope.
Message: Natural beauty and inspiring stories help us face sadness and keep our spirits alive.

John Keats’s poem “A Thing of Beauty is a Joy For Ever” celebrates the lasting happiness that beauty gives us. The poet describes nature through images of trees, daffodils, streams, roses, the sun and the moon. Beauty brings peace, sweet dreams and comfort even during gloomy times. The poet also values the inspiring stories of great people who have died. He uses imagery, metaphor and personification to make his ideas vivid. The poem teaches us that beautiful things can continue to nourish our minds and hearts long after we have seen or experienced them.

5. Central Message

The poem teaches that beauty has a lasting influence on human life. Nature, art and inspiring stories can lift our spirits when life feels gloomy. A beautiful experience may pass, but the joy and comfort it creates can remain with us.

6. Quick Revision

  • Poet: John Keats.
  • Longer poem: Endymion; the textbook says it was first published in 1818.
  • Main idea: A beautiful thing gives lasting joy.
  • Natural images: Sun, moon, trees, daffodils, streams, ferns and musk-rose blooms.
  • Beauty’s effect: Peace, sweet dreams, health, comfort and hope.
  • Poetic techniques: Imagery, metaphor, personification, epigram, rhyme, consonance and assonance.
  • Message: Beauty can help us endure sorrow and renew our spirits.

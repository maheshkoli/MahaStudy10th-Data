1. Chapter Overview

“His First Flight” by Liam O' Flaherty is a story about a young seagull who is afraid to make his first flight. His brothers and sister have already flown, while he remains on the ledge because he believes that his wings will not support him. His parents try to make him fly, but fear prevents him from taking the plunge.

After going without food, he sees his mother approaching with a piece of fish. She stops just beyond his reach. Driven by hunger, he dives towards the fish, falls into space and instinctively spreads his wings. He discovers that he can fly. His fear disappears and he finally joins his family in the air. The story ends with his first successful flight and his family's praise.

Central idea: Fear can prevent us from discovering our own abilities. Courage often comes when we take the first step, and appropriate support or motivation can help us overcome hesitation and become independent.

3. Detailed Explanation of the Story

The Young Seagull's Fear

The young seagull is alone on his ledge. His two brothers and sister have already flown away. He had attempted to run towards the brink and flap his wings, but the sight of the vast sea below frightened him. He believed that his wings would not support him, so he returned to the little hole under the ledge where he slept.

His Family's Attempts

His brothers and sister had successfully learned to fly. His parents had been teaching them to skim over the waves and dive for fish. The young seagull watched these activities but could not gather enough courage to join them. His parents called to him, scolded him and threatened to let him starve if he refused to fly.

Twenty-Four Hours of Hunger

Twenty-four hours pass. The young seagull has not eaten since the previous night. The sun becomes hot on his south-facing ledge. He tries to attract his family's attention by pretending to sleep, but nobody comes to him. His mother alone watches him.

The Mother and the Fish

The young seagull sees his mother eating a piece of fish. He begs her for food. She answers from a distance. Later she picks up a piece of fish and flies towards him. She stops when the fish is almost within reach. The young seagull, maddened by hunger, dives towards the fish.

The First Flight

The moment he falls, terror seizes him. But his wings spread naturally. The wind supports his breast, stomach and wings, and he realises that he is not falling headlong. He begins to soar. He flaps his wings and rises. His family flies around him, and he discovers the joy of flying.

The Final Scene

He flies over the sea and later sees his family on the green surface below. When he tries to land, his feet sink into the water. He becomes frightened again, but he discovers that he is floating. His family praises him and offers him scraps of dog-fish. He has successfully made his first flight.

7. Write-up — Learning a Bicycle / Swimming / Any Skill

Model: Learning to Ride a Bicycle

Learning to ride a bicycle was difficult for me at first. I was afraid of falling and I did not have enough confidence to balance the bicycle. The first few attempts were unsuccessful. My family encouraged me and helped me practise safely. I started by learning how to balance, then I learned to pedal and control the bicycle.

Gradually, my fear reduced. I understood that practice was more important than worrying about falling. After several attempts, I could ride independently. This experience taught me that confidence, courage, regular practice and patience are necessary for learning a new skill.

9. Homophones and Homographs

A. Definitions

Homophones: Words that have the same pronunciation but different spellings and meanings, such as fair–fare and know–no.

Homographs: Words that have the same spelling and pronunciation but different meanings in different situations, such as watch (observe) and watch (shows time).

Homonyms: Homophones and homographs are together referred to as homonyms in the textbook.

12. Passive Voice

In an active sentence, the subject performs the action. In passive voice, the thing affected by the action becomes the subject. The passive form generally uses be + past participle. The doer may be omitted or introduced using by.

Active VoicePassive Voice
Sonali ate the mango.The mango was eaten by Sonali.
The gardener pruned the plants.The plants were pruned by the gardener.
Someone offers a cup of tea to any guest.A cup of tea is offered to any guest.
People tell many anecdotes related to Mark Twain.Many anecdotes related to Mark Twain are told.
My aunt gave this pen to me.This pen was given to me by my aunt.
Important: The textbook notes that only transitive verbs can be used in the passive voice because they take an object that can become the subject of the passive sentence.

13. Character Sketch — Young Seagull

The young seagull is initially fearful, hesitant and lacking in confidence. Although his wings are capable of flight, he is convinced that they will not support him. He watches his siblings succeed but cannot gather courage to follow them. Hunger finally forces him to take the risk. Once he discovers that his wings can support him, he becomes confident and joyful. He quickly learns to soar, dive and curve through the air. His journey shows a transformation from fear and dependence to courage, confidence and independence.

14. Role of the Mother Seagull

The mother plays a decisive role in the young seagull's first flight. She does not simply carry him or force him physically. Instead, she uses the piece of fish to bring him to the point where he must act for himself. She flies towards him with the fish and stops just beyond his reach. His hunger makes him dive towards it. This action causes him to discover his own ability to fly. Thus, the mother provides motivation while allowing the young seagull to take the actual step independently.

17. Model Long Answers

Q. Explain the young seagull's transformation.

At the beginning, the young seagull is frightened and lacks confidence. Although his brothers and sister have already flown, he cannot take the risk because he believes his wings will not support him. His parents scold and encourage him, but he remains on the ledge. Hunger becomes the turning point. When his mother brings a piece of fish and stops just beyond his reach, he dives after it. He falls into space, experiences terror, but then spreads his wings and discovers that he can fly. Soon he is soaring, diving and curving through the air. Thus, he changes from a fearful and dependent bird into a confident and independent one.

Q. “Fear is the biggest obstacle to success.” Explain with reference to the story.

The young seagull already has wings, but fear prevents him from using them. He imagines that he will fall into the sea and therefore repeatedly returns to safety. His brothers and sister have learned to fly, but he remains behind. Only when hunger becomes stronger than fear does he take the plunge. Once he discovers that his wings can support him, his fear disappears and he enjoys flying. The story therefore shows that fear can hide our abilities and that taking the first step can help us discover our potential.

Q. What message do you learn from “His First Flight”?

The story teaches us that fear should not permanently stop us from trying. We may have abilities that we do not recognise because we are afraid to use them. Encouragement and support can help, but we must finally take the step ourselves. Experience builds confidence. The story also shows the importance of independence, courage and perseverance when facing a new challenge.

Chapter 4.3: World Heritage

1. Chapter at a Glance

This chapter introduces the concept of "World Heritage Sites," which are locations of significant cultural or natural importance to humanity, determined by UNESCO. The chapter covers the history of how these sites came to be protected, the role of the World Heritage Committee, the process for a site to be designated as a World Heritage Site, and the dangers that threaten these sites.

2. Important Concepts Explained

What is a World Heritage Site?

A World Heritage Site is a location determined by the United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization (UNESCO) to have significant cultural or natural importance to humanity. These sites are protected and maintained by the International World Heritage Programme, which is administered by the UNESCO World Heritage Committee.

Types of World Heritage Sites

  • Cultural Sites: Include monuments, buildings, and cities (e.g., Sydney Opera House, Historic Center of Vienna).
  • Natural Sites: Feature natural locations (e.g., Yellowstone and Grand Canyon National Parks).
  • Mixed Sites: A combination of both cultural and natural areas (e.g., Peru’s Machu Picchu).

History of World Heritage Sites

  • 1954: Egypt planned to build the Aswan High Dam, which would have flooded the valley containing the Abu Simbel Temples.
  • 1959: UNESCO launched an international campaign to dismantle and move the temples to higher ground. This success led to the idea of an international organization for protecting cultural heritage.
  • 1965: A White House Conference in the U.S. called for a "World Heritage Trust."
  • 1968: The International Union for Conservation of Nature developed similar goals.
  • 1972: The Convention concerning the Protection of World Cultural and Natural Heritage was adopted by UNESCO’s General Conference.

The World Heritage Committee

  • The main group responsible for establishing which sites are listed as UNESCO World Heritage Sites.
  • Meets once a year.
  • Consists of representatives from 21 State Parties elected for six-year terms.
  • Responsible for identifying and nominating new sites.

Becoming a World Heritage Site (Five Steps)

  1. Inventory: A country or State Party takes an inventory of its significant cultural and natural sites (Tentative List).
  2. Nomination File: Countries select sites from their Tentative List to be included on a Nomination File.
  3. Review: Two Advisory Bodies (International Council on Monuments and Sites, and the World Conservation Union) review the file and make recommendations to the World Heritage Committee.
  4. Decision: The World Heritage Committee meets to review recommendations and decide which sites to add to the World Heritage List.
  5. Inscription: The site must meet at least one of ten selection criteria to be inscribed on the World Heritage List.

World Heritage Sites in Danger

Sites can be destroyed by war, poaching, natural disasters, uncontrolled urbanization, heavy tourist traffic, and environmental factors (air pollution, acid rain). These sites are placed on a separate "List of World Heritage Sites in Danger," allowing the Committee to allocate resources for protection and restoration. If a site loses its characteristics, it can be deleted from the list.

3. Definitions and Key Terms

  • Heritage: That which is valued because of historical, natural, or cultural importance.
  • Characteristics: Distinguishing features or qualities.
  • Artefacts: Any objects made by a human being.
  • Dismantling: Disassembling or pulling down.
  • Inventory: A detailed list.
  • Recommendations: Suggestions or proposals.
  • Criteria: Fixed basis to judge.
  • Inscribe: To print, write, or engrave.
  • Territory: Region.
  • Urbanization: Including more areas in cities, expanding city area.
  • Allocate: To assign.

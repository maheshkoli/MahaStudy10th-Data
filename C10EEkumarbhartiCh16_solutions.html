1. Poem at a Glance

Poem: “The Will to Win”

Poet: Berton Braley. The textbook says he was born in Madison, Wisconsin, showed an early talent for writing, and wrote around 4,000 verses and several hundred short stories.

The poem explains that achieving a goal requires strong desire, planning, hard work, persistence, courage, faith, hope and confidence. It lists obstacles such as poverty, hunger, sickness and pain, and urges readers not to let opposition or hardship stop their efforts.

Central message: A strong will, sustained effort and determination help people pursue their goals despite difficulties.

1(C). Meaning of “scheme”

Correct answer: (d) Plan of action.

1(D). Does “Give up your time and your peace and your sleep for it” mean being desperate or restless?

The line means that we should be deeply committed to our goal and willing to make sacrifices to achieve it. It does not mean that we should behave irrationally or harm our health. The poet emphasises dedication, focus, planning and persistent effort.

1(E). Explain the phrases

  1. To go out and fight for it: To work actively towards a goal and overcome obstacles.
  2. Makes you quite mad enough: To desire the goal so strongly that it becomes the centre of your attention.
  3. Lose all your terror of the opposition for it: Do not let fear of competitors or difficulties stop you.
  4. With all your capacity, strength and sagacity: Use all your ability, energy and wisdom.

  1. False. The poet says that a person may have to give up time, peace and sleep to pursue a goal. This is an expression of dedication, not a recommendation to neglect necessary rest.

  4. True according to the poem. The final line says, “with the help of GOD you’ll get it!”

7. Why does every stanza begin with “If”?

The repeated word “If” introduces conditions that a person must fulfil to achieve a goal. It gives the poem a steady rhythm, connects the stanzas and builds emphasis as the poet lists the commitment and determination required for success.

4. Poetic Devices

8(A). Climax — two examples

  1. “faith, hope and confidence”
  2. “capacity, strength and sagacity”

These are suggested examples from the poem’s lists. Climax is clearest when the ideas rise in intensity or importance.

8(B). Tautology — two examples

  1. “useless and worthless”
  2. “time and your peace and your sleep” — this is a possible classroom interpretation of closely related sacrifices, though it is not a strict synonym pair.

5. Writing Activities

9. Sample speech: “How to Achieve Success”

Good morning respected teachers and my dear friends.

Today I am going to speak about how to achieve success. Success does not come only from dreaming; it requires planning, hard work and determination. We should set a clear goal, use our abilities wisely and continue working even when we face difficulties. Failure should teach us lessons instead of making us give up. We need patience, confidence, discipline and the courage to face opposition. As the poem “The Will to Win” teaches us, a strong desire combined with continuous effort helps us move towards our goals. At the same time, we should protect our health and respect others. Let us work honestly, learn every day and keep trying until we improve.

Thank you.

10. Appreciation of the poem “The Will to Win”

“The Will to Win” is an inspirational poem by Berton Braley. Its central theme is the importance of determination, hard work and persistence in achieving one’s goals. The poet explains that a person must want a goal strongly, plan for it and use all their capacity, strength and wisdom. The poem mentions obstacles such as poverty, hunger, sickness and pain, but encourages readers not to surrender to them. The repeated use of “If” gives the poem rhythm and emphasises the conditions for success. Expressions such as “day and night” and “faith, hope and confidence” strengthen its message. The poem inspires readers to face difficulties with courage and keep working towards their ambitions.

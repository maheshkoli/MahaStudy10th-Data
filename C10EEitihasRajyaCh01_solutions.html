Chapter 1: Historiography — Development in the West

Chapter Map from the Textbook

The textbook chapter is organised into four sections: 1.1 Tradition of Historiography, 1.2 Modern Historiography, 1.3 Development of Scientific Perspective in Europe and Historiography, and 1.4 Notable Scholars.

Historical Research Method

The textbook's diagram on page 1 places the writing of historical narrative at the centre. It connects this work with examining historical references, critically examining sources, collecting information and comparing historical transitions, understanding time and space, formulating relevant questions, and forming hypotheses.

Modern Historiography: Four Characteristics

  1. Its method is based on scientific principles and begins with relevant questions.
  2. The questions are anthropocentric: they concern the deeds of people in a particular period.
  3. Answers are supported by reliable evidence.
  4. History presents humankind's journey through past human deeds.

Notable Scholars in the Textbook

  • Herodotus: The Greek historian associated with the book The Histories.
  • René Descartes (1596–1650): Emphasised critically checking the reliability of historical documents and excluding grounds for doubt.
  • Voltaire (1694–1778): Emphasised social traditions, trade, economy and agriculture alongside chronology; the textbook calls him the founder of modern historiography.
  • Georg Wilhelm Friedrich Hegel (1770–1831): Emphasised logical presentation of historical reality and the role of opposing ideas in dialectics: thesis, antithesis and synthesis.
  • Leopold von Ranké (1795–1886): Emphasised original documents and careful critical examination of historical sources.
  • Karl Marx (1818–1883): Explained history through human needs, means of production, class divisions and class struggle.
  • Annales School: Expanded historical writing beyond politics, leaders and wars to include climate, local people, agriculture, trade, technology, communication and collective psychology.
  • Feminist historiography: Re-examines history from women's perspectives and studies women's work, family lives, institutions and social roles.
  • Michel Foucault (1926–1984): Developed the “archaeology of knowledge” approach and focused on transitions and areas often overlooked in historical writing.

1. Chapter at a Glance

This chapter provides an overview of the Indian democracy, focusing on the working of the Constitution since it came into force on 26th January 1950. It examines the progress made in political processes and the steps taken to establish social justice and equality. The chapter focuses on three main areas:

  1. Democracy
  2. Social Justice
  3. Judicial System

2. Important Concepts Explained

Democracy

  • Political Maturity: Democracy is not just a representative structure; it is a way of life. It requires integrating democratic principles into actual practice.
  • Right to Vote: The Constitution guaranteed adult suffrage. Initially, the voting age was 21, later reduced to 18 to encourage participation from the young generation. India is the largest democracy in the world with the largest number of voters.
  • Decentralisation of Democracy: This is central to democratic government to control the misuse of power. The 73rd and 74th amendments to the Constitution gave constitutional status to local self-governing institutions.
  • Right to Information (R.T.I. 2005): This act empowers citizens, ensures transparency and accountability, and reduces secrecy in government functioning.
  • Democratic Reforms as Rights: Since 2000, rights like education and food security have been considered fundamental rights of citizens, strengthening democracy.

Social Justice and Equality

  • Objective: To remove social conditions responsible for injustice and ensure equality of dignity.
  • Policy of Reservation: Reserved seats in educational institutions and government services for Scheduled Castes (SC), Scheduled Tribes (ST), and Other Backward Classes (OBC) to provide opportunities to historically deprived sections.
  • Prevention of Atrocities Act: Protects SC and ST communities from injustice.
  • Provisions for Minorities: Policies to protect minorities and prohibit discrimination based on caste, religion, race, language, or region.
  • Laws for Women: Various acts (e.g., Dowry Prohibition Act, Domestic Violence Prohibition Act) aim to empower women and protect their freedom.

Role of Judiciary

  • The judiciary has played a crucial role in interpreting the Constitution to protect fundamental rights and achieve social justice.
  • Basic Framework: The Judiciary established that the Parliament cannot alter the "basic structure" of the Constitution while amending it.
  • Significant Judicial Decisions: The Court has given landmark judgments on rights of children, human rights, protection of women's dignity, individual freedom, and tribal empowerment.

3. Definitions and Key Terms

  • Democracy: A system of government where power is vested in the people and exercised by them directly or through freely elected representatives.
  • Adult Suffrage: The right of all adult citizens to vote in an election.
  • Decentralisation: The transfer of authority and responsibility from a central government to local or regional levels.
  • Social Justice: The fair and just relation between the individual and society, measured by the distribution of wealth, opportunities, and social privileges.
  • Basic Structure: The fundamental principles of the Constitution that cannot be altered by the Parliament.

4. Formulas, Rules, and Properties

  • 73rd and 74th Amendments: Provided constitutional status to local self-governing institutions.
  • Right to Information Act (2005): A legal provision ensuring transparency and accountability in government.

6. Exercise / Practice Set Solutions

1. Choose the correct option from the given options and complete the sentences.

(1) In Maharashtra ............ seats are reserved for women in local self-governing institutions.

(a) 25% (b) 30% (c) 40% (d) 50%

  • Answer: (d) 50%

(2) Which of the following laws created a favourable environment for women to secure freedom and self-development?

(a) Right to Information Act (b) Dowry Prohibition Act (c) Food Security Act (d) None of the above

  • Answer: (b) Dowry Prohibition Act

(3) The essence of democracy is ..........

(a) universal adult franchise. (b) decentralisation of power. (c) policy of reservation of seats. (d) judicial decisions.

  • Answer: (b) decentralisation of power.

2. State whether following statements are true or false. Give reasons for your answer.

(1) Indian democracy is considered the largest democracy in the world.

  • Answer: True.
  • Reason: India has the largest number of voters compared to any other democratic nation in the world.

(2) Secrecy in the working of Government has increased due to the Right to Information.

  • Answer: False.
  • Reason: The Right to Information Act has made the government more transparent and has reduced the element of secrecy in the working of the government.

(3) The nature of Constitution is seen as a living document.

  • Answer: True.
  • Reason: The Constitution is dynamic and can be amended by the Parliament to adapt to changing conditions, while maintaining its basic structure.

3. Write short notes.

(1) Provisions regarding minorities:

  • The Indian Constitution includes several provisions to protect minorities. It prohibits discrimination based on caste, religion, race, language, or region. It provides policies to ensure minorities have equal opportunities for education and employment, and protects their right to equality, freedom, and cultural/educational rights.

(2) Policy of reservation of seats:

  • This policy is designed to provide opportunities to sections of society that have been historically deprived of education and employment. Seats are reserved in educational institutions and government services for Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes, and Other Backward Classes to bring them into the mainstream.

(3) Women representation in the Loksabha:

  • Historically, women have had inadequate representation in Indian politics. While the 73rd and 74th amendments reserved 50% of seats for women in local self-governing institutions in many states, their representation in the Loksabha has been low, though it has shown a gradual increase from 4.50% in 1951-52 to 12.15% in 2014.

4. Explain the following concepts.

(1) Right based approach:

  • In the decades after independence, citizens were often viewed as beneficiaries of government policies. The "rights-based approach" shifts this perspective, considering development and access to services (like information, education, and food security) as the fundamental rights of the citizens.

(2) Right to information:

  • The Right to Information Act (2005) empowers citizens by giving them the right to know about the functioning of the government. It is an essential condition for democracy, as it builds trust between the government and citizens, ensures transparency, and promotes accountability.

5. Answer the following questions.

(1) What are the effects of reducing the voting age from 21 years to 18 years?

  • Answer: Reducing the voting age from 21 to 18 gave the young generation in India a direct opportunity for political participation. This change was not just quantitative (increasing the number of voters) but also qualitative, as new political parties emerged with the active support of young voters, representing their expectations.

(2) What is meant by establishment of social justice?

  • Answer: Establishing social justice means removing social conditions that cause injustice and insisting on the equality of dignity for all individuals. It aims to end discrimination based on caste, religion, language, gender, race, or property, and ensures equal opportunity for development for every individual.

(3) Which decision of the Court has resulted in protection of honour and dignity of women?

  • Answer: The judiciary has made several significant decisions to protect the honour and dignity of women. Specific laws like the Dowry Prohibition Act and the Domestic Violence Prohibition Act, supported by judicial interpretations, have created a favourable environment for protecting women's freedom and securing their development.

7. Important Questions and Answers

Q: What is the "basic structure" of the Constitution?

  • A: The basic structure includes fundamental provisions such as the republican and democratic form of government, the federal structure, the unity and integrity of the nation, the sovereignty of the nation, and secularism. The Judiciary has ruled that the Parliament cannot alter these basic features.

Q: Why is decentralisation of power important?

  • A: Decentralisation of power is central to democratic government because it controls the misuse of power and creates opportunities for political participation for common people.

8. Common Errors and How to Avoid Them

  • Error: Confusing "representative democracy" with "direct democracy."
  • Correction: Remember that India is a representative democracy where citizens elect representatives to run the government, rather than participating in every decision directly.
  • Error: Thinking the Parliament can change any part of the Constitution.
  • Correction: The Parliament can amend the Constitution, but the Judiciary has specified that it cannot alter the "basic structure."

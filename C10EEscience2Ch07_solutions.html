1. Chapter at a Glance

This chapter explores the field of microbiology, focusing on how microorganisms are applied in various industries to benefit society. Key topics include:

  • Applied and Industrial Microbiology: The commercial use of microbes.
  • Products: Production of dairy products (yoghurt, cheese, butter), probiotics, bread, vinegar, and beverages.
  • Microbial Enzymes: Their use as eco-friendly catalysts in industry.
  • Microbes and Fuels: Production of biofuels and gaseous fuels.
  • Microbial Pollution Control: Using microbes for waste management, sewage treatment, and bioremediation.
  • Microbes and Farming: Use of bio-inoculants and bio-insecticides.

2. Important Concepts Explained

Applied and Industrial Microbiology

  • Applied Microbiology: A branch of biology studying enzymes related to prokaryotic and eukaryotic microbes, proteins, and molecular biology. It focuses on large-scale production of food and medicines.
  • Industrial Microbiology: Focuses on the commercial use of microbes for economic, social, and environmental processes. Features include fermentation for producing bread, cheese, wine, and chemicals, as well as using microbes for pollution control.

Dairy Products

  • Basic Process: Milk is pasteurized to remove unwanted microbes, then fermented using Lactobacilli. Lactose sugar is converted into lactic acid, which coagulates milk proteins.
  • Yoghurt: Produced by fermenting milk with Streptococcus thermophilus and Lactobacillus delbrueckii (1:1 ratio). Streptococcus produces lactic acid for consistency, and Lactobacilli produce acetaldehyde for taste.
  • Cheese: Produced by adding specific microbes (Lactobacillus lactis, L. cremoris, Streptococcus thermophilus) and rennet (or fungal protease) to milk. The whey is removed, and the curd is processed and ripened.

Probiotics

  • These are milk products containing active bacteria (e.g., Lactobacillus, Bifidobacterium). They maintain intestinal microbial balance, improve resistance, and lower the effects of harmful substances.

Bread and Vinegar

  • Bread: Made by mixing flour with baker’s yeast (Saccharomyces cerevisiae). Fermentation produces CO2 and ethanol; CO2 makes the bread spongy.
  • Vinegar: Produced by fermenting carbon compounds (fruit juices, sugar molasses) with Saccharomyces cerevisiae to get ethanol, followed by microbial degradation using Acetobacter and Gluconobacter. It is 4% acetic acid (CH3COOH).

Microbial Enzymes

  • Used as catalysts in industries (textile, leather, paper, etc.) because they are active at low temperature, pH, and pressure, saving energy and being eco-friendly. Examples: oxidoreductases, transferases, hydrolases.

Microbial Pollution Control

  • Land-filling: Degradable waste is dumped in pits, covered with soil/leafy waste, and decomposed by microbes to form compost.
  • Sewage Management: Microbes decompose carbon compounds in sewage, releasing methane and CO2. Phenol-oxidizing bacteria decompose xenobiotic chemicals.
  • Bioremediation: Using microbes (e.g., Pseudomonas, Alcanovorax borkumensis) to clean oil spills in oceans.

Microbes and Farming

  • Bio-inoculants: Microbes sprayed on seeds or released into plants to supply nutrients and improve growth (e.g., Azotobacter).
  • Bio-insecticides: Bacterial/fungal toxins (e.g., Spinosad) integrated into plants to kill pests without harming the plant.

3. Definitions and Key Terms

  • Fermentation: A metabolic process that produces chemical changes in organic substrates through the action of enzymes in microorganisms.
  • Probiotics: Food products containing active beneficial bacteria that improve intestinal health.
  • Bioremediation: The use of microorganisms to consume and break down environmental pollutants.
  • Bio-inoculants: Microbial preparations used to improve plant growth and soil fertility.
  • Bio-insecticides: Biological agents (bacteria, fungi, viruses) used to control pests.

4. Formulas, Rules, and Properties

  • Vinegar: 4% Acetic acid (CH3COOH).
  • Fermentation (Bread): Sugar $\xrightarrow{\text{Yeast}}$ CO2 + Ethanol.

Page 95: Exercise 3

3. Answer the following.

  • a. Which fuels can be obtained by microbial processes? Why is it necessary to increase the use of such fuels?
  • Fuels like methane (biogas) and ethanol can be obtained. It is necessary to increase their use because they are renewable, cleaner (smokeless), and help reduce dependence on limited fossil fuel reserves.
  • b. How can the oil spills of rivers and oceans be cleaned?
  • Oil spills can be cleaned using hydrocarbonoclastic bacteria (HCB) like Pseudomonas spp. and Alcanovorax borkumensis, which decompose the hydrocarbons into CO2 and water.
  • c. How can the soil polluted by acid rain be made fertile again?
  • Bacteria like Acidophillium spp. and Acidobacillus ferroxidens can be used to consume sulphuric acid present in the soil due to acid rain, thereby reducing soil pollution.
  • d. Explain the importance of biopesticides in organic farming.
  • Biopesticides (bacterial/fungal toxins) are important because they are toxic only to specific pests, do not harm the plants, and prevent the harmful effects of chemical pesticides on humans and the environment.
  • e. Which are the reasons for increasing the popularity of probiotic products?
  • Probiotics are popular because they maintain intestinal microbial balance, improve digestion, boost resistance, and help treat conditions like diarrhoea.
  • f. How the bread and other products produced using baker’s yeast are nutritious?
  • Yeast contains carbohydrates, fats, proteins, vitamins, and minerals. Fermentation makes these nutrients more accessible and adds nutritional value to the bread.
  • g. Which precautions are necessary for proper decomposition of domestic waste?
  • Waste should be segregated into wet and dry waste at the source. Wet waste should be composted, and dry waste should be processed appropriately to prevent environmental pollution.
  • h. Why is it necessary to ban the use of plastic bags?
  • Plastic is non-biodegradable and accumulates in the environment, causing pollution. While some microbes can decompose PET, the process is slow, making plastic a major environmental hazard.

Exercise 7 — Microbes used in environmental management

  • Oil spills: Pseudomonas species and Alcanovorax borkumensis decompose hydrocarbons.
  • PET plastic: species of Vibrio and Ideonella sakaiensis are described as able to decompose PET.
  • Rubber waste: fungi such as Actinomycetes, Streptomyces, Nocardia and Actinoplanes can decompose rubber.
  • Acid-rain-related soil pollution: the textbook names Acidophillium spp. and Acidobacillus ferroxidens.
  • Uranium salts in wastewater: Geobacter converts soluble uranium salts into insoluble salts, helping prevent contamination of groundwater.
  • Organic farming: microbial inoculants supply nutrients and support plant growth; bioinsecticides such as spinosad help control pests.

7. Important Questions and Answers

  • Q: What is the role of Lactobacilli in dairy production?
  • A: Lactobacilli ferment lactose sugar in milk into lactic acid, which coagulates milk proteins, giving dairy products their texture and consistency.
  • Q: Why are microbial enzymes preferred over chemical catalysts?
  • A: They are active at low temperature, pH, and pressure, which saves energy, prevents the need for erosion-proof instruments, and minimizes by-products.

8. Common Errors and How to Avoid Them

  • Confusing Probiotics with Antibiotics: Probiotics are beneficial live bacteria, while antibiotics are substances used to kill harmful bacteria.
  • Misunderstanding Fermentation: Students often think fermentation only produces alcohol; remember it also produces acids (like lactic acid) and gases (like CO2) depending on the microbe and substrate.

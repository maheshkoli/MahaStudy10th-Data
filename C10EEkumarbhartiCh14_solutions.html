1. Story at a Glance

Writer: Jerome Weidman (1913–1998), an American playwright and novelist.

The narrator attends a concert but feels uncomfortable because he does not understand classical music and thinks he is tone-deaf. His neighbour turns out to be Albert Einstein. Einstein patiently begins with a familiar Bing Crosby song, moves to John McCormack singing “The Trumpeter,” and gradually introduces music without words. He explains that learning to appreciate music is like learning mathematics: one starts with simpler ideas and advances step by step. The narrator finally listens to Bach with understanding.

Main idea: With patience, encouragement and the right starting point, people can learn to appreciate something that once seemed difficult. Einstein calls this “opening up the frontiers of beauty.”

  2. When the narrator turned to look at his neighbour, he saw a very famous face with a shock of white hair and a pipe.

4. Grammar and Word Work

7. Rewrite in indirect narration

  1. He told me that I was not tone-deaf.
  2. Einstein asked me whether I could have done it.
  3. He ordered me to sing that back.
  4. He asked me what kind of music I liked.
  5. She apologised to Dr Einstein that he had missed so much. (A natural reported version of the apology; the original statement is “I’m so sorry, Dr Einstein.”)

8(A). Degrees of comparison: choose the correct expression

  1. I am not just as tall as my friend. (The sentence is awkward with the given choices; “not as tall as” is the standard construction.)
  2. They are as helpful as their neighbours.
  3. No other desert in Africa is nearly so big as the Sahara Desert. (From the options printed; more natural English: “No other desert in Africa is as big as the Sahara Desert.”)
  4. Very few animals in the wild are exactly as huge as an elephant.
  5. Some snacks are at least as tasty as a pizza.

8(B). Change the degree without changing the meaning

  1. Superlative: Tanvi is the most brilliant student in the class.
  2. Comparative: A diamond is more precious than most other stones.
  3. Positive: Her sister’s handwriting is not as good as hers.
  4. Comparative: Jupiter is larger than any other planet.
  5. Positive: He is not as great as all the other leaders. (This is one possible positive-degree rendering; the original is somewhat ambiguous.)
  6. Superlative: This is the best painting in the palace.

9. Word chain

One possible chain using the final letter of each word as the first letter of the next:

Pianist → Tune → End → Danger → Rich → House → Yellow → Water → Rat

5. Writing Activities

11. “Music is the medicine of the mind.” Write about an experience.

Once, I was worried about an examination and could not concentrate on my studies. I took a short break and listened to my favourite songs. The music helped me relax and gradually improved my mood. After a while, I felt calmer and returned to my work with a clearer mind. This experience taught me that music cannot solve every problem, but it can comfort us, reduce stress and give us fresh energy. In this way, music can act like medicine for the mind.

12. Write a few lines about the personalities

  • Albert Einstein: A theoretical physicist best known for the theory of relativity. He received the 1921 Nobel Prize in Physics for his explanation of the photoelectric effect.
  • Johann Sebastian Bach: A German composer and musician of the Baroque period, remembered for his influential choral, orchestral, keyboard and instrumental works.
  • Bing Crosby: An American singer and actor, widely known for his warm singing voice and popular recordings.
  • John McCormack: An Irish tenor celebrated for opera and concert singing, including recordings of popular and classical songs.

6. Quick Revision

  • Writer: Jerome Weidman.
  • Main personality: Albert Einstein.
  • Narrator’s initial belief: He thinks he is tone-deaf.
  • Einstein’s teaching method: Start with familiar songs and move gradually to more advanced music.
  • Learning analogy: Music appreciation is like mathematics—basic steps support more advanced learning.
  • Important phrase: “Opening up the frontiers of beauty.”
  • Message: Patient guidance can help people discover and appreciate new experiences.

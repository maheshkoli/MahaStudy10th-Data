This chapter explores the fundamental mechanisms of heredity, how biological characters are transferred across generations, and the process of evolution. Students will learn about:

2. Important Concepts Explained

Heredity and Hereditary Changes

Heredity is the transfer of biological characters from one generation to another via genes. Johann Gregor Mendel is the pioneer of modern genetics. In 1944, Avery, McCarthy, and MacLeod proved that DNA is the genetic material in all living organisms (except viruses).

Central Dogma (Protein Synthesis)

Genes (DNA) control the structure and function of the body through proteins.

  1. Transcription: mRNA is produced from DNA in the nucleus. The sequence of nucleotides in mRNA is complementary to the DNA strand used. Uracil is used in RNA instead of thymine.
  2. Translation: The mRNA moves to the cytoplasm. tRNA brings amino acids as per the code on mRNA (triplet codon).
  3. Translocation: The ribosome moves along the mRNA by the distance of one triplet codon, bonding amino acids with peptide bonds to form a protein chain.

Mutation

A mutation is a sudden change in the position of any nucleotide of a gene, causing a minor or considerable change. This is a proof for Darwin’s theory of natural selection.

Evolution

Evolution is the gradual, slow, and continuous change in living organisms over a long duration (approx. 300 crore years).

Evidences of Evolution

  • Morphological: Similarities in structure (mouth, nostrils, ear pinnae) in animals and leaf shape/venation in plants suggest common ancestry.
  • Anatomical: Similarities in bone structure (e.g., human hand, cat’s foreleg, whale’s flipper) despite different functions indicate a common ancestor.
  • Vestigial Organs: Degenerated or underdeveloped useless organs (e.g., appendix, tail-bone, wisdom teeth in humans) that were functional in ancestors.
  • Paleontological: Fossils (remnants/impressions of organisms) preserved underground. Carbon dating (C-14) is used to determine their age.
  • Connecting Links: Organisms showing characters of two different groups (e.g., Peripatus between Annelida and Arthropoda; Duckbill Platypus between reptiles and mammals).
  • Embryological: Similarities in initial embryonic stages of various vertebrates suggest a common origin.

Theories of Evolution

  • Darwin’s Theory of Natural Selection: Organisms reproduce prolifically and compete in a life-threatening manner. Nature selects only those fit to survive ("Survival of the Fittest").
  • Lamarckism (Inheritance of Acquired Characters): Proposed that morphological changes occur due to activities or laziness of an organism ("use or disuse of organs") and are transferred to the next generation. This theory was later disproved.

3. Definitions and Key Terms

  • Heredity: The transfer of biological characters from one generation to another via genes.
  • Transcription: The process of RNA synthesis from DNA.
  • Translation: The process of protein synthesis from mRNA using tRNA.
  • Translocation: The movement of the ribosome along the mRNA during protein synthesis.
  • Mutation: A sudden change in the position of a nucleotide in a gene.
  • Evolution: The gradual change occurring in living organisms over a long duration.
  • Vestigial Organs: Degenerated or underdeveloped useless organs in an organism.
  • Fossils: Remnants and impressions of organisms preserved underground.
  • Connecting Links: Organisms that show morphological characters related to two different groups.
  • Species: A group of organisms that can produce fertile individuals through natural reproduction.

---

4. Formulas, Rules, and Properties

  • Central Dogma: DNA → mRNA → Protein.
  • Carbon Dating: A method to determine the age of fossils by measuring the radioactivity of C-14 and the ratio of C-14 to C-12.

2. Read the following statements and justify same in your own words with the help of suitable examples.

a. Human evolution began approximately 7 crore years ago.

  • Justification: The last dinosaurs disappeared about 7 crore years ago. At that time, monkey-like animals evolved from ancestors similar to modern lemurs. These evolved into ape-like animals, eventually leading to the human lineage.

b. Geographical and reproductive isolation of organisms gradually leads to speciation.

  • Justification: Species are groups that reproduce naturally. When a population is separated geographically, they adapt to different conditions. Over generations, genetic variations accumulate, and reproductive isolation prevents them from interbreeding with the original population, leading to the formation of a new species.

c. Study of fossils is an important aspect of study of evolution.

  • Justification: Fossils provide physical evidence of organisms that existed millions of years ago. By studying their structure and the rock layers they are found in, we can trace the evolutionary history and the gradual transition from simple to complex organisms.

4. Write short notes based upon the information known to you.

  • a. Lamarckism: Proposed by Jean-Baptiste Lamarck, it suggests that morphological changes occur due to the "use or disuse of organs" and these acquired characters are passed to the next generation. It was later disproved.
  • b. Darwin’s theory of natural selection: Charles Darwin proposed that organisms reproduce prolifically and compete for survival. Nature selects those with favorable modifications ("Survival of the Fittest").
  • c. Embryology: The study of embryonic developmental stages. Similarities in the initial stages of embryos of different vertebrates suggest a common origin.

5. Define heredity. Explain the mechanism of hereditary changes.

  • Definition: Heredity is the transfer of biological characters from one generation to another via genes.
  • Mechanism: Hereditary changes occur through mutations (sudden changes in gene nucleotide sequence) or through the recombination of genes during sexual reproduction. These changes are then passed to the progeny.

6. Define vestigial organs. Write names of some vestigial organs in human body and write the names of those animals in whom same organs are functional.

  • Definition: Vestigial organs are degenerated or underdeveloped useless organs in an organism that were functional in ancestors.
  • Examples:
  • Appendix: Functional in ruminants.
  • Ear muscles: Functional in monkeys for ear movement.
  • Tail-bone (Coccyx): Functional as a tail in other mammals.

7. Answer the following questions.

  • a. How are the hereditary changes responsible for evolution? Hereditary changes (mutations/recombinations) introduce variations in a population. Natural selection acts on these variations, favoring those that are better adapted, which over time leads to evolution.
  • b. Explain the process of formation of complex proteins. DNA stores the code for proteins. mRNA is transcribed from DNA. tRNA brings specific amino acids to the ribosome based on the mRNA code (translation). Ribosomes move along the mRNA (translocation), linking amino acids with peptide bonds to form complex protein chains.
  • c. Explain the theory of evolution and mention the proof supporting it. Evolution is the gradual development of organisms. Proofs include morphological, anatomical, vestigial, paleontological, connecting links, and embryological evidences.
  • d. Explain with suitable examples importance of anatomical evidences in evolution. Anatomical evidence, like the similar bone structure in the human hand, cat's foreleg, and whale's flipper, shows that despite different uses, these organs share a common structural plan, suggesting a common ancestor.
  • e. Define fossil. Explain importance of fossils as proof of evolution. Fossils are preserved remnants or impressions of ancient organisms. They provide a chronological record of life, showing the transition from simple to complex forms over geological time.
  • f. Write evolutionary history of modern man. Human evolution began with ape-like ancestors. Over millions of years, they developed erect posture, larger brains, and the ability to use tools. Key stages include Ramapithecus, Australopithecus, Homo habilis (skilled human), Homo erectus (man with erect posture), Neanderthal man, and finally Homo sapiens (modern man).

7. Important Questions and Answers

  • Q: Who is the pioneer of modern genetics?
  • A: Johann Gregor Mendel.
  • Q: What is the 'Central Dogma'?
  • A: The flow of genetic information from DNA to RNA to Protein.
  • Q: Why is the Duckbill Platypus considered a connecting link?
  • A: It lays eggs like reptiles but has mammary glands and hair like mammals.
  • Q: What is the main objection to Darwin's theory?
  • A: It does not explain the origin of useful and useless modifications or the difference between slow and abrupt changes.

8. Common Errors and How to Avoid Them

  • Confusing Transcription and Translation: Remember: Transcription is DNA → RNA (in the nucleus); Translation is RNA → Protein (in the cytoplasm).
  • Misunderstanding Lamarckism: Students often think Lamarckism is correct. Remember it was disproved because acquired characters (like strong muscles from exercise) are not inherited.
  • Forgetting the "Connecting Link" examples: Ensure you correctly pair Peripatus with Annelida/Arthropoda and Duckbill Platypus with Reptiles/Mammals.

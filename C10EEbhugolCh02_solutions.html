1. Chapter at a Glance

Main topic

Chapter 2 compares the location, extent, neighbouring countries, historical background and government systems of India and Brazil.

India

India is in the Northern and Eastern Hemispheres, in the southern part of Asia. Its capital is New Delhi.

Brazil

Brazil lies in both the Northern and Southern Hemispheres, mostly in the Southern Hemisphere, and in the Western Hemisphere. It is in the northern part of South America. Its capital is Brasilia.

Geographical skills

The chapter asks students to use political maps and a world outline map to identify countries, oceans, hemispheres, latitudinal and longitudinal extent, the Equator, and directions.

2. India — Location and Extent

Location

India is located in the Northern and Eastern Hemispheres, in the southern part of the Asian continent.

Capital

New Delhi.

Mainland extent given in the chapter

Latitude: 8°4′ N to 37°6′ N. Longitude: 68°7′ E to 97°25′ E.

Southernmost point

The chapter states that Indira Point is the southernmost tip of India and is located at 6°45′ N. This is the southernmost point of India, including its islands; the mainland extent begins at 8°4′ N.

Surrounding water bodies shown on the map

Arabian Sea to the west, Bay of Bengal to the east, and Indian Ocean to the south.

Neighbouring countries shown or named on the map

Pakistan, Afghanistan, China, Nepal, Bhutan, Bangladesh and Myanmar; Sri Lanka lies across the sea to the south. The map also shows other nearby island territories and boundaries.

3. Brazil — Location and Extent

Location

Some part of Brazil lies in the Northern Hemisphere, while most lies in the Southern Hemisphere. Brazil is in the Western Hemisphere and in the northern part of the South American continent.

Capital

Brasilia.

Extent given in the chapter

Latitude: 5°15′ N to 33°45′ S. Longitude: 34°45′ W to 73°48′ W.

Neighbouring countries shown on the map

The map labels neighbouring countries including Venezuela, Guyana, Suriname, French Guiana, Colombia, Peru, Bolivia, Paraguay, Argentina and Uruguay. Ecuador and Chile do not share a border with Brazil.

Ocean

Brazil has a coastline along the Atlantic Ocean, not the Pacific Ocean.

Name origin

The textbook states that the name ‘Brazil’ comes from ‘Pau Brasil’, a local wooded tree.

4. Key Terms

Latitude

An angular measure used to locate a place north or south of the Equator, expressed in degrees North (N) or South (S).

Longitude

An angular measure used to locate a place east or west of the Prime Meridian, expressed in degrees East (E) or West (W).

Latitudinal extent

The north-to-south range of latitude covered by a country.

Longitudinal extent

The east-to-west range of longitude covered by a country.

Equator

The 0° latitude line. It passes through the northern part of Brazil but does not pass through India.

Tropic of Cancer

The latitude line at about 23½° N. The textbook map shows it crossing India.

Tropic of Capricorn

The latitude line at about 23½° S. The textbook map shows it crossing Brazil.

Hemisphere

One half of the Earth, such as the Northern/Southern or Eastern/Western Hemisphere.

Peninsula

A piece of land surrounded by water on three sides. The chapter describes the southern part of India as a peninsula.

Republic

The chapter states that both India and Brazil are republics, though their government systems differ.

5. Historical Background and Government

India

The textbook states that India was under British rule for almost one-and-a-half centuries and gained independence in 1947. It mentions challenges after independence, including three wars and famine situations. It describes India as a major developing country and global market, and calls it a young country because the proportion of youth in its population is high.

Brazil

The textbook states that Brazil was under Portuguese rule for more than three centuries and gained independence in 1822. It says Brazil was under a populist military government from 1930 to 1985 and faced global financial difficulties in the late twentieth century.

Government systems

The textbook’s ‘Do you know?’ box states that India has a federal parliamentary republic system, whereas Brazil has a federal presidential republic system.

Independence days

According to the textbook, India celebrates Independence Day on 15 August and Brazil on 7 September.

6. India and Brazil — Comparison

Location

India: southern part of Asia; Northern and Eastern Hemispheres. Brazil: northern part of South America; Western Hemisphere, crossing the Equator and lying mostly south of it.

Capital

India: New Delhi. Brazil: Brasilia.

Independence

India: 1947 from British rule. Brazil: 1822 from Portuguese rule.

Government system as stated in the chapter

India: federal parliamentary republic. Brazil: federal presidential republic.

Ocean/coast

India is bordered by the Arabian Sea and Bay of Bengal, with the Indian Ocean to the south. Brazil has a coastline along the Atlantic Ocean.

Country size

The chapter asks students to compare the countries on the world map. Brazil is larger in area than India.

Extent

Use the textbook map and stated coordinates to compare latitude and longitude ranges. Brazil has a greater north–south latitudinal span than mainland India.

7. Solved Textbook Exercise — Q1: Right or Wrong

(a) Brazil is mainly located in the Southern Hemisphere.

Right.

(b) Tropic of Capricorn passes through the middle of India.

Wrong. The Tropic of Cancer passes through India.

(c) The longitudinal extent of Brazil is less than India.

Wrong. From the coordinates given in the chapter, Brazil has a greater longitudinal span than India.

(d) Equator passes through the northern part of Brazil.

Right.

(e) Brazil has a coastline along the Pacific Ocean.

Wrong. Brazil has a coastline along the Atlantic Ocean.

(f) Pakistan is a neighbouring country to the south-east of India.

Wrong. Pakistan lies to the north-west of India.

(g) The southern part of India is called Peninsula.

Right, as stated in the textbook exercise.

8. Solved Textbook Exercise — Q2: Answer in Brief

(a) What problems did Brazil and India face after independence?

India faced problems including three wars, famine situations in different parts of the country and other challenges. Brazil experienced global financial difficulties in the late twentieth century; the textbook also describes a long period of populist military government from 1930 to 1985.

(b) How are Brazil and India different in terms of location?

India lies in the Northern and Eastern Hemispheres, in the southern part of Asia. Brazil lies in the Western Hemisphere, in the northern part of South America, and extends across the Equator; most of Brazil lies in the Southern Hemisphere.

(c) Describe the latitudinal and longitudinal extent of India and Brazil.

India (mainland): 8°4′ N to 37°6′ N latitude and 68°7′ E to 97°25′ E longitude. Brazil: 5°15′ N to 33°45′ S latitude and 34°45′ W to 73°48′ W longitude.

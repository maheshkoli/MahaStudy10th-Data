Chapter 7: Mensuration

1. Chapter at a Glance

This chapter focuses on the surface area and volume of various solid figures. It builds upon previous knowledge of three-dimensional shapes and introduces the concept of the frustum of a cone. The chapter also covers the geometry of circles, specifically the length of arcs, the area of sectors, and the area of segments of a circle.

Main Topics:

  • Surface area and volume of solid figures (cuboid, cube, cylinder, cone, sphere, hemisphere).
  • Frustum of a cone (volume and surface area).
  • Arc of a circle (length of arc).
  • Area of a sector.
  • Area of a segment of a circle.

2. Important Concepts Explained

Three-Dimensional Figures

The textbook provides a review of standard three-dimensional figures:

  • Cuboid: Defined by length ($l$), breadth ($b$), and height ($h$).
  • Cube: Defined by side length ($l$).
  • Cylinder: Defined by radius ($r$) and height ($h$).
  • Cone: Defined by radius ($r$), height ($h$), and slant height ($l = \sqrt{h^2 + r^2}$).
  • Sphere: Defined by radius ($r$).
  • Hemisphere: Defined by radius ($r$).

Frustum of a Cone

When a cone is cut by a plane parallel to its base, the part containing the base is called a frustum of a cone.

  • It has two circular faces with radii $r_1$ and $r_2$ ($r_1 > r_2$).
  • It has a height $h$ and a slant height $l$.

Sector of a Circle

A sector is a part of a circle enclosed by two radii and the arc joining their end points.

  • Minor Sector: Enclosed by two radii and the corresponding minor arc.
  • Major Sector: Enclosed by two radii and the corresponding major arc.

Segment of a Circle

A segment is the region bounded by a chord and its corresponding arc.

  • Minor Segment: Area enclosed by a chord and its corresponding minor arc.
  • Major Segment: Area enclosed by a chord and its corresponding major arc.

3. Definitions and Key Terms

  • Lateral Surface Area: The area of the vertical surfaces of a solid.
  • Total Surface Area: The sum of the areas of all faces of a solid.
  • Slant Height ($l$): The distance from the apex of a cone to any point on the circumference of its base.
  • Frustum: The portion of a cone that remains after its top is cut off by a plane parallel to the base.
  • Central Angle ($\theta$): An angle whose vertex is the centre of the circle.

Frustum of a Cone Formulas:

  • Slant height ($l$): $\sqrt{h^2 + (r_1 - r_2)^2}$
  • Curved Surface Area: $\pi l(r_1 + r_2)$
  • Total Surface Area: $\pi l(r_1 + r_2) + \pi r_1^2 + \pi r_2^2$
  • Volume: $\frac{1}{3}\pi h(r_1^2 + r_2^2 + r_1r_2)$

Circle Geometry Formulas:

  • Area of a Sector: $A = \frac{\theta}{360} \times \pi r^2$
  • Length of an Arc: $l = \frac{\theta}{360} \times 2\pi r$
  • Relation between Arc and Sector: $A = \frac{l \times r}{2}$
  • Area of a Segment: $A(\text{segment}) = A(\text{sector}) - A(\text{triangle}) = r^2 \left[ \frac{\pi\theta}{360} - \frac{\sin\theta}{2} \right]$

5. Worked Examples — Step by Step

Ex (1): Cylindrical Reservoir

  • Given: $r = 2.8$ m, $h = 3.5$ m, daily requirement per person = 70 litres.
  • Step 1: Calculate volume $V = \pi r^2h = \frac{22}{7} \times 2.8 \times 2.8 \times 3.5 = 86.24$ m$^3$.
  • Step 2: Convert to litres ($1$ m$^3 = 1000$ litres): $86.24 \times 1000 = 86240$ litres.
  • Step 3: Divide by daily requirement: $86240 / 70 = 1232$ persons.
  • Final Answer: The reservoir holds 86240 litres, sufficient for 1232 persons.

Ex (2): Melting Sphere into Cylinders

  • Given: Sphere $r = 30$ cm; Cylinder $R = 10$ cm, $H = 6$ cm.
  • Step 1: Volume of sphere = $\frac{4}{3} \pi (30)^3$.
  • Step 2: Volume of one cylinder = $\pi (10)^2 \times 6$.
  • Step 3: Number of cylinders $n = \frac{\text{Volume of sphere}}{\text{Volume of cylinder}} = \frac{\frac{4}{3} \pi (30)^3}{\pi (10)^2 \times 6} = \frac{4 \times 27000}{3 \times 100 \times 6} = 60$.
  • Final Answer: 60 cylinders can be made.

Ex (3): Circus Tent

  • Given: Diameter = 48 m ($r = 24$ m), Cylindrical height $H = 15$ m, Total height = 33 m.
  • Step 1: Conical height $h = 33 - 15 = 18$ m.
  • Step 2: Slant height $l = \sqrt{18^2 + 24^2} = \sqrt{324 + 576} = 30$ m.
  • Step 3: Canvas area = $2\pi rH + \pi rl = \pi r(2H + l) = \frac{22}{7} \times 24 \times (30 + 30) = 4525.71$ m$^2$.
  • Step 4: Volume = $\pi r^2H + \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2h = \pi r^2(H + \frac{h}{3}) = \frac{22}{7} \times 24^2 \times (15 + 6) = 38016$ m$^3$.
  • Final Answer: Canvas area = 4525.71 m$^2$, Volume = 38016 m$^3$.

7. Important Questions and Answers

  • Q: What is the relationship between the volume of a cylinder and a sphere of the same radius and height?
  • A: The volume of a cylinder with height $2r$ and radius $r$ is $2\pi r^3$. The volume of a sphere with radius $r$ is $\frac{4}{3}\pi r^3$. The ratio is $3:2$.
  • Q: How does the slant height of a frustum differ from the height?
  • A: The height ($h$) is the perpendicular distance between the two circular bases, while the slant height ($l$) is the distance along the side, calculated as $\sqrt{h^2 + (r_1-r_2)^2}$.

8. Common Errors and How to Avoid Them

  • Units: Always ensure all dimensions are in the same unit (e.g., cm, m) before calculating. Convert litres to cm$^3$ ($1$ litre $= 1000$ cm$^3$) when dealing with volume.
  • Radius vs Diameter: Always check if the problem gives the radius or the diameter. If diameter is given, divide by 2.
  • Formula Confusion: Ensure you are using the correct formula for the specific solid (e.g., lateral vs total surface area).

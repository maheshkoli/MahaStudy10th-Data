2. Energy Transfer, Electrical Power and Heating

When charge Q moves between two points with potential difference V, the work done is VQ. A cell supplies this energy. In a resistor, electrical energy is converted into heat.

Important formulas

Electrical power: P = Energy / time = V × I
P = VI = I²R = V²/R
Heat produced: H = P × t = VIt = I²Rt = (V²/R)t

Joule’s law of heating: The heat produced in a resistor is proportional to the square of the current, the resistance and the time for which current flows.

Electricity-bill unit: 1 kilowatt-hour (1 kWh) = 1000 W × 3600 s = 3.6 × 10⁶ J. One kWh is commonly called one unit of electrical energy.

Heating appliances

Electric cookers, water boilers, heaters and irons use the heating effect of current. Nichrome is used in heating coils because it has relatively high resistivity and withstands high temperatures. A tungsten filament in a bulb becomes very hot and emits light.

3. Fuse, Short Circuit and Overloading

  • Short circuit: A fault, such as damaged insulation, causes live and neutral wires to touch. A very large current can flow and generate dangerous heat.
  • Fuse: A safety wire that melts when excessive current flows, breaking the circuit.
  • MCB: A miniature circuit breaker automatically opens the circuit when current rises abnormally.
  • Overloading: Excessive current is drawn from a supply, for example when many appliances operate together beyond the circuit’s capacity.
  • Earthing: The earth wire provides a safe path for fault current in properly earthed equipment.
Electrical safety: never touch exposed wires or attempt repairs on a live circuit. Switch off the supply and use a qualified electrician for electrical faults.

4. Magnetic Effect of Electric Current

A current-carrying conductor produces a magnetic field around it. A magnetic needle near the wire deflects when current flows. The field becomes stronger when current increases and weaker as distance from the wire increases.

Right-hand thumb rule

Imagine holding a straight conductor in the right hand. Point the thumb in the direction of conventional current; the curled fingers show the direction of the magnetic field lines around the conductor.

Magnetic field of a circular loop and solenoid

  • Every part of a current-carrying circular loop contributes to the magnetic field at the centre; the contributions point in the same direction.
  • A solenoid is a long coil made by winding insulated wire in many turns. Its magnetic field resembles that of a bar magnet.
  • One end of the solenoid acts as a north pole and the other as a south pole. The field inside an ideal solenoid is approximately uniform, represented by parallel field lines.

Force on a current-carrying conductor

A conductor carrying current in a magnetic field experiences a force. The force direction depends on both the current direction and the magnetic-field direction. Reversing either one reverses the force; the force is greatest when current is perpendicular to the field.

Fleming’s left-hand rule

Stretch the thumb, forefinger and middle finger of the left hand so that they are mutually perpendicular. Forefinger indicates magnetic field, middle finger indicates current, and thumb indicates force/motion. Memory aid: left hand = motor force.

5. Electric Motor

An electric motor converts electrical energy into mechanical energy. The textbook model contains a rectangular coil between the north and south poles of a magnet, an axle, a split-ring commutator and carbon brushes.

  1. When the circuit is completed, current flows through the coil.
  2. The two opposite sides of the coil experience forces in opposite directions because their currents flow in opposite directions within the magnetic field.
  3. These forces turn the coil and axle.
  4. After each half-turn, the split ring reverses the current in the coil. This keeps the torque acting in the direction that continues the rotation.
Role of the split ring: It reverses the current in the motor coil after every half rotation so that the coil continues rotating in the same direction.

Uses include fans, refrigerators, mixers, washing machines, pumps and other appliances.

6. Electromagnetic Induction

Michael Faraday showed that current can be produced in a conductor when the magnetic field passing through it changes. This is called electromagnetic induction; the produced current is called induced current.

  • Moving a magnet relative to a coil can cause a galvanometer deflection.
  • Switching a nearby solenoid current on or off, changing its current, or moving it relative to a coil can induce current.
  • Faster relative movement generally produces a larger galvanometer deflection in the textbook experiment.

Fleming’s right-hand rule

Stretch the thumb, forefinger and middle finger of the right hand at right angles. Thumb indicates the motion of the conductor, forefinger the magnetic field, and middle finger the induced current. Memory aid: right hand = generator current.

7. Alternating Current, Direct Current and Electric Generator

Direct current (DC)Alternating current (AC)
Flows in one direction. Its magnitude may be steady, increasing or decreasing.Changes direction periodically and varies in magnitude.
A DC generator uses a split-ring commutator in the textbook model.An AC generator uses two conducting slip rings.
Commonly supplied by cells/batteries.Household supply described in the textbook is AC.

The textbook states that the frequency of AC supply in India is 50 Hz (50 cycles per second).

Electric generator: construction and working

A generator converts mechanical energy into electrical energy. A coil rotates between the poles of a magnet. Its ends connect to conducting rings and carbon brushes in an AC generator. As the coil rotates, the magnetic flux through it changes and current is induced. After each half-turn, the induced current in the external circuit reverses, producing AC.

To produce DC in the external circuit, a split-ring arrangement is used so the external current maintains the same direction.

Example 2 — Find potential difference

Given: R = 9 Ω and heat produced per second = 400 J/s, so P = 400 W.

P = V²/R ⇒ V² = PR = 400 × 9 = 3600
V = √3600 = 60 V

Answer: 60 V.

Example 3 — Electric iron at two settings

Given: V = 220 V, P₁ = 1100 W, P₂ = 330 W.

I₁ = P₁/V = 1100/220 = 5 A; R₁ = V/I₁ = 44 Ω
I₂ = P₂/V = 330/220 = 1.5 A; R₂ = V/I₂ = 220/1.5 ≈ 146.7 Ω

Example 4 — Bulb power and energy

Given: V = 220 V, I = 0.45 A, time = 10 h.

P = VI = 220 × 0.45 = 99 W
Energy = 99 W × 10 h = 990 Wh = 0.99 kWh

Answer: 99 W and 0.99 units of electricity.

9. Textbook Exercise — Answers

Questions follow the exercise printed on textbook pages 60–61. For diagram-based questions, the diagram’s meaning is identified in words.

Q1. Tell the odd one out and explain.

a. Fuse wire, bad conductor, rubber gloves, generator.
Answer: Generator. The other items are associated with electrical safety or insulation in the context of the question.

b. Voltmeter, ammeter, galvanometer, thermometer.
Answer: Thermometer. The other three are electrical measuring instruments.

c. Loud speaker, microphone, electric motor, magnet.
Answer: Magnet. The other three are devices; the magnet is a component/material rather than a complete device in this list.

Q2. Explain the construction and working of the following. Draw and label diagrams.

a. Electric motor

Answer: A rectangular coil is placed between the poles of a magnet. Its ends connect to a split-ring commutator on an axle. Carbon brushes connect the commutator to the battery. When current flows, opposite sides of the coil experience forces in opposite directions, making the coil rotate. The split ring reverses the current after every half-turn, so rotation continues in the same direction.

b. AC electric generator

Answer: A coil ABCD rotates between the poles of a magnet. The coil ends connect to two conducting slip rings mounted on the axle, and carbon brushes connect the rings to the external circuit. Rotation changes the magnetic flux through the coil and induces current. The current reverses after each half-turn, producing AC.

Q3. Electromagnetic induction means—

Correct option: (c) Generation of a current in a coil due to relative motion between the coil and the magnet.

Q6. How does a short circuit form? What is its effect?

Answer: A short circuit can occur when live and neutral wires touch because of damaged insulation or an appliance fault. The resistance becomes very low and a large current may flow, producing intense heat, damaging equipment and potentially causing fire. A fuse or MCB helps disconnect the circuit.

Q7. Give scientific reasons.

a. Tungsten is used to make the coil/filament in an electric bulb.
Answer: Tungsten has a very high melting point, so its filament can become extremely hot and emit light without melting under normal operating conditions.

b. Nichrome is used in heating equipment instead of pure metals.
Answer: Nichrome has high resistivity and can withstand high temperatures; it is suitable for converting electrical energy into heat.

c. Copper or aluminium wire is used for electric power transmission.
Answer: These metals have low resistivity and conduct electricity well, reducing resistive power losses.

d. kWh is used to measure electrical energy rather than joule in practice.
Answer: The joule is a small unit for household consumption. The kilowatt-hour is more convenient; 1 kWh = 3.6 × 10⁶ J.

Q8. Which statement correctly describes the magnetic field near a long, straight current-carrying conductor?

Correct option: (d). Magnetic field lines are concentric circles centred on the wire, in a plane perpendicular to the conductor.

Q9. What is a solenoid? Compare its magnetic field with that of a bar magnet.

Answer: A solenoid is a coil of insulated wire wound in many turns. Its field resembles that of a bar magnet: one end behaves as a north pole and the other as a south pole. Inside the solenoid, the field lines are approximately parallel, indicating a nearly uniform field.

Q10. Name the diagrams and explain the concept behind them.

a. Fleming’s left-hand rule: Used to determine the direction of force on a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field. Left-hand thumb = force, forefinger = magnetic field, middle finger = current.

b. Fleming’s right-hand rule: Used to determine the direction of induced current when a conductor moves in a magnetic field. Right-hand thumb = motion, forefinger = magnetic field, middle finger = induced current.

Q11. Identify the figures and explain their use.

a. Fuse wire: A safety device that melts when excessive current flows, opening the circuit and helping prevent damage or fire.

b. MCB (miniature circuit breaker): An automatic switch that trips and breaks the circuit when excessive current or a fault occurs.

Q12. Solve the numerical examples.

a. Heat is produced at 100 W and current is 3 A. Find resistance.

P = I²R ⇒ R = P/I² = 100/3² = 100/9 ≈ 11.1 Ω

Answer: approximately 11 Ω.

b. 100 W and 60 W bulbs are connected in parallel across 220 V. Find total current.

I₁ = P₁/V = 100/220 ≈ 0.455 A
I₂ = P₂/V = 60/220 ≈ 0.273 A
I(total) = I₁ + I₂ ≈ 0.728 A ≈ 0.72 A

Answer: approximately 0.72 A.

c. Which uses more energy: a 500 W TV for 30 minutes or a 600 W heater for 20 minutes?

TV: 500 W × 0.5 h = 250 Wh
Heater: 600 W × (20/60) h = 200 Wh

Answer: TV set.

d. An 1100 W iron runs 2 hours daily in April (30 days). Cost is ₹5 per unit. Find the cost.

Energy = 1.1 kW × 2 h/day × 30 days = 66 kWh (units)
Cost = 66 × ₹5 = ₹330

Answer: ₹330.

10. Quick Revision Sheet

  • Power: P = VI = I²R = V²/R.
  • Joule’s law: H = I²Rt.
  • Energy unit: 1 kWh = 3.6 × 10⁶ J.
  • Right-hand thumb rule: thumb = current; curled fingers = magnetic field.
  • Fleming’s left hand: thumb = force, forefinger = field, middle finger = current.
  • Fleming’s right hand: thumb = motion, forefinger = field, middle finger = induced current.
  • Motor: electrical energy → mechanical energy.
  • Generator: mechanical energy → electrical energy.
  • AC: periodically reverses direction; textbook states 50 Hz for India.
  • DC: flows in one direction.
  • Fuse/MCB: disconnect the circuit when current is too high.

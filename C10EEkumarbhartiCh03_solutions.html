2. Questions from the Text

Why is Air Force Marshal Arjan Singh considered an outstanding military personality?
He was a fearless and exceptional pilot who flew more than 60 types of aircraft. He led the Indian Air Force during the 1965 Indo-Pak war, helped strengthen the IAF and was the only IAF officer to receive the five-star rank of Marshal of the Air Force.

What personality traits of Arjan Singh are mentioned?
He was fearless, knowledgeable, hardworking, disciplined, calm, efficient, firm and a capable leader. He was also described as a man of few words.

What enabled Arjan Singh to receive the Distinguished Flying Cross (DFC)?
His leadership and successful combat service, including leading a squadron during the Arakan and Imphal campaigns, earned him the DFC in 1944. The textbook states that he was the first Indian pilot to receive it.

At what age did Arjan Singh become a Squadron Leader?
He was born on 15 April 1919 and promoted to Squadron Leader in 1944, when he was about 25 years old.

For how long did Arjan Singh hold the post of AOC of an operational base?
The textbook gives two periods: 1949–1952 and 1957–1961.

What uphill task did Singh face when he took over as Air Marshal?
He took charge of the IAF on 1 August 1964 when it was still rebuilding and preparing to meet new challenges.

What other important positions did Arjan Singh hold after retirement?
He served as India’s Ambassador to Switzerland and as Lieutenant Governor of Delhi from December 1989 to December 1990.

4. Actions that preceded the promotions / honours

  1. Selected for Empire Pilot training at RAF: He was a promising young pilot and was selected for the course at RAF Cranwell in 1938, at age 19.
  2. Promoted to Squadron Leader: He served as a pilot, flew operational missions and led a squadron in combat during the Arakan and Imphal campaigns.
  3. Led a fly-past of over 100 aircraft over the Red Fort: On 15 August 1947, he led the ceremonial fly-past over the Red Fort in Delhi.
  4. Awarded Padma Vibhushan: He was honoured for his astute leadership of the IAF during the 1965 war.
  5. Became the first Air Chief Marshal of the Indian Air Force: The text says the rank of Chief of the Air Staff was upgraded in recognition of the Air Force’s contribution during the 1965 war.

8. Notes from the text

  • Arjan Singh — early life: born 15 Apr 1919, Lyalpur; educated at Montgomery; selected for RAF Cranwell pilot course in 1938 (age 19).
  • Early service: No. 1 RIAF Squadron; Westland Wapiti biplanes; Hawker Hurricane; Squadron Leader in 1944.
  • Wartime service: Arakan and Imphal campaigns; DFC in 1944; led 100+ aircraft fly-past over Red Fort on 15 Aug 1947.
  • Senior appointments: Wing Commander; Group Captain at Ambala; Air Commodore in 1949; Air Vice Marshal; Deputy Chief and Vice Chief of Air Staff; Air Marshal in 1964.
  • 1965 war: led IAF with courage, determination and skill; received Padma Vibhushan; became first Air Chief Marshal after CAS rank upgrade.
  • Retirement and recognition: retired Aug 1969; Ambassador to Switzerland; Lieutenant Governor of Delhi (Dec 1989–Dec 1990); Marshal of the Air Force in Jan 2002.

9. Story ending with Sanyogita More receiving the National Bravery Award

The Courageous Little Girl

One afternoon, ten-year-old Sanyogita More was returning home when she noticed smoke coming from a nearby house. People were shouting that a small child was trapped inside. Although she was frightened, Sanyogita immediately called adults for help and told someone to contact the fire brigade. She saw the child near a doorway and, keeping low to avoid the thick smoke, guided the child towards an open exit with the help of a neighbour. She did not enter deeper into the burning house or try to fight the fire herself. Soon the fire brigade arrived and secured the area. The child was rescued safely, and Sanyogita’s quick thinking helped everyone respond in time. Her brave and sensible action was reported to the authorities. And so, with tears of joy and pride, the 10-year-old Sanyogita More received the National Bravery Award from the Prime Minister.

This is a model creative story written to lead into the exact ending printed in the textbook. Students may create a different plausible story.

1. Chapter Overview

“A Teenager’s Prayer” is a poem by J. Morse. The speaker is a teenager who understands that every new day brings new beginnings and decisions. The poem is written as a prayer asking God for guidance, strength, moral clarity and the ability to resist temptation.

The speaker recognizes that the choice of life’s path is his/her own. One path leads towards success, while the “darkened road” represents wrong or evil choices and leads to distress. The prayer asks God to help the teenager stand for what is right, bring out the best in him/her, and keep the body clean and fit. fileciteturn170file0L32-L52

Central idea: Teenage life involves important choices. A young person should make responsible decisions, resist temptation, stand for what is right and seek guidance to live a meaningful life.

2. Poem — Stanza-wise Explanation

Stanza 1 — New beginnings and choices

Each day gives the teenager a fresh beginning. With every new day, there are decisions to make. The speaker understands that he/she is responsible for choosing the road to follow in life.

Stanza 2 — Two roads

The speaker sees two possible paths. The road of life can lead to great success, while the darkened road symbolizes a wrong or evil path that can lead to great distress.

Stanza 3 — Prayer for moral strength

The teenager asks God to “open up my eyes” so that the difference between right and wrong can be understood clearly. The speaker also asks for strength to stand for what is right and to bring out the best qualities within.

Stanza 4 — Resisting temptation

The speaker asks God for the strength to say “no” when temptation appears. The aim is to keep the body clean and fit and to maintain self-discipline.

Stanza 5 — Looking towards adulthood

At the end of the teenage years, the speaker hopes to realize that life has been lived at its very best. The speaker wants God to remain a companion throughout the journey of life.

4. Chit-Chat / Warming Up

When you have to take important decisions, what do you generally do?

Model response: I usually think carefully about the situation, consider the consequences, and discuss important matters with my parents or elders. When appropriate, I may also ask a teacher or trusted friend for advice. The final decision should be made responsibly rather than by chance.

5. Textbook Questions and Answers

1. What is the age-group of the speaker in the poem?

The speaker belongs to the teenage age group.

2. What decisions does the speaker have to take?

The speaker has to decide which path to choose in life — a path leading towards success or a wrong path leading towards distress.

3. What does each new day fetch?

Each new day brings new beginnings and new decisions to make. fileciteturn170file0L33-L40

4. How should one react to temptation?

One should have the strength to say “no” to temptation and avoid actions that are wrong or harmful. fileciteturn170file0L45-L48

5. What effect does the speaker wish to see in himself at the end of his teens?

The speaker wishes to see that he has lived his life to the very best during his teenage years. fileciteturn170file0L49-L52

6. In what condition does the poet wish to maintain his body?

He wishes to keep his body clean and fit for life each day.

7. Whose company does the poet wish for in the walk of life?

The poet wishes for God’s company and guidance — with God “walking next to” him through life.

11. Appreciation of the Poem

Title: A Teenager’s Prayer

Poet: J. Morse

Rhyme Scheme: abcb

Favourite Line: “Help me stand for what is right, / Bring out the best in me.”

Theme / Central Idea: The poem expresses a teenager’s prayer for guidance, moral strength, wise choices and the ability to resist temptation.

Figures of Speech: Apostrophe, Inversion, Metaphor and Alliteration.

Special Features: The poem is a sincere and reflective prayer. Its language is simple and meaningful. The road/journey imagery makes the idea of life choices easy to understand.

Tone: Sincere, thoughtful, prayerful and hopeful.

Why I like the poem: I like the poem because it reminds teenagers that their choices matter and encourages them to stand for what is right.

The textbook specifically asks for a 12–15 sentence appreciation covering title, poet, rhyme scheme, favourite line, theme, figures of speech, special features and personal response. fileciteturn170file0L136-L147

12. Short Prayer for Teenage Years

Dear God,
Guide me when I have to choose,
Give me strength when I am weak,
Help me stand for what is right,
And give me courage when I speak.
When temptation comes my way,
Help me calmly say “No”.
Keep my heart and body clean,
And guide me wherever I go.
Walk beside me through my years,
Help me learn from every day,
So that when my teenage years end,
I can look back and say,
“I lived my life with care and truth,
And always chose the better way.”

1. Lesson at a Glance

The lesson “Unbeatable Super Mom — Mary Kom” presents an interview with Indian boxer Mary Kom. It describes her early life in rural Manipur, her interest in athletics and boxing, her achievements in national and international competitions, and her experience at the 2012 Olympics. The interview also discusses training, nutrition, sponsorship and her life as a mother.

Full name in the text: Chungneijang Mary Kom. Birth date: 24 November 1982. Sport: Boxing. Olympic achievement described: Bronze medal at the 2012 London Olympics in the 51 kg flyweight category.

8. Answer in your own words

(a) What makes the writer call Mary Kom a legend?
She is described as a five-time World Amateur Boxing Champion and as the only boxer to win a medal in each of the six world championships mentioned in the lesson. Her achievements and determination make her a role model.

(b) What prime quality did she display during her first Olympics attempt? What was her bad luck?
She displayed confidence and strong preparation. She believed that some judging decisions in the semi-final were unfortunate and did not work in her favour.

(c) What does she suggest so India can win more Olympic gold medals?
She suggests that more people should take up sports as a full-time career and that companies should sponsor athletes so they can focus on training without financial pressure.

16. Short paragraph: The life and work of Mary Kom

Mary Kom, whose full name is Chungneijang Mary Kom, was born on 24 November 1982 in Manipur. She came from a poor family and helped her parents with farm-related work. Inspired by boxer Dingko Singh, she took up boxing and worked hard to improve her skills. She won the Manipur State women’s boxing championship and later competed internationally. She became a five-time World Amateur Boxing Champion and won a bronze medal at the 2012 Olympics in the 51 kg category. During her preparation, she trained in Pune, sparred with male boxers and followed a healthy diet. Mary Kom is admired for her determination, confidence and humility. She is also proud of being a mother and has expressed interest in possibly coaching Indian women boxers in the future.

17. “What men can do, women can do better” — views and counterviews

For the topic

Women have proved their abilities in science, education, sports, defence, business and public service. Mary Kom is an example of a woman who achieved success in a sport often associated with men. Talent, training and determination are not limited by gender. Equal opportunities help everyone develop their abilities and contribute to society.

Against the topic

It is not necessary to compare men and women or claim that one gender is better than the other. People have different strengths and abilities. Success depends on opportunity, training, support and individual effort. Men and women should be judged by their achievements and treated with equal respect.

18. Paragraphs on proverbs

“Actions speak louder than words.”

Mary Kom’s life reflects the proverb “Actions speak louder than words.” Instead of only talking about her ambitions, she trained hard and took part in competitions. Her achievements, including the Olympic bronze medal described in the lesson, demonstrate the results of dedication and effort. Her story teaches us that consistent action is more powerful than promises.

“Fortune favours the brave.”

The proverb “Fortune favours the brave” suits Mary Kom because she had the courage to enter boxing and continue despite obstacles. She came from humble surroundings, trained seriously and competed at the highest level. Her confidence and willingness to face challenges helped her make progress. Her journey encourages young people to be brave, prepared and persistent.

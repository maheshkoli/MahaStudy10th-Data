1. Chapter Overview

“Book Review — Swami and Friends” introduces students to the art of reviewing a book. The lesson uses R. K. Narayan's Swami and Friends as its main example and discusses the central character, setting, language, literary value and appeal of the book.

The review deliberately avoids revealing the plot in detail so that readers are not given “spoilers”. It presents Swami as a mischievous ten-year-old boy living in India during British rule. He dislikes school, especially Mathematics, gets into trouble and is frequently punished by his father. fileciteturn179file0L41-L66

Central learning: A good book review should identify the book, author, type/background, important characters or ideas, language, strengths, weaknesses, personal opinion and conclusion without unnecessarily revealing the story.

3. Understanding the Review

What is the most striking quality of ten-year-old Swami?

Swami's most striking quality is his mischievous and talkative nature. He is restless, dislikes school and Mathematics, loves playing and getting into adventures, and often tries to talk himself out of trouble. At the same time, the review presents him as loving, tender and sensitive.

In what ways is Swami a typical boy?

Swami behaves like many children of his age. He wants to play outdoors, climb trees, kick things, break things and trouble others. He dislikes staying in one place and finds school, especially Mathematics, unpleasant. His curiosity, restlessness and mischief make him recognisable as a typical growing child. fileciteturn179file0L48-L66

What does the review say about R. K. Narayan's style and language?

The review says that Narayan writes in simple, plain English without unnecessary difficult words. He uses short and simple sentences. The writer also praises Narayan for entering deeply into the mind of ten-year-old Swami and explaining his thoughts and perspective clearly. fileciteturn179file0L69-L74 fileciteturn179file0L101-L127

What are the two advantages of reading this book?

  1. It can be enjoyed as an interesting read by children and teenagers.
  2. It can help readers improve their English skills because the language is simple and easy to understand.
The textbook concludes that the book is a good read for children of all ages, whether they want entertainment or want to improve their English. fileciteturn179file0L147-L150

  • spilled flour or food

  • biscuits
  • jam or sweets

What comes to mind when you hear “mischievous”?

  • playful
  • naughty but innocent
  • trouble others
  • pranks

Questions to gather more information

  1. When were the children last seen in the kitchen?
  2. What was missing from the jars?
  3. Why were the utensils scattered?
  4. Was anyone preparing food?
  5. Were the footprints made before or after the kitchen became messy?

Model Story — “The Secret Kitchen Mission”

Mother returned home and noticed children's footprints in the kitchen. Several utensils were scattered and some flour had spilled on the floor. She also discovered that jars of biscuits and jam were empty. Instead of immediately blaming the children, she asked what had happened. The children finally confessed that they had planned a surprise snack for her birthday. They had tried to prepare the food themselves but had made a terrible mess. Mother laughed at their mischievous attempt, helped them clean the kitchen and appreciated their effort. The children learned that a good intention should also be supported by careful planning and cleaning up afterwards.

8. Tips for Reading a Book Review

Skimming

  • Identify the title, text and content.
  • Select exactly what needs to be read.
  • Identify supporting resources and references.
  • Search for associated ideas when required.
  • Use technology where useful to correlate information.

Scanning

  • Look up meanings of unfamiliar words and phrases.
  • Find exact associations between words and ideas.
  • Check sequence and details.
  • Identify the exact order of ideas or events.
  • Use relevant technology and references where necessary.

10. Model Answers — Important Questions

Q. Why is Swami a typical boy in his growing years?

Swami is a typical boy because he is restless, playful, curious and mischievous. He dislikes remaining in one place and hates school, especially Mathematics. He prefers playing outdoors, climbing trees, kicking things, breaking things and troubling others. His behaviour reflects the ordinary curiosity and energy associated with childhood. At the same time, he is loving and sensitive, which gives his character greater depth.

Q. What are the important qualities of the book's language?

The review describes the language as plain and simple. It does not depend on difficult words or heavy technical jargon. Short sentences make it easy for young readers to follow. This simplicity allows the reader to understand Swami's thoughts and perspective without unnecessary linguistic difficulty. fileciteturn179file0L101-L114

Q. Explain the literary significance of “Swami and Friends”.

The review says that the book is not merely an entertaining read. It has literary value because Narayan presents the mind and perspective of a ten-year-old child in an engaging way. The book also captures aspects of India under British rule. Its simple language, humour, characterisation and child-centred viewpoint make it accessible while still giving it literary value.

Q. Write a character sketch of Swami.

Swami is a ten-year-old Indian boy living during British rule. He is mischievous, talkative, restless and playful. He dislikes school and especially Mathematics. He often gets into trouble and tries to talk his way out of difficult situations. Beneath his mischief, however, he is loving, tender and sensitive. The review's strength lies in showing both sides of his personality rather than presenting him simply as a naughty child.

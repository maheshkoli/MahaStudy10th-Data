1. Chapter Overview

“An Encounter of a Special Kind” is a narrative by Tapan Mukherjee. The narrator recalls an incident from his childhood in Raniganj, West Bengal. His father was a medical professional working for a private company, and the family lived in a large, nature-rich campus. The narrator's encounter with a baby langur and its mother becomes a memorable lesson in compassion, trust and communication between humans and animals. fileciteturn171file0L21-L43

Central idea: Compassion, trust and mutual understanding can create a bond between human beings and animals. Communication is not limited to words or food; emotions can also communicate care and gratitude.

2. Warming Up — Chit-Chat

What do you see in the picture?

A girl is carrying a vessel containing a cat while trying to move through a difficult situation. The picture suggests determination and a willingness to help or protect another living being.

What do you think the girl is trying to do? Why?

She appears to be trying to carry the animal safely to another place. She may be doing this because she wants to protect or rescue it.

If you were in the place of that girl, what would you do?

I would first make sure that the animal was safe and then seek help from an adult or an appropriate animal-care service if necessary.

Do you think she will become successful in her mission?

Yes, she may succeed if she remains careful, determined and takes appropriate help when needed.

3. Detailed Story Explanation

Setting

The family lived in individual bungalows on a large company campus in Raniganj, West Bengal. The campus had green grass, colourful flowers, tall trees, kitchen gardens, squirrels, many birds and a group of langurs living in an aswatha tree. fileciteturn171file0L22-L35

The quiet afternoon

During the Puja vacation, after a hearty lunch, the narrator's parents and younger sisters rested while he read a storybook. The quietness was broken by household and bird sounds. He became deeply absorbed in his book. fileciteturn171file0L39-L52

The disturbance

Street dogs began barking furiously. The barking became louder and more aggressive, while crows added to the noise. The disturbance came closer, so curiosity made the narrator leave his book and go to the veranda. fileciteturn171file0L53-L59

The attack on the baby langur

The narrator saw a large male langur, apparently the leader of the group, holding and biting a baby langur with the intention of killing it. The mother and other langurs watched from nearby roofs. The story explains the group custom described by the narrator: a dominant male usually does not allow another male baby or adult to survive within the group. fileciteturn171file0L93-L103

The rescue

The narrator acted immediately. He took a stout stick and threw stones at the male langur. The unexpected attack from an unknown direction forced the male langur to drop the baby. As the baby slid down the roof, the narrator kept the dogs at bay and caught the baby's tail just before it fell. fileciteturn171file0L104-L116

Reviving the baby

The narrator carried the baby to the backyard and placed it in a poultry coop. His father gave first aid, cleaned the wounds and stopped the bleeding. The baby was still breathing, though shallowly. Splashes of cold water helped it stir and sit up. The narrator then offered it a peeled banana. fileciteturn171file0L147-L171

The mother returns

The mother langur watched from the kitchen roof. When the baby sensed her presence, the narrator moved away from the coop so she could reach her child. She entered, inspected the injuries and cuddled the baby. The baby found comfort in her caring arms. fileciteturn171file0L172-L198

The special encounter

The mother looked directly into the narrator's eyes. He interpreted her look as silent gratitude for saving her child. The experience convinced him that interspecies communication and mutual trust are possible and that food is not the only way to establish a bond. Trust, compassion and mutual understanding can also create a connection. The narrator still remembered this encounter fifty-five years later. fileciteturn171file0L219-L245

10. Discussion — Injured Bird

What would you do if you found an injured bird while returning from school?

If I found an injured bird, I would first keep myself and the bird safe. I would avoid handling it unnecessarily and would call an adult for help. If possible, I would contact a veterinarian or an appropriate animal-rescue service. I would provide water only if advised and would keep the bird away from danger until professional help arrived.

What opinion do you form about the narrator?

The narrator appears to be courageous, compassionate, observant and quick to act. He did not remain a passive observer when the baby langur was in danger. He also showed sensitivity by allowing the mother to reach her child and by recognizing the significance of the mother’s response.

14. Paragraph — Birds and Animals

Title: Birds and Animals — Our Best Friends

Birds and animals are an important part of our world and often become our loyal companions. They show love and affection and can help reduce loneliness. Some animals serve human beings by providing protection and security. We should treat them with kindness, honesty and compassion. We should never hurt or destroy them unnecessarily. When an animal is injured, we should try to protect it and seek proper help. Respecting animals also teaches us empathy and responsibility.

Model News Report

Headline: Tiger attacks 8-year-old at Rajiv Gandhi National Park

Dateline: Bengaluru, Monday, 12 August — Staff Reporter

Lead: An eight-year-old child was attacked by a tiger at Rajiv Gandhi National Park on Monday, according to officials.

Body: The child was reportedly near the designated visitor area when the incident occurred. Immediate assistance was provided and the child was shifted for medical treatment. Park authorities secured the affected area and initiated an investigation into the incident.

Conclusion: Visitors were advised to follow safety instructions and remain within designated areas. Further information was expected from park authorities.

The date and location in this model are illustrative because the textbook provides the headline but does not supply an actual event date, so they should be replaced with verified details if this is being presented as a real news report.

16. Language Study — Present Participle

A present participle is the form of a verb ending in -ing. It can function as a participle/adjective or help form a continuous tense. The textbook gives “twinkling eyes” as an example and “started trembling” as an example of use in a continuous construction. fileciteturn171file0L399-L431

Present Participle UseUse in Continuous / Verb Construction

clinging babywas breathing
Exam tip: In “twinkling eyes”, twinkling describes the noun “eyes”. In “started trembling”, trembling is part of the verb construction. The distinction is based on how the -ing form functions in the sentence.

20. Long Answer — Model Answer

Explain the narrator’s rescue operation and the significance of his encounter with the mother langur.

The narrator heard a growing commotion and went to the veranda. He saw a dominant male langur attacking a baby langur. He immediately picked up a stout stick and threw stones at the male langur. The unexpected attack caused the male langur to drop the baby. The narrator kept the dogs away and caught the baby’s tail before it fell. He carried it to the backyard, where his father cleaned its wounds and gave first aid. Cold water helped the baby stir and sit up, and the narrator offered it a banana. Later, the mother langur came down after the narrator moved away from the coop. She examined her baby and cuddled it. When she looked into the narrator’s eyes, he felt that she was silently expressing gratitude. The experience taught him that trust, compassion and mutual understanding can create communication between humans and animals.

Chapter 2: Pythagoras Theorem

1. Chapter at a Glance

This chapter introduces the fundamental concepts of the Pythagoras Theorem and its applications in geometry. Students will learn about Pythagorean triplets, the properties of right-angled triangles, the theorem of geometric mean, the application of the Pythagoras theorem, and the Apollonius theorem.

2. Important Concepts Explained

Pythagoras Theorem

In a right-angled triangle, the square of the hypotenuse is equal to the sum of the squares of the remaining two sides.

For $\triangle PQR$ where $\angle PQR = 90^\circ$:

$PR^2 = PQ^2 + QR^2$

Pythagorean Triplet

In a triplet of natural numbers, if the square of the largest number is equal to the sum of the squares of the remaining two numbers, the triplet is called a Pythagorean triplet.

Example: $(11, 60, 61)$

$11^2 = 121$, $60^2 = 3600$, $61^2 = 3721$

$121 + 3600 = 3721$. Since $11^2 + 60^2 = 61^2$, it is a Pythagorean triplet.

Properties of Special Right-Angled Triangles

  1. 30°-60°-90° Triangle:
  • Side opposite to 30° angle = $\frac{1}{2} \times$ hypotenuse
  • Side opposite to 60° angle = $\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \times$ hypotenuse
  1. 45°-45°-90° Triangle:
  • Each perpendicular side = $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \times$ hypotenuse

Similarity and Right-Angled Triangles

If an altitude is drawn to the hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle, the two triangles formed are similar to the original triangle and to each other.

Theorem of Geometric Mean

In a right-angled triangle, the perpendicular segment to the hypotenuse from the opposite vertex is the geometric mean of the segments into which the hypotenuse is divided.

If $QS \perp PR$, then $QS^2 = PS \times SR$.

Converse of Pythagoras Theorem

In a triangle, if the square of one side is equal to the sum of the squares of the other two sides, then the triangle is a right-angled triangle.

Application of Pythagoras Theorem

  • Acute Angle: $AB^2 = BC^2 + AC^2 - 2BC \times DC$ (where $AD \perp BC$)
  • Obtuse Angle: $AB^2 = BC^2 + AC^2 + 2BC \times CD$ (where $AD \perp BC$)

Apollonius Theorem

In $\triangle ABC$, if $M$ is the midpoint of side $BC$, then:

$AB^2 + AC^2 = 2AM^2 + 2BM^2$

3. Definitions and Key Terms

  • Hypotenuse: The side opposite the right angle in a right-angled triangle.
  • Geometric Mean: The square root of the product of two segments of the hypotenuse created by an altitude.
  • Median: A segment joining a vertex to the midpoint of the opposite side.
  • Centroid: The point of concurrence of the medians of a triangle.

4. Formulas, Rules, and Properties

  • Pythagoras Theorem: $c^2 = a^2 + b^2$ (for hypotenuse $c$)
  • Pythagorean Triplet Formula: For $a > b$, $(a^2 + b^2), (a^2 - b^2), (2ab)$ form a triplet.
  • Geometric Mean Theorem: $QS^2 = PS \times SR$
  • Apollonius Theorem: $AB^2 + AC^2 = 2AM^2 + 2BM^2$

5. Worked Examples — Step by Step

Ex. (1) (Page 45)

Given: $\triangle ABC$, $\angle B = 90^\circ$, $\angle A = 30^\circ$, $AC = 14$.

Find: $AB$ and $BC$.

Solution:

  1. Since $\angle B = 90^\circ$ and $\angle A = 30^\circ$, then $\angle C = 60^\circ$.
  2. By 30°-60°-90° theorem:
  • $BC = \frac{1}{2} \times AC = \frac{1}{2} \times 14 = 7$
  • $AB = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \times AC = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \times 14 = 7\sqrt{3}$

Ex. (3) (Page 45)

Given: $\triangle PQR$, $\angle PQR = 90^\circ$, $QN \perp PR$, $PN = 9$, $NR = 16$.

Find: $QN$.

Solution:

  1. By theorem of geometric mean: $QN^2 = PN \times NR$
  2. $QN^2 = 9 \times 16 = 144$
  3. $QN = \sqrt{144} = 12$

6. Exercise / Practice Set Solutions

Practice Set 2.1 (Page 47)

1. Identify Pythagorean triplets:

(i) $(3, 5, 4)$: $3^2+4^2 = 9+16 = 25 = 5^2$. Yes.

(ii) $(4, 9, 12)$: $4^2+9^2 = 16+81 = 97 \neq 144$. No.

(iii) $(5, 12, 13)$: $5^2+12^2 = 25+144 = 169 = 13^2$. Yes.

(iv) $(24, 70, 74)$: $24^2+70^2 = 576+4900 = 5476 = 74^2$. Yes.

(v) $(10, 24, 27)$: $10^2+24^2 = 100+576 = 676 \neq 729$. No.

(vi) $(11, 60, 61)$: $11^2+60^2 = 121+3600 = 3721 = 61^2$. Yes.

2. In $\triangle MNP$, $\angle MNP = 90^\circ$, $NQ \perp MP$, $MQ = 9$, $QP = 4$, find $NQ$.

  • By geometric mean theorem: $NQ^2 = MQ \times QP$
  • $NQ^2 = 9 \times 4 = 36$
  • $NQ = 6$

3. In $\triangle QPR$, $\angle QPR = 90^\circ$, $PM \perp QR$, $PM = 10$, $QM = 8$, find $QR$.

  • By geometric mean theorem: $PM^2 = QM \times MR$
  • $10^2 = 8 \times MR \Rightarrow 100 = 8 \times MR \Rightarrow MR = 12.5$
  • $QR = QM + MR = 8 + 12.5 = 20.5$

Practice Set 2.2 (Page 52)

1. In $\triangle PQR$, $S$ is midpoint of $QR$. $PQ = 11, PR = 17, PS = 13$, find $QR$.

  • By Apollonius theorem: $PQ^2 + PR^2 = 2PS^2 + 2QS^2$
  • $11^2 + 17^2 = 2(13^2) + 2QS^2$
  • $121 + 289 = 2(169) + 2QS^2$
  • $410 = 338 + 2QS^2 \Rightarrow 72 = 2QS^2 \Rightarrow QS^2 = 36 \Rightarrow QS = 6$
  • $QR = 2 \times QS = 2 \times 6 = 12$

7. Important Questions and Answers

  • Q: What is the converse of the 30°-60°-90° theorem?

A: In a right-angled triangle, if one side is half of the hypotenuse, then the angle opposite to that side is 30°.

  • Q: Can a triangle with sides 7, 24, 25 be a right-angled triangle?

A: Yes, because $7^2 + 24^2 = 49 + 576 = 625 = 25^2$.

8. Common Errors and How to Avoid Them

  • Error: Misidentifying the hypotenuse in the Pythagoras theorem.
  • Correction: Always identify the side opposite the 90° angle as the hypotenuse.
  • Error: Forgetting to square the lengths in the Apollonius theorem.
  • Correction: Ensure every length term in the formula $AB^2 + AC^2 = 2AM^2 + 2BM^2$ is squared.

9. Quick Revision Sheet

  • Pythagoras Theorem: $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$
  • Geometric Mean: $h^2 = p \times q$
  • Apollonius Theorem: $AB^2 + AC^2 = 2(AM^2 + BM^2)$
  • 30-60-90: Sides are $x, x\sqrt{3}, 2x$.
  • 45-45-90: Sides are $x, x, x\sqrt{2}$.

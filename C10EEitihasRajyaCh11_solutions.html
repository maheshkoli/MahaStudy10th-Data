Elections and Representation

Elections are an important part of Indian democracy. They allow people to choose representatives and enable changes in government through peaceful means. Different political parties get opportunities to govern, and government policies and society may change.

Representation means choosing people to act on behalf of the population in decision-making. In a representative democracy, elected representatives are expected to be responsible to the people and give priority to public welfare.

The textbook says that the entire election process—from announcing dates to declaring results—is conducted under the direction and control of the Election Commission. It describes free, fair and reliable elections as essential to democracy.

Election Commission of India

Article 324 of the Indian Constitution establishes the Election Commission as an autonomous body central to the election process. The textbook describes its structure as one Chief Election Commissioner and two other Election Commissioners. The President appoints the Election Commissioners.

The textbook explains that safeguards around the Chief Election Commissioner’s removal and separate provisions for the Commission’s expenses help preserve its independence. It also notes that government officers, teachers and other employees assist with conducting elections.

Sukumar Sen was the first Chief Election Commissioner of independent India after the Commission was established in 1950, according to the textbook. The book also notes that National Voter’s Day campaigns help create awareness and encourage voter registration.

Functions of the Election Commission

  1. Preparing the voters’ list: Prepare and update the electoral roll, add eligible new voters and issue voter identity cards. The textbook says citizens aged 18 and above have the right to vote, subject to the applicable voter-list requirements.
  2. Deciding the timetable and programme: Decide when elections will be held and in how many stages.
  3. Scrutinising candidates’ applications: Examine nomination applications and allow eligible candidates to contest.
  4. Recognising political parties: Recognise parties and allot election symbols; the textbook notes that recognition may also be withdrawn.
  5. Resolving election disputes: Address election-related disputes and, in specified circumstances, arrange re-elections in a constituency or declare a candidate disqualified.

Why disclose candidate information? The textbook asks why candidates provide information about themselves and their property. The purpose is to allow voters to make informed decisions and to support transparency in the election process.

Election Process and Constituencies

The diagram on textbook page 78 shows the stages of the election process in this order:

  1. Establishment of constituencies
  2. Finalising the voters’ list
  3. Nomination of candidates and scrutiny of nomination forms
  4. Election campaign
  5. Actual voting
  6. Counting of votes
  7. Declaration of election results
  8. Resolution of disputes regarding elections

Restructuring constituencies

The textbook explains that each Lok Sabha member represents one constituency. It gives 543 as the total number of Lok Sabha members and constituencies in the context of the chapter. The Delimitation Commission is responsible for creating or restructuring constituencies and, according to the textbook, should not yield to pressure while doing so.

Types of elections

  • General elections: Lok Sabha elections held after every five years, as described in the textbook.
  • Midterm elections: Elections held before the end of the term when a government loses its majority and no alternative government can be formed.
  • By-elections: Elections held to fill a vacant seat when an elected representative resigns or dies.

Model Code of Conduct

The Model Code of Conduct sets out rules that the government, political parties, candidates and voters are expected to follow before and during elections. The Election Commission uses it to help control malpractice and maintain free and fair elections. The textbook says even the government must observe the code.

Examples from the textbook discussion

  • Appealing for votes on the basis of caste or religion is identified as a violation.
  • Distributing household items to influence voters is presented as a possible violation.
  • Door-to-door contact to meet voters and request votes is listed as a campaign activity, not automatically as a violation in the textbook’s exercise.
  • A promise to resolve a local water problem if elected is discussed as a campaign promise; its acceptability depends on the applicable election rules and context.

Voters’ responsibility: Voting is described as both a duty and a responsibility. Voters should make informed choices and avoid bribery, intimidation and discriminatory appeals.

Challenges to Free and Fair Elections

The textbook identifies several challenges faced in conducting elections in a large country with many voters:

  • Misuse of money: Excessive election spending can undermine fairness, so the Election Commission must take steps to prevent misuse.
  • Criminalisation of politics: Giving tickets to candidates with criminal backgrounds makes it harder to ensure a fair electoral environment.
  • Violence: Violence during elections threatens safety and free participation.
  • Family monopoly: Giving election tickets to relatives can concentrate influence within political families.

The textbook encourages political parties to avoid candidates with criminal backgrounds, increase women’s candidacy, cooperate with the Election Commission to prevent violence, and support transparent management of election expenses.

From Ballot Boxes to EVMs and VVPAT

The textbook describes the first general elections in independent India as taking place in 1951–52. Early elections used ballot boxes and paper ballots; election symbols helped voters identify their chosen party even when many voters could not read.

According to the chapter, Electronic Voting Machines (EVMs) came into use from the 1990s. The book highlights several benefits, including the NOTA option (“None of the Above”), easier voting for people with disabilities, reduced paper use, and faster declaration of results.

The chapter also explains that an expert committee was established on 8 October 2010 to consider the Voter Verified Paper Audit Trail (VVPAT). VVPAT allows voters to verify that their vote was recorded as intended and is described as a measure to help prevent electoral malpractice.

2. True or false, with reasons

(1) The Election Commission lays down the code of conduct during elections.
Answer: True. The textbook says the Election Commission adopts the Model Code of Conduct and uses its powers to help control electoral malpractice.

(2) Under special circumstances, the Election Commission holds re-elections in a particular constituency for a second time.
Answer: True, according to the textbook. The chapter says the Commission is responsible for resolving election disputes and may arrange a re-election in a constituency where circumstances require it.

(3) The State Government decides when and in how many stages elections will be held in a particular state.
Answer: False. The textbook assigns the responsibility for deciding the election timetable and stages to the Election Commission.

3. Write short notes

(1) Reorganising the constituencies:
Each elected Lok Sabha member represents a constituency. The Delimitation Commission is responsible for creating or restructuring constituencies. The textbook emphasises that the Commission should work without yielding to pressure.

(2) Journey from the ballot box to EVM machine:
Early elections used ballot boxes and paper ballots, with symbols helping voters identify their choice. EVMs began to be used from the 1990s, according to the textbook. The chapter lists NOTA, accessibility benefits, less paper use and quicker results as advantages, and explains that VVPAT helps voters verify their recorded vote.

4. Complete the concept map

The process of elections

  • Role of the Election Commission: Prepare electoral rolls, announce the election programme, scrutinise nominations, recognise parties and symbols, enforce the code of conduct, and resolve election disputes.
  • Role of voters: Register as eligible voters, check their names on the electoral roll, consider candidates’ information, vote freely and responsibly, and avoid bribery or intimidation.
  • Role of political parties and candidates: Select eligible candidates, submit nomination forms, campaign within the rules, respect the Model Code of Conduct, and accept the election process peacefully.

Important Questions and Quick Revision

Important questions

Why is the Election Commission independent?
Independence helps it conduct elections without improper political pressure. The textbook points to safeguards for the Chief Election Commissioner and separate provisions for the Commission’s expenses.

What is delimitation?
It is the process of creating or restructuring electoral constituencies. The textbook assigns this task to the Delimitation Commission.

What is VVPAT?
Voter Verified Paper Audit Trail is a facility associated with EVMs that allows voters to verify whether their vote has been recorded correctly.

Quick revision sheet

  • Article 324: Establishes the Election Commission.
  • First Chief Election Commissioner: Sukumar Sen, according to the textbook.
  • Five core functions: Voters’ lists, election timetable, candidate scrutiny, party recognition and symbols, dispute resolution.
  • Election stages: Constituencies → voters’ list → nominations → campaign → voting → counting → results → disputes.
  • Election types: General, midterm and by-election.
  • Code of Conduct: Rules to support free and fair elections.
  • Main challenges: Money misuse, criminalisation, violence and family monopoly.
  • Voting technology: Ballot boxes → EVMs → VVPAT.

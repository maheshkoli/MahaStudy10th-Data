Chapter at a Glance

This chapter reviews how the Indian Constitution works in practice, focusing on democracy, social justice and equality, and the judiciary. It describes changes in political participation, decentralisation, rights-based reforms, protection for disadvantaged groups, women's representation and the judiciary's role in interpreting the Constitution.

  • Explain how democracy works through elections, voting and decentralisation.
  • Describe the Right to Information and the rights-based approach.
  • Explain constitutional efforts to promote social justice and equality.
  • Understand women's representation and the role of the judiciary.

Democracy and Political Maturity

The textbook explains that the Constitution aims to establish India as a secular, democratic republic and to protect justice and freedom. It came into force on 26 January 1950.

Political maturity means more than having representative institutions. Democratic principles must be practised in everyday political life. Free and fair elections held at regular intervals, active public participation and political competition are described as indicators of a successful democracy.

Right to Vote

The Constitution provided adult suffrage. The textbook explains that the voting age was initially 21 years and was later reduced to 18, allowing more young people to participate in politics. The chapter links this change to a wider voter base and the emergence of political parties supported by young voters.

Decentralisation of Democracy

Decentralisation distributes power and creates opportunities for ordinary people to participate in government. It can help prevent misuse of power. The textbook highlights the 73rd and 74th Constitutional Amendments, which gave constitutional status to local self-governing institutions and increased their powers.

Reservations for women and weaker sections in local institutions, State Election Commissions, and the 11th and 12th Schedules are discussed as parts of democratic decentralisation.

Right to Information and Rights-Based Reform

The Right to Information (RTI), 2005, gives citizens a way to seek information about government functioning. According to the textbook, communication between citizens and government can strengthen trust and citizen participation.

RTI supports transparency and accountability and reduces secrecy in government work. The chapter explains that after 2000, democratic reforms increasingly came to be viewed as citizens’ rights rather than merely benefits provided to them. It gives the Right to Information, education and food security as examples of rights-based reforms.

Rights-based approach

In a rights-based approach, development is treated as a right of citizens, not simply as a benefit granted to them. This changes the relationship between government and people by emphasising entitlements, accountability and citizens’ ability to ask institutions to fulfil their responsibilities.

Social Justice and Equality

Social justice and equality are important objectives of the Constitution. The textbook describes social justice as removing social conditions that cause injustice and insisting on equal dignity for all people.

The principles of justice and equality aim to end discrimination based on caste, religion, language, gender, place of birth, race and property, and to provide equal opportunities for development. Inclusive democracy requires bringing all sections of society into the mainstream and accommodating differences peacefully.

Policy of reservation of seats

The textbook describes reservation as a measure for groups that have long been deprived of educational and employment opportunities. It discusses reserved seats in educational institutions and government services for Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes and Other Backward Classes.

Scheduled Castes and Tribes (Prevention of Atrocities) Act

The chapter presents this Act as an important measure for social justice. It aims to prevent injustice and atrocities against people belonging to Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes and provides for punishment for acts of atrocity.

Provisions for minorities

The Constitution provides protections for minorities. The textbook notes policies intended to improve educational and employment opportunities and describes constitutional protection for equality, freedom, protection against exploitation, and cultural and educational rights.

Women: Laws and Representation

The textbook describes several measures intended to protect women’s freedom and support their development. Examples include equal rights to property, the Dowry Prohibition Act, laws against sexual harassment, and the law protecting women from domestic violence. It also mentions the National Commission for Women and similar commissions at State level.

The 73rd and 74th Constitutional Amendments reserved 33% of seats for women in local self-governing institutions. The chapter states that this proportion had been increased to 50% in several states, including Maharashtra.

Role of the Judiciary

The judiciary strengthens democracy and helps achieve social justice and equality by interpreting the Constitution in light of its objectives and the intentions of its makers.

Basic structure of the Constitution

The textbook describes the Constitution as a living document that may change with changing conditions. Parliament has the power to amend it, but the judiciary has held that Parliament cannot alter its basic structure.

The chapter lists the following as elements of the basic structure:

  • Republican and democratic form of government.
  • Federal structure of the Constitution.
  • Unity and integrity of the nation.
  • Sovereignty of the nation.
  • Secularism and supremacy of the Constitution.

Significant judicial decisions

The textbook says court decisions have helped make fundamental rights more meaningful. It lists subjects including children's rights, human rights, the honour and dignity of women, individual freedom and tribal empowerment.

Good Governance

The textbook describes good governance as a requirement of democracy and lists these features:

  • Accountable and responsible government
  • Effective and efficient government
  • Responsive government
  • Transparency in government functioning
  • Just and inclusive development
  • People’s participation in governance and decision-making

These features help citizens understand how public decisions are made, encourage participation, and make public institutions more responsive to people’s needs.

RTI, described in the chapter as a 2005 reform, gives citizens a right to know about government functioning. It improves transparency, reduces secrecy and helps build trust and accountability between government and citizens.

Important Questions and Quick Revision

Important questions

Why is decentralisation important in a democracy?
It distributes power, helps prevent misuse of authority and creates opportunities for common people to participate in political decision-making. The textbook highlights the 73rd and 74th Amendments as important steps.

How does RTI strengthen democracy?
It allows citizens to seek information about government functioning, encourages transparency and accountability, reduces secrecy and can strengthen trust between citizens and government.

What is the basic structure doctrine as described in the chapter?
Parliament may amend the Constitution, but the judiciary has held that amendments cannot alter its basic structure, including the democratic and republican form of government, federalism, national unity and integrity, sovereignty, secularism and constitutional supremacy.

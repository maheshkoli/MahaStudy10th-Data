1. Chapter Overview

“Joan of Arc” is an extract from George Bernard Shaw's play about Joan, a peasant girl from eastern France who led the French army to several victories during the Hundred Years War. The extract is set in 1429, when large parts of France were under English and Burgundian control and Orleans was held by the English. fileciteturn190file0L34-L47

The scene focuses on Joan's meeting with Captain Robert de Baudricourt. She confidently asks for a horse, armour and soldiers so that she can go to the Dauphin and help free Orleans. Her confidence, courage and faith gradually influence Robert and Poulengey. Robert finally agrees to send her to Chinon under an escort. fileciteturn190file0L74-L113 fileciteturn190file0L271-L313

2. Author — George Bernard Shaw

  • George Bernard Shaw (1856–1950) was an Irish playwright and critic.
  • He wrote more than sixty plays.
  • Important works mentioned in the textbook include Man and Superman, Pygmalion and Saint Joan.
  • He was a leading dramatist of his time.
  • He was awarded the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1925.

7. Questions from the Text — Complete Answers

Q1. Why had Joan wanted to meet the Captain squire?

Joan wanted to meet Captain Robert de Baudricourt because she wanted him to give her a horse, armour and some soldiers and send her to the Dauphin. She believed that she could help free Orleans. fileciteturn190file0L74-L77

Q2. Name the persons whom Joan needed to free Orleans.

Joan said that three men would be enough to accompany her. She named Monsieur de Poulengey (Jack), John Godsave, Dick the Archer and their servants John of Honecourt and Julian in her plan; she also said that Jack and Polly had promised to come with her. fileciteturn190file0L100-L113

Q3. Was the Dauphin fit to be a Prince and heir?

Poulengey's description suggests that the Dauphin was in a difficult position: he was in Chinon, was reluctant to fight, and his men were frightened. Robert, however, recalled that the Dauphin had defeated the English at Montargis. Thus, the dialogue presents doubts about his current fighting spirit rather than simply declaring him unfit to be a prince. fileciteturn190file0L169-L179

Q4. What was the squire's opinion about miracles?

Robert says that miracles are acceptable as an idea but that the difficulty is that they do not happen nowadays. Poulengey disagrees and begins to feel that Joan herself may be a kind of miracle. fileciteturn190file0L180-L186

Q5. What did Robert accuse Poulengey of?

Robert called Poulengey mad for taking Joan's plan seriously and for believing that there was something special about her. fileciteturn190file0L208-L221

Q6. What shows that Joan is a person of immense faith?

When Robert questions her about fighting the English, Joan confidently says, “I don’t think it can be very difficult if God is on your side.” Her confidence in God's support shows her strong faith. fileciteturn190file0L232-L247

Q7. Why were the French soldiers always beaten?

According to Joan, the French soldiers fought mainly to save their own lives. Since the shortest way to save their lives was to run away, they were easily defeated. Joan wanted to teach them to fight for France instead. fileciteturn190file0L271-L281

Q8. What dress did Joan want?

Joan asked whether she could have a soldier's dress. Robert told her to take whatever she wanted and said that he would not take responsibility for it. fileciteturn190file0L305-L308

Q9. What made Robert finally agree to the plan?

Robert was influenced by Joan's confidence, determination and ability to inspire others. Poulengey also strongly supported giving her a chance. Robert finally decided to send Joan to Chinon under Poulengey's escort and three of his friends. fileciteturn190file0L282-L304

8. Character Sketch — Joan of Arc

Confident: She speaks to Robert without fear and assumes that he will provide what she needs.
Courageous: She is not frightened by Robert's authority or by descriptions of the English army.
Determined: She has already arranged people and plans for her journey.
Faithful: She believes that success is possible if God is on her side.
Practical: She calculates the cost of a horse and says she can save money on armour.
Inspirational: Her words put “fire” into Poulengey and may inspire the soldiers and Dauphin.

12. Activity 4 — Say WHY?

(a) Joan wanted to meet the Captain squire.

She wanted him to give her a horse, armour and soldiers and send her to the Dauphin so that she could help free Orleans.

(b) Joan did not ask for many soldiers from the Captain squire.

She believed that she did not need many soldiers. She said that three men would be enough and that the Dauphin would provide what was needed to free Orleans.

(c) Poulengey, Jack and Dick had offered to accompany Joan.

They believed there was something special about Joan and felt that her plan might be worth trying. Poulengey considered it their last chance and wanted to see what she could do.

(d) French soldiers were always beaten in war.

According to Joan, they were fighting only to save their own lives, and the easiest way to save their lives was to run away. Therefore they lacked the fighting spirit needed to defeat the enemy.

(e) Captain squire Robert said “I wash my hands off it.”

Robert was willing to send Joan to Chinon, but he did not want to take responsibility for what happened after she left. The phrase means that he was refusing further responsibility for the plan.

15. Activity 7 — Indirect Speech Summary

Summary of the section from Joan's greeting to “Polly and Jack have promised to come with me.”

Joan greeted Captain Robert de Baudricourt and asked him to give her a horse, armour and some soldiers and to send her to the Dauphin. Robert thought that she was mad and threatened to send her back to her father. Joan confidently said that he would give her what she wanted. She explained that she could save money by using a soldier's armour instead of specially made armour. She said that she would not need many soldiers because the Dauphin would provide what was necessary to free Orleans. She stated that three men would be enough and that Polly and Jack had promised to accompany her. She also named other people whom she expected to join her. Joan told Robert that everything had already been arranged and that he only needed to give the order.

19. Important Exam Questions

Q1. Describe Joan's character.

Joan is confident, courageous, determined, faithful and practical. She speaks fearlessly to Robert, has a clear plan, calculates what equipment she needs, and believes that God will support her. She also inspires others to take action.

Q2. How did Joan influence Robert?

At first Robert considered Joan mad and wanted her sent away. However, her confidence, practical planning and strong faith impressed Poulengey, who urged Robert to give her a chance. Robert finally agreed to send her to Chinon with an escort. fileciteturn190file0L208-L225 fileciteturn190file0L282-L304

Q3. What was the difference between Robert's and Poulengey's opinions?

Robert was doubtful and initially considered Joan's plan foolish. Poulengey was more open-minded. He believed there was something special about Joan and felt that trying her plan was worth the risk because the French side badly needed a change.

Q4. Explain “put fire into someone”.

It means to inspire or motivate someone to fight or act bravely. In the play, Poulengey says Joan's words have put fire into him, and Robert thinks she may also inspire the troops and the Dauphin.

Q5. Why is Joan an inspirational leader?

She has a clear purpose, strong faith and confidence. She does not merely criticise the frightened soldiers; she offers a different attitude—fighting for France instead of simply trying to save their own lives. Her confidence makes other characters consider taking a chance on her plan.

Chapter 24: The Gift of Magi

1. Chapter at a Glance

"The Gift of the Magi" is a short story by O. Henry about a young, impoverished couple, Della and Jim, who want to buy each other meaningful Christmas gifts. The story explores themes of selfless love, sacrifice, and the true meaning of giving. Students will learn about situational irony, character motivation, and the narrative structure of a short story.

2. Important Concepts Explained

  • Situational Irony: The story is a classic example of situational irony, where the outcome is the opposite of what was expected. Both characters sacrifice their most prized possession to buy a gift for the other's prized possession, rendering both gifts temporarily useless.
  • Sacrifice and Love: The central theme is that the value of a gift lies in the love and sacrifice behind it, not its monetary worth.
  • The Magi: The author compares Jim and Della to the Magi (the wise men who brought gifts to the baby Jesus). He concludes that while the Magi were wise, Jim and Della were the wisest because they gave gifts of love.

3. Definitions and Key Terms

  • Bulldozing: Pressurizing or forcing someone.
  • Imputation: Associative action or attribution to someone.
  • Parsimony: Extreme unwillingness to spend money.
  • Instigates: Provokes or brings about.
  • Mendicancy Squad: Police who arrest beggars and the homeless.
  • Coax: To urge or persuade gently.
  • Appertaining: Concerning or relating to.
  • Depreciate: To gradually diminish in value.
  • Cascade: A small waterfall (used here to describe hair).
  • Prudence: The quality of being wise and careful.
  • Coveted: Eagerly sought after or desired.
  • Idiocy: Foolishness.
  • Inconsequential: Unimportant or insignificant.

5. Exercise / Practice Set Solutions

1. Complete the following.

(b) Della wanted to buy a Christmas present for Jim.

2. Answer the following questions:

(a) How much did Della save for Christmas? What does it show?

Della saved one dollar and eighty-seven cents. This shows that she is extremely poor and has been struggling to save every penny for months.

(b) Why did Della take pride in the beauty of her hair?

Della took pride in her hair because it was one of the two prized possessions of the couple (the other being Jim's gold watch) and it was exceptionally long and beautiful.

(c) Why and how did Della sell her hair? Do you think she did well?

Della sold her hair to Madame Sofronie for twenty dollars because she needed money to buy a Christmas gift for Jim. Whether she "did well" is subjective; while she lost her hair, she gained the satisfaction of sacrificing for her husband, which the author considers a wise act of love.

(d) Why did Jim sell his gold watch? Do you think he did well?

Jim sold his gold watch to buy the set of combs for Della. Like Della's sacrifice, this shows his deep love for his wife, prioritizing her happiness over his own prized possession.

(e) Why did Jim and Della want to present the gifts?

They wanted to present gifts to show their love and appreciation for each other during Christmas, despite their poverty.

(f) Why did Della feel sad on losing her hair?

She felt sad because her hair was a part of her identity and beauty, and she feared Jim might not find her as attractive without it.

(g) What did Jim bring out from his coat? Describe the scene.

Jim brought out a package containing the set of combs Della had long admired in a Broadway window. The scene is one of ecstatic joy followed by hysterical tears when Della realizes the combs are now useless for her short hair.

(h) How beautiful was the gold watch chain? Would you have liked to own it?

The chain was made of platinum, simple and chaste in design, proclaiming its value by substance rather than gaudy ornamentation. (Personal opinion: Yes, it sounds like a timeless and elegant piece.)

(i) How did the Magi want to celebrate their Christmas (OR) who were the first people to give Christmas gifts?

The Magi were the wise men who brought gifts to the baby Jesus in the manger; they are considered the inventors of the art of giving Christmas presents.

(j) What is the moral of the story?

The moral is that the greatest gifts are those given with selfless love and sacrifice, and that such love makes people truly wise.

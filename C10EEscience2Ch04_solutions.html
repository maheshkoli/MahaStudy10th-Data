1. Chapter at a Glance

This chapter explores the relationship between ecosystems and the environment. It covers the importance of environmental conservation, the impact of human activities on the environment, types of pollution, and the need for sustainable practices. Students will learn about biodiversity, the role of various organizations in conservation, and the importance of individual responsibility in maintaining environmental balance.

2. Important Concepts Explained

Ecosystem - A Review

An ecosystem is formed by biotic (living) and abiotic (non-living) factors and their interactions.

  • Producers: Plants that produce food.
  • Consumers: Herbivores (e.g., deer, goats) feed on producers. Predators (e.g., lion, tiger) control herbivore populations.
  • Decomposers: Organisms like caterpillars, termites, and insects in dung are crucial for cleaning the environment by decomposing dead organic matter.

Relationship between Environment and Ecosystem

  • Environment: A broad concept including physical, chemical, and biological factors affecting living organisms. It is the condition of the surroundings.
  • Ecology: The study of interactions between biotic and abiotic factors.
  • Ecosystem: The basic functional unit of ecology.
  • Environmental Balance: Maintained through natural cycles (water, carbon, nitrogen, oxygen cycles) and food chains.

Environmental Conservation

Human existence depends on nature. We have a responsibility to conserve the Earth for future generations. Environmental degradation occurs due to natural and artificial factors.

Pollution

  • Definition: Unnecessary and unacceptable change in the surrounding environment due to natural events or human activities.
  • Types: Air, water, soil, sound, thermal, and light pollution.
  • Radioactive Pollution: Caused by natural (UV, IR) and artificial (X-rays, atomic energy plants) radiations. Effects include cancerous ulceration, tissue destruction, genetic changes, and vision impairment.

Biodiversity

The richness of living organisms in nature.

  • Genetic Diversity: Diversity among organisms of the same species (e.g., humans).
  • Species Diversity: Innumerable species of plants, animals, and microbes.
  • Ecosystem Diversity: Different ecosystems in each region.

Hotspots of Biodiversity

There are 34 highly sensitive biodiversity spots globally. India has significant biodiversity, particularly in the eastern region and the Western Ghats.

Endangered Heritage Places

  • Western Ghats: Threatened by mining and natural gas exploration.
  • Manas Sanctuary (Assam): Threatened by dams and water use.
  • Sunderban Sanctuary (West Bengal): Threatened by dams, deforestation, and excessive fishing.

Classification of Threatened Species

  1. Endangered Species: Population or habitat has declined to the point of potential extinction (e.g., Lion-tailed monkey).
  2. Rare Species: Population is considerably declined (e.g., Red panda).
  3. Vulnerable Species: Population is extremely low and declining (e.g., Tiger).
  4. Indeterminate Species: Appear endangered but lack substantial information (e.g., Giant squirrel/Shekhru).

3. Definitions and Key Terms

  • Ecosystem: A system formed by the interaction of biotic and abiotic factors.
  • Environment: The sum of physical, chemical, and biological factors affecting living organisms.
  • Ecology: The study of interactions between biotic and abiotic factors.
  • Pollution: Contamination of the environment by harmful substances.
  • Biodiversity: The variety of life in the world or in a particular habitat or ecosystem.
  • Sacred Grove: A forest patch conserved by society in the name of a deity, providing special protection.

4. Exercise / Practice Set Solutions

Page 55: Exercise

1. Reorganize the following food chain. Describe the ecosystem to which it belongs.

  • Given: Grasshopper – Snake – Paddy field – Eagle – Frog.
  • Reorganized Food Chain: Paddy plants (Producer) → Grasshopper (Primary Consumer) → Frog (Secondary Consumer) → Snake (Tertiary Consumer) → Eagle (Quaternary Consumer / top predator).
  • Ecosystem: This belongs to an agricultural or terrestrial ecosystem (specifically a paddy field ecosystem).

2. Explain the statement- ‘we have got this Earth planet on lease from our future generations and not as an ancestral property from our ancestors.’

  • Explanation: This statement emphasizes sustainability. If we treat the Earth as ancestral property, we might feel entitled to exploit its resources without limit. By viewing it as a "lease" from future generations, we acknowledge that we are merely caretakers. We must return the planet in a healthy, functional state to those who will inherit it, rather than depleting its resources for short-term gain.

3. Write short notes.

  • a. Environmental Conservation: The practice of protecting the natural environment by individuals, organizations, and governments to conserve natural resources and restore the natural environment. It is essential for the survival of all living beings.
  • b. Chipko Movement of Bishnoi: A movement where people hugged trees to prevent them from being cut down. It is a historic example of community-led environmental protection.
  • c. Biodiversity: The variety of life forms, including genetic, species, and ecosystem diversity. It is vital for ecosystem stability.
  • d. Sacred Groves: Forest patches protected by local communities in the name of a deity. They serve as "sanctuaries" and preserve local flora and fauna.
  • e. Disaster and its management: Disasters are sudden events causing huge loss of life and property. Disaster management involves planning, preparation, and execution of strategies to minimize the impact of these events.

4. How will you justify that overcoming the pollution is a powerful way of environmental management?

  • Justification: Pollution directly harms the biotic and abiotic components of an ecosystem. By reducing pollution, we restore the natural balance, improve the health of living organisms, and ensure the sustainability of natural resources. It is a proactive approach to prevent environmental degradation rather than just managing the aftermath.

5. Which projects will you run in relation to environmental conservation? How?

  • Projects:
  • Tree Plantation Drive: Organize planting native species in local areas to increase green cover.
  • Waste Segregation Awareness: Educate the community on separating wet and dry waste to facilitate better composting and recycling.
  • Water Conservation: Promote rainwater harvesting and reducing water wastage in schools and homes.

Chapter at a Glance

Democracy: A Continuous Process

The textbook describes democracy as a continuous living process. Merely adopting a democratic system does not make democracy work in everyday life. Alertness, awareness and conscious efforts are needed to sustain it. Threats to democracy should be identified in time and addressed through democratic means.

The chapter distinguishes between the political form of democracy—such as voting, elections, government structures and the judiciary—and democracy as a way of life. For democracy to become a way of life, democratic values must be practised throughout society.

The textbook names inclusion of all social components, autonomy for social institutions, empowerment of citizens and protection of human values as measures needed for the real cultivation of democracy.

Challenges to Democracy at the Global Level

The chapter notes that many countries claim to be democratic, but relatively few fully protect people’s rights and freedoms and prioritise public welfare. It identifies the spread of democratic values and the adoption of genuine democracy by all nations as a major global challenge.

It also discusses military takeovers as a threat to democracy and the need to make democratic values deeply rooted in society. Freedom, equality, fraternity, justice, peace, development and humanitarianism should be practised at all levels, and support for these values should be gathered through democratic means.

Textbook discussion question: China adopted economic reforms and joined the World Trade Organisation, yet the chapter notes that it continues to have a one-party system. The textbook poses this as a question for students to consider; it does not provide a full answer in the supplied pages.

Challenges Faced by Indian Democracy

1. Communalism and terrorism

The textbook identifies religious conflict and the resulting terrorism as major challenges. Increasing religious hostility can harm social stability, while terrorism can greatly reduce people’s participation in the democratic process.

2. Left extremism — Naxalism

The chapter says Naxalism began as a movement against injustice faced by landless farmers and tribal people. It describes the movement as having become a violent struggle, with some groups using violence against government policies and attacking police forces. The textbook says the original importance of farmers’ and tribal problems has diminished in this violent phase.

3. Corruption

The textbook describes corruption in public-sector, political and administrative work as reducing government effectiveness. Delays, poor public services and financial irregularities can create distrust and dissatisfaction. Electoral malpractice—such as bogus voting, bribing voters and abducting voters—can weaken confidence in the democratic process.

4. Family monopoly in politics

The chapter identifies political control concentrated in one family as a problem because it reduces democratic space and makes it harder for ordinary people to participate in public life.

5. Criminalisation of politics

The textbook explains that giving candidacy to people with criminal backgrounds or allegations of crime and corruption can increase the influence of money and muscle power. It may also contribute to violence during elections.

6. Social challenges

The chapter identifies unemployment, unequal distribution of resources, a growing gap between rich and poor, and caste-based issues as social problems that Indian democracy needs to address.

Steps to Make Indian Democracy Successful

1. Include minorities in decision-making

Democracy gives importance to majority opinion, but majority rule should not mean rule by the majority community alone. The textbook says minority groups must also be included in decision-making. Religious, linguistic, ethnic and caste groups should have equal opportunities to participate, and the government should consider minority interests.

2. Make political processes more transparent

The chapter describes the judiciary as making efforts to increase transparency in politics. It mentions measures such as strict punishment for criminals and preventing criminals from participating in the political process as ways to address criminalisation of politics.

3. Make efforts at every level

Democracy cannot be strengthened only by government, administration and courts. Individuals and society must also make conscious efforts. The textbook lists initiatives such as Education for All, Clean Bharat, Gram Samruddhi Yojana, self-help groups and the Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Scheme. It also notes that 50% of seats are reserved for women in local self-governing institutions, as described in the chapter.

4. Increase people’s participation

Greater participation at all levels, especially in government, can help shape public policies. The chapter says public policies develop through interaction with people and that interaction with people who are not in power is also important.

5. Practise democratic values in daily life

The textbook emphasises equality, liberty, social justice and secularism. Citizens share responsibility for the success of democracy and should respect these values in personal and public life.

Answer: True. The chapter explains that democracy is a continuous process and needs conscious efforts. Citizens must recognise threats in time and address them democratically.

(2) Importance of the problems of farmers and tribals has increased in the left extremist movement.
Answer: False, according to the textbook. The chapter says that the importance of farmers’ and tribal problems has reduced in the violent phase of the Naxalite movement.

(3) People may lose confidence in the democratic process due to corruption during elections.
Answer: True. The textbook mentions bogus voting, bribery and abduction of voters as practices that reduce people’s confidence in the electoral and democratic process.

4. Answer the following questions briefly

(1) What factors are required for the success of democracy in India?
Answer: The textbook highlights inclusion of minority communities in decision-making, transparent political processes, action against criminalisation, conscious effort by government and citizens, increased public participation, and respect for equality, liberty, social justice and secularism. It also emphasises empowerment of citizens and protection of human values.

(2) What are the effects of criminalisation of politics?
Answer: When candidates with criminal backgrounds or allegations of crime and corruption are given tickets, money and muscle power can become more influential in politics. This may encourage violence during elections and weaken public confidence in the democratic process.

Answer: The textbook points to the judiciary’s efforts to make political processes transparent. It mentions strict punishment for criminals and preventing criminals from participating in the political process. It also stresses citizens’ participation, interaction with people outside power, and conscious efforts by government, administration, judiciary and society.

Important Questions and Quick Revision

Important questions

Why is democracy described as a continuous process?
Because adopting elections and institutions alone does not make democracy a way of life. Citizens and institutions must continually protect rights, include social groups and practise democratic values.

How does corruption weaken democracy?
It reduces government effectiveness, causes delays and poor public services, creates distrust, and can undermine elections through bogus voting, bribery and voter intimidation or abduction.

Why should minorities be included in democratic decision-making?
Majority rule should not become rule by the majority community alone. Religious, linguistic, ethnic and caste groups should have equal opportunities to participate, and their interests should be considered.

1. Chapter Overview

“A Lesson in Life from A Beggar” is a story by Sudha Murthy. It describes Meena, an LIC officer who has a good salary and comfortable circumstances but remains unhappy, cynical and pessimistic. Her attitude changes after she observes an old beggar and his young granddaughter enjoying the rain despite hunger and poverty.

The story's central lesson is that happiness is not determined simply by comfort or possessions. Meena learns gratitude, contentment and the ability to find happiness in small things, situations and people. The uploaded solution file identifies the beggar and his granddaughter as the catalyst for her transformation. fileciteturn182file1L36-L43

Central message: We should appreciate what we have, avoid constant negativity, and learn to find happiness in the life we actually have.

3. Story — Detailed Explanation

Meena's Initial Nature

Meena is the narrator's friend and an LIC officer earning a good salary. Despite her comfortable position, she is forever unhappy. Her gloom and cynicism affect people around her. She rarely says anything positive. When the narrator praises Rakesh's academic achievement, Meena belittles it. When the narrator praises Shwetha's beauty, Meena predicts that she will become ugly. Even a pleasant day and a walk become reasons for complaint.

Meena lives alone because her parents live in Delhi, and she is an only child. Her habit of complaining about everything makes her unpleasant company. The textbook then says that she is transferred to Bombay and the narrator loses contact with her for some years. fileciteturn182file0L33-L61

The Meeting in Mumbai

Years later, the narrator is caught in heavy rain at Flora Fountain, Mumbai. She is standing near Akbarallys, waiting for the rain to subside. She notices Meena and initially wants to avoid her, expecting the old stream of complaints.

Instead, Meena warmly holds her hand, smiles and speaks enthusiastically. She is excited to meet an old friend and even says that old friends and memories are precious and rare. When the narrator cannot stay for the night, Meena suggests having tea. She accepts the rain, suggests buying an umbrella, and says they can walk when the narrator worries that they will not get a taxi. The narrator is amazed at this new, flexible Meena. fileciteturn182file0L98-L134

The Beggar — The Turning Point

At the Grand Hotel, the narrator asks what caused such a dramatic change. Meena explains that a beggar changed her life.

The old beggar used to stay outside her house with his five-year-old granddaughter. Meena gave him leftovers every day but never spoke to him. One monsoon day she complained about the rain and could not give them their usual food.

When she looked from her bedroom window, she saw the beggar and his granddaughter playing on the road because there was no traffic. They were laughing, clapping and screaming joyfully, as if they were in paradise. They were hungry and drenched, but the hunger and rain did not destroy their happiness. fileciteturn182file0L157-L190

Meena's Realisation

The scene forced Meena to examine her own life. She realised that she had many comforts which the beggar and his granddaughter did not have, yet she was unhappy while they were joyful.

She made a list of what she had and did not have and discovered that she had far more to be grateful for than she had previously recognised. She decided to change her attitude and use the beggar as her role model.

The change did not happen instantly. Meena says it took her almost two years to put the change into effect. After that, she began finding happiness in every small thing, every situation and every person. fileciteturn182file0L194-L217

Gurudakshina

The narrator asks whether Meena gave gurudakshina to her guru. Meena explains that the beggar had died by the time she fully understood the lesson. However, she sponsored his granddaughter to a boarding school as a mark of respect to him. fileciteturn182file0L213-L225

8. Summary Using the Given Points

Meena was initially a pessimistic and unhappy LIC officer who complained about almost everything. Years later, the narrator met her in Mumbai and was surprised to find her cheerful and positive. Meena explained that an old beggar and his granddaughter had changed her life. One rainy day, she saw them playing joyfully on the road despite being hungry and drenched. Their happiness made her examine her own comfortable life and realise how much she had to be grateful for. She decided to change her attitude and use the beggar as her role model. It took her almost two years to put the change into effect. She eventually learned to find happiness in small things, situations and people.

9. Important Exam Questions and Model Answers

Q1. Describe Meena's character before and after her transformation.

Initially, Meena is pessimistic, cynical and forever unhappy. She complains about people, achievements, weather and ordinary activities. Her negative attitude makes her unpleasant company. Later, after observing the beggar and his granddaughter, she becomes cheerful, flexible and grateful. She begins to find happiness in small things, situations and people. Her transformation is therefore a change in both her thinking and her daily attitude.

Q2. Why did the beggar become Meena's guru?

The beggar became Meena's guru because he taught her an important life lesson without giving her a formal lecture. Although he and his granddaughter were hungry and drenched in rain, they enjoyed themselves joyfully. Meena, who had many comforts but was constantly unhappy, learned from their attitude. She recognised that happiness depends greatly on how we look at life.

Q3. Explain the significance of the rain scene.

The rain scene is the turning point of the story. Meena was complaining about the rain while the beggar and his granddaughter were enjoying it. Their laughter, clapping and play showed that they could find happiness despite hardship. This contrast made Meena compare their lives with her own and realise how much she had to be grateful for.

Q4. How did Meena express gratitude after the beggar's death?

She could not thank the beggar directly because he had died before she fully understood his lesson. As a mark of respect to him, she sponsored his granddaughter to a boarding school. This became her practical expression of gratitude.

Q5. What is the main message of the story?

The story teaches that happiness is not simply a result of wealth, comfort or favourable circumstances. A positive and grateful attitude can help a person find joy even in difficult situations. We should appreciate what we have instead of constantly focusing on what we lack.
